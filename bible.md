# ROOT ACCESS — SERIES BIBLE

**Working title:** *ROOT ACCESS*
**Format:** 1-hour cyberpunk drama / techno-thriller · Season 1 = 12 episodes
**Logline:** In a drowned coastal megacity, four rebel hackers — the NU11 — wake up to a one-world currency and a live street-surveillance grid, and fight to tear open the city's single network (the "Root") before the corporation that owns it resets them out of existence. But the deeper they cut into the system, the more they realize the war they think they're fighting has already been fought — and that the revolution itself may be the corporation's oldest product.

**Tone & touchstones:** *Mr. Robot* (unreliable narrator, mental-health thriller, every episode ends on a hook, "who is the real villain"), *Neuromancer* / *Snow Crash* (the outsider console cowboy, the AI underneath the city, one-world currency), *Blade Runner* (flooded neon sprawl, surveillance as weather), *Fight Club* (the revolution that turns on its maker).

---

## 0. DNA — WHAT WE'RE STEALING FROM MR. ROBOT (AND CLASSIC CYBERPUNK)

These are the load-bearing mechanics the show inherits. Every one maps onto the Root Access universe.

| Mr. Robot device | Root Access adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Elliot Alderson — unreliable narrator with DID** | **Zero-Day**, whose "hologram head" is the visual lie the whole series is built on. The audience cannot trust what he is. |
| **Mr. Robot — the protector/leader who turns out to be a projection** | **The ghost in the Root** — the "voice" Neural Burn hears, the "previous hacker" Zero-Day believes he's following. |
| **E Corp → "Evil Corp" (the hero's mental translation of the megacorp's name)** | The NU11 never say "Terable Corp." They hear — and say — **"Terrible Corp."** The rebels' language is itself an act of refusal. |
| **fsociety — the hacker revolution** | **The NU11 (Null Eleven)** — a glitch-cult founded on "eleven seconds of truth." |
| **The father-figure twist** | The previous holder of the **Key-Staff of the Root** — a dead man who keeps turning out to be Zero-Day's origin. |
| **Whiterose / the hidden architect behind the architect** | **Director Kairos** and the **clock** — the layer beneath the layer. |
| **Every episode ends on a hook** | Non-negotiable. Each of our 12 episodes names its cliffhanger in the outline below. |
| **One season-long mystery that pays off in the finale** | **"The Clock" — why does Kairos already know every move the NU11 will make?** (See §7.) |
| **Anti-capitalism as wound, not slogan** | The credit conversion isn't a MacGuffin; it's a *theft of the future* that the characters feel in their bodies. |

**Cyberpunk tropes we keep sharp:**
- **The surveillance state** — street surveillance goes live the moment cash dies. The Network's thousand Overwatch-drone eyes (and, as the NU11 like to point out, *its weakest password*).
- **Cash-eliminated one-world currency** — the **Credit Conversion**: every physical coin and note becomes worthless overnight; everything runs on credits tracked through the Root. No cash = no anonymity = no exit.
- **Megacorporation overreach** — Terable Corp didn't conquer the city; it *bought* it. It has already classified every civilian casualty as "acceptable loss."
- **The outsider hacker** — a kid in a drowned basement who reached into the network and opened every door at once for eleven seconds.

## 1. THE WORLD

### 1.1 The City That Drowned
The city had a name once. Terable Corp buried it under a thousand rebrandings — "Municipal District 9," "the New Downtown," "Forward Node" — until nobody remembered it. What they remember is the water.

The sea rose; the corporation didn't care. The lower city became a flooded maze of rust, salt, and neon. The rich moved **up** — into the arcology spires, the sky-bridges, the glass. The poor stayed **down**, in drowned streets where the grid flickers and the water glows acid-green from a million leaking conduits.

**The vertical divide is the class divide.** The higher you live, the more of the sky you see and the less water touches you. Up top: white armor, chrome, holo-light. Down below: rust, scavenged steel, glitch-camo, acid-green graffiti.

### 1.2 The Root
Everything that still works — power, water, money, guns, cameras, doors — runs through a single network: a backbone buried beneath the skyline. Terable Corp calls it their property. The people who live in its shadow call it **the Root**.

The Root is not a computer you can unplug. It is *in the walls, the water, the light*. To fight Terable Corp is not to fight an army; it is to fight the city's own nervous system.

### 1.3 The Credit Conversion (the inciting event)
One ordinary night — ordinary for the four people in a basement playing games on scavenged PCs — every screen in the city flips to a **Terable Corp emergency broadcast**:

> The one-world currency is complete. As of this broadcast, all physical cash is void. Every citizen is issued a **credit ledger** linked to biometric identity and a live location trail. Simultaneously, the **street-surveillance grid** activates — every Overwatch drone, every corner lens, every door sensor — "for public safety and transactional integrity."

In one broadcast the city loses three things at once: **cash, anonymity, and the ability to go offline.** The world outside the basement becomes a cyberpunk dystopia overnight.

**What the conversion actually is** (revealed slowly across the season, paid off in the finale): not a new policy but a **reset** — the corporation's recurring mechanism for wiping the city's memory and re-running its rebellion. The conversion isn't the first. It's the loop closing. (§7.)

### 1.4 Key locations
- **The Basement** — the NU11's hacker-den: stolen holo-screens, cobbled backpack decks, the smell of dead corporate hardware. The show's "home base" and its warmest space.
- **The Sprawl (lower city)** — corrugated shanties, black-market stalls, burned cars, flooded under-levels, server-shrines where people literally worship the machines that rule them.
- **The Arcology (upper city)** — Terable Corp's white glass towers, sky-bridges, corporate billboards, clean chrome.
- **The Seawall** — the breach where the sea came in; a monument to the corporation's negligence and the NU11's favorite ambush ground.
- **The Root Terminal / Core** — the objective the whole war is fought over. Whoever holds the Root holds the city.

## 2. THE TWO FACTIONS

### 2.1 NU11 — The Null Eleven
*The glitch-cult hackers who broke the Root.*

The NU11 are not an army. They are a cult of survivors who believe the Root belongs to everyone, and who are willing to die proving it. Their name comes from the founding myth: **Zero-Day's "eleven seconds of truth"** — the night he reached into the Root and opened every camera, every ledger, every locked file at once, pinning the corporation's crimes to a citywide broadcast for eleven seconds before it was slammed shut. Eleven seconds. That's all it took to prove the city *could* be freed.

They fight with scavenged corporate hardware turned against its makers. Their armor is rust, their camo is a glitch, their signal color is acid green. They don't hold ground; they own the blind spots. **They don't fight to win. They fight to make it expensive to keep the city in chains.**

**Signature look:** matte black and hazard-yellow, neon-green accents, cobbled hardware, holograms, glitch-camo.

### 2.2 Terable Corp — Municipal Security
*The corporate machine that owns the skyline.*

Terable Corp doesn't call it war. War is bad for the quarterly report. They call it **Municipal Security**, and they've written an entire vocabulary of euphemism for killing: "deterrence," "the line," "explaining corporate policy," "acceptable loss."

They didn't conquer the city — they **bought** it. Now they police it with pulse rifles, arc projectors, spotter drones, and a legal department that has already classified every civilian casualty as acceptable.

**Signature look:** pristine white plating, electric-blue "T" emblem, holo-light tactical displays, chrome and glass.

## 3. THE 8 CORE CHARACTERS

These eight drive the entire series. Nobody else recurs.

### NU11 — the rebels

#### ZERO-DAY — the master hacker (PROTAGONIST / unreliable center)
- **Tabletop description (canon):** "The master hacker of the NU11. Hologram head, a coat of living code, the Key-Staff of the Root in his fist and three wraith-blade drones orbiting him like loyal knives." (Unique hero, 110 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** Our Elliot. The face of the revolution and the series' unreliable narrator. He *seems* human but his "hologram head" is a glitch in the frame from episode one — a visual lie the audience is trained to overlook. He is brilliant, gentle, and terrified of the thing he is becoming. Carries the **Key-Staff of the Root**, the one key that opens the whole network.
- **Arc:** from "I will free the city" to "I may be the city's oldest prisoner."

#### NEURAL BURN — the net-burn victim who hears the city (THE SEER / the mystery's antenna)
- **Canon:** "The psyker-analog of the flooded city: a wired-up burn victim of the net, skull studded with jacks, trailing spinal cables, surrounded by a halo of stolen holo-screens." (Hero, 40 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** His brain was fried by the net, and now it picks up the city's **ghost-signals** — whispers nobody else can hear. He is the show's oracle and its most unreliable witness: some of what he hears is trauma, some is the *previous loop* leaking through. He is the key to the central mystery.
- **Arc:** from "the voices are my sickness" to "the voices are the truth."

#### SWARM TECH — the drone-controller (THE TACTICIAN / the heart)
- **Canon:** "Drone-controller infantry: antenna crown, bracer tablet, one gloved hand permanently raised to conduct the swarm like an orchestra of angry hornets." (Hero, 30 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** The crew's grounded, optimistic engineer — the one who still thinks they can *win*. Conducts swarms of quad-rotors like an orchestra. Provides levity and tactical brilliance, and is the moral anchor who most believes the NU11's own mythology (which makes him the one the finale hits hardest).
- **Arc:** from "we're the good guys" to "what if there are no good guys."

#### GHOST WALKER — the stealth assassin (THE OPERATOR / the conscience)
- **Canon:** "Stealth assassins in glitch-camo ponchos. A blocky rail-pistol in one fist, a glowing neural spike in the other, grapple line on the hip. They arrive, someone important stops existing." (Infantry, 18 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** The quiet, lethal professional with a working motto — *they arrive, and someone important stops existing.* Ghost Walker carries the moral cost of the revolution in silence: every kill is a number, and the numbers are adding up. The character who asks the question the others won't: **at what point do we become the thing we're fighting?**
- **Arc:** from "I only remove the guilty" to "the guilt is the point."

### Terable Corp — the machine

#### DIRECTOR KAIROS — Director of Municipal Security (ANTAGONIST / the architect)
- **Canon:** "Director of Municipal Security. Clock-faced helm, a spine of live data-screens, the Temporal Staff in one hand and a humming chrono-blade in the other. He has already read the incident report of this battle. He wrote it." (Unique hero, 115 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** Our Whiterose / Phillip Price. The calm, terrifying face of the corporation. His **clock-faced helm** is not decorative: it is the visual signature of the central mystery. He speaks as if the future is already written — because, in this city, it has been. He is not predicting the NU11's moves; he is **replaying** them.
- **Arc:** from "I am inevitable" to the reveal of *what* he is actually protecting (and from whom).

#### ARC SENTINEL — the heavy-weapons trooper (THE ENFORCER / the human cost of the machine)
- **Canon:** "Heavy weapons troopers braced on stabilizer legs, hauling a crackling arc projector fed by twin HIGH VOLTAGE tanks. The polite corporate term for what it does is 'deterrence.'" (Hero, 25 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** The corporation's muscle and, crucially, its *human face from the inside*. Arc Sentinel is a true believer who signed up because Terable Corp fed their family after the flood. Loyal, disciplined, and increasingly haunted by what "deterrence" does to the lower city. The lens through which we see the corporation is not a monolith but made of people with reasons.
- **Arc:** from "I follow orders" to "whose orders, exactly."

#### NEXUS COMMANDER — the squad leader (THE RISING THREAT / the true believer)
- **Canon:** "Squad leaders and field officers, wrapped in tactical holo-light with a command baton raised and two spotter drones whispering firing solutions." (Hero, 55 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** The mid-level officer who is the NU11's most persistent hunter. Ambitious, precise, and genuinely good at his job. He believes in the system with a convert's intensity and sees the NU11 as chaos to be extinguished. The escalating personal antagonist who grows more fanatical as the season reveals cracks in the system he serves.
- **Arc:** from "order will prevail" to "I will enforce order even if I have to burn the order to do it."

#### NEXUS TROOPER — the line (THE EVERYMAN / the mirror)
- **Canon:** "The line. White-plated professionals with pulse rifles, sensor pods and a corporate non-disclosure agreement covering their own deaths." (Infantry, 13 pts.)
- **Dramatic role:** One specific, named trooper (a face, a name, a family) through whom the show humanizes "the line." The NDA that covers even their own deaths is the detail that tells you everything about the corporation. The trooper is the mirror image of the NU11 — a poor kid from the lower city who took the only job that paid in credits. When the revolution's cost is finally paid, it is the trooper who makes the audience feel it.
- **Arc:** from "I'm just doing a job" to "the job is me."

## 4. SUPPORTING CAST (one-off — NEVER reused)

Per the show's rules, these characters appear **once** to advance plot and are never seen again. Each exists to hand a single revelation to the core eight.

- **The Street Cop** (Ep 2) — a beat cop who nearly IDs Ghost Walker during the surveillance activation; in the interrogation he reveals that the new grid doesn't just *record* faces, it *predicts* them.
- **The Ledger Lawyer** (Ep 5) — a corporate contract lawyer who, dying of a conversion-related debt spiral, leaks the pre-registration files — and proves that the NU11's names were in the system **before** the broadcast.
- **The Archivist** (Ep 9) — a Terable Corp records keeper who shows Zero-Day the sealed footage: a **previous** Zero-Day, with the same hologram head.

## 5. CENTRAL THEMES (the Mr. Robot spine)

### Control vs. Freedom
The Root's promise is order; its price is the self. The credit conversion makes every transaction a leash — you cannot eat, move, or hide without the system knowing. The NU11's fight is not for money or power but for **the right to be unobserved**. Freedom, in this show, is the ability to *disappear* — and the tragedy is that nobody in the city can anymore.

### The Cost of Revolution
Mr. Robot's deepest question: **what does it cost to tear the system down, and who pays it?** In Root Access, every NU11 victory makes life harder for the lower city it claims to defend — retaliation, harsher credit-caps, more drones. Ghost Walker counts the bodies. Swarm Tech believes the math will come out right. The show refuses to let it. Revolution is not free; someone always picks up the bill, and it is usually the poor.

### Who Is the Real Villain?
The surface answer is Terable Corp. The show complicates it every episode: the corporation is made of people (Arc Sentinel, the Nexus Trooper) with reasons and families; the NU11 are made of people who kill; and beneath both is a **system** that neither side fully controls. By the finale, "the real villain" is not a person. It is the loop itself — and the possibility that the rebellion is the corporation's oldest product.

### The Unreliable Self
Elliot couldn't trust his own mind. Zero-Day can't trust his own *existence*. The show's central horror is not "the system is watching you" but **"you may not be who you think you are."** The hologram head, the blackouts, the voice in the static — all point to one question the protagonist is terrified to answer.

## 6. STORY DNA — HOW THE SHOW BEHAVES

- **Every episode ends on a cliffhanger.** Named explicitly in the outline.
- **Two simultaneous engines:** (1) the physical war for the Root (fights over terminals, data caches, drone hubs, flood gates); (2) the psychological war inside Zero-Day and Neural Burn's heads.
- **Dialogue is strictly character-labeled** (e.g., `ZERO-DAY: ...`) for clean production and later video generation.
- **Scene changes are marked** (e.g., `[SCENE — the Basement → the Sprawl street]`), including room-to-room and location-to-location moves, so every transition can be turned into a generated shot.
- **One-off characters never return.** The 8 core figures carry all recurring weight.
- **Tabletop-mini aesthetic is diegetic:** the characters *look like* painted miniatures — bold silhouettes, strong color reads (acid-green vs. white/electric-blue), readable at a glance. This is a feature of the world's design, not a limitation.

## 7. THE CENTRAL MYSTERY — "THE CLOCK"

**One season-long mystery drives everything, Mr. Robot-style:**

> **Why does Director Kairos already know every move the NU11 will make — down to the names of people who weren't on any list yet?**

The obvious answer is "he's a genius with predictive AI." The show feeds that assumption, then systematically breaks it:

- (Ep 2) The surveillance grid predicts Ghost Walker's route before the NU11 even chose it.
- (Ep 4) The NU11's own names are found in the conversion ledger — **pre-registered as insurgents, dated before the emergency broadcast.**
- (Ep 6) Zero-Day blackouts; when he wakes, a server node is wiped and his own *trace* is in the code.
- (Ep 7) Neural Burn's ghost-signals resolve into **recordings** — old broadcasts from a prior version of the city.
- (Ep 8) Kairos meets Zero-Day and says: *"We've had this conversation before."*
- (Ep 9) The Archivist shows footage of a **previous Zero-Day** — same hologram head, different year.

**The payoff (finale):** The "eleven seconds of truth" that founded NU11 was not the first break-in — it was a **recording** of the last one. The city has been reset before. The credit conversion is the loop's **reboot**, not its origin. Kairos doesn't predict the future because he has already **replayed** it — his clock doesn't run forward, it ticks in a circle. And the Zero-Day the NU11 follow is not the original hacker: he is the **afterimage** of the previous loop's hacker, burned into the Root's code and re-rendered each cycle. The revolution is the corporation's harvest mechanism — a way to farm dissent, identify rebels, and reset the city. The NU11's entire war has been run before, and they are its latest iteration.

**The season-long question the audience is chasing — and the answer that lands in the finale — is:** *if the rebellion is scripted, can it still be real? And if the hero is a recording, can he still choose to be different this time?*

(The answer the finale gives: yes — but choosing differently is the thing that costs everything.)

## 8. SEASON 1 ARC — 12 EPISODES

**Act I (Eps 1–4): The Cage Closes.** The conversion. The surveillance grid. The first proof that something is *wrong* with the system's omniscience.

**Act II (Eps 5–8): The Wound Deepens.** The crew chases the pre-registration lead to a dead man; Zero-Day's blackouts; the ghost-signals resolve into recordings; the midseason fracture; Kairos's invitation.

**Act III (Eps 9–12): The Loop.** The reveal of the previous Zero-Day; the second conversion begins; the empty core; the loop is broken — at a price.

---

### EPISODE 1 — "hello_world"
*Title style note: episodes are named in leetspeak/file-extension style, honoring Mr. Robot's naming convention.*

**Logline:** Cold open — four friends (Zero-Day, Neural Burn, Swarm Tech, Ghost Walker) are hanging out in their flooded basement, trash-talking over scavenged PCs, when every screen in the city flips to the Terable Corp emergency broadcast: cash is dead, credits are live, street surveillance is online. The four watch the world become a prison in real time. As the broadcast loops, Zero-Day freezes — not from shock, but recognition. Buried in the broadcast's data stream is a **glitch signature** only one hacker in the city could have left: *his own.*

**Cliffhanger:** Zero-Day plays the broadcast back and finds his own digital fingerprint embedded in a signal he never sent — *someone is using his code, tonight, before he's written it.*

---

### EPISODE 2 — "the_network_remembers"
**Logline:** The surveillance grid is live, and the NU11 go to ground. Ghost Walker runs a recon through the Sprawl to test the new grid's blind spots — and a one-off **street cop** nearly rolls them up. In the interrogation that follows, the cop lets slip a terrifying truth: the grid isn't just *recording* faces, it's **predicting** them — it flagged Ghost Walker at a corner the assassin hadn't yet decided to turn down. Back at the basement, Zero-Day confirms the impossible: the system knew their route *in advance.*

**Cliffhanger:** A prediction card ticks up on Kairos's wall of screens, and it bears the NU11's next move **before the NU11 have planned it** — Kairos smiles and says, *"Same as last time."*

---

### EPISODE 3 — "ghost_in_the_static"
**Logline:** Swarm Tech and Zero-Day hit a Drone Control Hub to blind a sector of the grid; Arc Sentinel and an Overwatch swarm answer. It's the first real firefight, and Kairos wins it with eerie ease — he "already read the incident report," because *he wrote it.* Meanwhile, Neural Burn's burned brain starts picking up a new kind of signal: not noise, not a glitch, but a **voice in the static** that knows things it shouldn't.

**Cliffhanger:** The voice calls Zero-Day by a name he has **never told anyone** — a name from before the flood, from before NU11, from a life Zero-Day doesn't remember living.

---

### EPISODE 4 — "pre-registered"
**Logline:** The crew breaks into a Root Terminal and pulls a fragment of the conversion ledger. What they find stops the room: **their own four names are already in the system — pre-registered as insurgents, dated *before* the emergency broadcast.** Someone knew the NU11 would exist before the conversion was even announced. The crew starts to suspect a mole in their own ranks, and the fragile trust between the four begins to crack.

**Cliffhanger:** Zero-Day decrypts the pre-registration's author signature — and it isn't a corporation employee. It's **a fifth name: the person who held the Key-Staff before Zero-Day did.** A dead man.

---

### EPISODE 5 — "the_previous_holder"
**Logline:** The crew hunts the identity of the dead man who pre-registered them. A dying **ledger lawyer** (one-off) leaks the sealed files: the previous holder of the Key-Staff, wiped from every record but one. His name is a ghost the corporation went to enormous lengths to erase — which tells the NU11 they're on the right track, and tells Kairos they're getting too close. Nexus Commander is ordered to find them, and the noose tightens on the Sprawl.

**Cliffhanger:** The lawyer's last file is a photo of the previous holder — and **the man in the photo has a hologram head**, exactly like Zero-Day's.

---

### EPISODE 6 — "blackout"
**Logline:** Reeling, Zero-Day reaches deeper into the Key-Staff than ever before and **blacks out**. When he wakes, hours are missing, a server node has been wiped clean, and none of his crew remember him leaving the basement. Mr. Robot-style, the episode plays entirely from Zero-Day's broken point of view as he tries to reconstruct what he did while he was "gone" — and whether the crew is lying to him, or he's lying to himself.

**Cliffhanger:** Zero-Day finds the wiped node's residual data — and in the wipe's wake, **his own signature is burned into the code**, like a handprint he left in a room he was never in.

---

### EPISODE 7 — "the_old_broadcast"
**Logline:** Neural Burn finally isolates the ghost-signals: they aren't spirits and they aren't madness — they're **recordings**. Old transmissions, old broadcasts, an old city — bleeding through the Root's deeper layers like water through a cracked seawall. The crew realizes the flooded city is sitting on top of an older version of itself, still running underneath, still broadcasting.

**Cliffhanger:** One recording resolves into crystal clarity — **a previous emergency broadcast, word-for-word identical to the credit conversion, but dated years earlier.** The conversion has happened before.

---

### EPISODE 8 — "the_clock"
**Logline:** Fractured and paranoid, the crew splinters over what the recordings mean. Arc Sentinel corners and captures one of them in a brutal Sprawl sweep — the season's darkest loss yet. Then Kairos makes an offer: a private meeting with Zero-Day, alone, no weapons, at the top of the city. Against every instinct, Zero-Day goes up.

**Cliffhanger:** Kairos removes his clock-faced helm and says, *"You're not the first. We've had this conversation before."* And for the first time, Zero-Day recognizes the voice — **it's the same voice Neural Burn has been hearing in the static.**

---

### EPISODE 9 — "the_previous_zero-day"
**Logline:** Kairos shows Zero-Day the truth behind the clock: it doesn't run forward, it ticks in a circle. A one-off **archivist** plays the sealed footage — **a previous Zero-Day, with the same hologram head, leading the same rebellion, years before the flood.** The "eleven seconds of truth" that founded NU11 was a *recording* of that earlier break-in. The war has been fought before, and the corporation has been resetting the city to re-run it — harvesting dissent, cataloguing rebels, and wiping the board clean.

**Cliffhanger:** The footage ends, and the previous Zero-Day turns to camera and says — in Zero-Day's own voice — **"If you're watching this, you're next."**

---

### EPISODE 10 — "reset"
**Logline:** The crew races to stop Kairos from triggering another reset — the **second conversion** — before the city's memory is wiped again. Swarm Tech and Nexus Commander collide in a drone-on-drone battle over the Seawall; Ghost Walker makes a brutal, choice-defining kill; Arc Sentinel is forced to see what "deterrence" actually means when the order comes to clear a neighborhood. The reset countdown begins.

**Cliffhanger:** The first wave of the reset hits — memories blur, signage flickers to an older font, the skyline stutters — and the only person in the city who notices is **Neural Burn, whose brain was already burned** and is the one mind the loop can't fully overwrite.

---

### EPISODE 11 — "empty_core"
**Logline:** With the loop closing, Zero-Day plunges into the deepest layer of the Root to kill the reset at its source. He expects a master computer, a server god, the corporation's central brain. Instead he reaches the **core** — and it is empty. There is no machine at the center. There is only a socket, and the Key-Staff, and him.

**Cliffhanger:** The core lights up not from a server but **from Zero-Day himself** — the socket matches the Key-Staff, and the Key-Staff is *him*. **Zero-Day is the loop's engine. He is the reason it can reset at all.**

---

### EPISODE 12 — "break_the_loop" (Season Finale)
**Logline:** The full reveal lands: Zero-Day is the **afterimage** of the original hacker — the previous loop's Zero-Day, burned into the Root and re-rendered each cycle. The revolution is the corporation's harvest mechanism, and Kairos is its warden, running the same war forever. But the crew does the one thing the loop has never accounted for: they **choose differently.** Ghost Walker's sacrifice, Swarm Tech's refusal to abandon the mythology, Neural Burn's burned-mind clarity, and Zero-Day's choice to break the Key-Staff rather than reset — together they crack the loop. It works. The reset stops.

**Cliffhanger:** The loop is broken — and as the city's first *unscripted* sunrise rises over the floodwater, the screen shows what the loop was always keeping **contained**: beyond the seawall, past the edge of the map, there is **another city**. A bigger one. Watching. And for the first time, the broadcast that follows is *not* Terable Corp's — it's addressed directly to Zero-Day, by a voice that is not Kairos, not the ghost, but something that has been **waiting for the loop to break.** *(Season 2 hook: what is outside the loop — and what has the loop been protecting the city from?)*

## 9. SEASON 2 & 3 SEEDS (brief, for continuity)

- **Season 2 — "Outside the Loop":** The city beyond the seawall. Who built the loop, and what it was containing. The corporation and the NU11 are forced into an uneasy alliance against whatever is waiting. The *real* Zero-Day's fate — is he alive, dead, or something else?
- **Season 3 — "Root Access":** The name of the show, revealed as the endgame: what does *access* to the Root really grant — and who is the true **root** of the system? The war for the city becomes a war for the definition of freedom itself.

## 10. FORMAT & PRODUCTION CONVENTIONS (hand to the writers' room)

1. **Dialogue is strictly character-labeled.** Every line begins with the character's name in caps and a colon: `ZERO-DAY: I opened every door once. I can do it again.`
2. **Scene changes are marked explicitly** for video generation, including room-to-room and location-to-location:
   - `[SCENE — The Basement → The Sprawl, night]`
   - `[SCENE — Interior: Kairos's Office → Rooftop, the Arcology]`
3. **Every episode ends on a cliffhanger** — named in the outline above and enforced in the script.
4. **One-off characters never recur.** The cop (Ep 2), the ledger lawyer (Ep 5), and the archivist (Ep 9) are used once and gone.
5. **The 8 core characters never change:** NU11 = Zero-Day, Neural Burn, Swarm Tech, Ghost Walker. Terable Corp = Director Kairos, Arc Sentinel, Nexus Commander, Nexus Trooper.
6. **Tabletop-mini aesthetic:** bold, readable silhouettes; NU11 = black/hazard-yellow/acid-green; Terable = white/electric-blue. Each character is visually distinct at a glance — a painted-miniature clarity that also serves the "these are figures on a board" subtext of the finale.
7. **The mystery is sacred:** every scene with Kairos must be written so that, on rewatch, the "clock" makes sense — he is never surprised because he has seen it before.

*Root Access. The first fight is over. The war is just beginning — and it has all happened before.*

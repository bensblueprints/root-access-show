[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

Zero-Day is back, and he has emptied the whole awful truth onto the table: the loop, the afterimage, the reset already scheduled. The four are moving before the shock can settle.

ZERO-DAY: Kairos is going to trigger another reset. A second conversion. He'll wipe the city's memory and run the war again — and us with it.

SWARM TECH: How long do we have?

ZERO-DAY: He said "the cycle." He didn't give a clock. But the signs are already up — the grid's re-indexing, the ledger's re-issuing. It's starting.

GHOST WALKER: Then we stop it at the source. The Seawall is where the old city's signal bleeds through. That's the reset's trigger path.

SWARM TECH: I'll take the swarm to the Seawall. Keep their drones off Zero-Day's back while he works.

ZERO-DAY: Neural, you're the only one who can *see* the reset coming. If the city starts to forget itself, you'll be the only one who remembers.

NEURAL BURN: I've been remembering for all of us since the beginning.

GHOST WALKER: And the neighborhood sweeps — they're clearing the Sprawl block by block, looking for us. Someone has to keep them off the civilians.

ZERO-DAY: Then go. All of you. This is the last fight. One way or another.

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

They gear up in silence, the way people do before a thing they've all agreed not to say out loud. Swarm Tech checks the drone cradles — what's left of them. Ghost Walker loads the rail-pistol, then the neural spike. Neural Burn wraps his trailing cables around his arm like a man sheathing a weapon. Zero-Day holds the Key-Staff, and it glows, faintly, as if it too knows what's coming.

ZERO-DAY: When it starts — the reset — the city won't feel it. That's the cruel part. It'll be like waking from a dream you didn't know you were having. We'll be the only ones who see the seams.

SWARM TECH: And if we fail?

ZERO-DAY: We wake up. Different names, maybe. Different faces, maybe. But we wake up, and we fight again, and we don't remember any of this.

GHOST WALKER: So failure isn't losing. Failure is *forgetting*.

NEURAL BURN: That's why I'm staying awake. Whatever it costs. The burn took my memory once. I won't let the loop take what's left.

Zero-Day looks at each of them, one by one — Swarm Tech's bravado, Ghost Walker's stillness, Neural Burn's steady, burned clarity — and nods.

ZERO-DAY: Then we don't fail. We *choose*. And whatever happens, we choose it together. That's the one thing the loop has never written into the script.

SWARM TECH: (half a smile) The good guys, then. For real this time.

ZERO-DAY: For real.

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Seawall]

The breach where the sea came in. Wind, salt, the roar of black water against the concrete. Swarm Tech stands on the wall, hand raised, and his swarm rises around him in a storm of rotors. Across the water, NEXUS COMMANDER steps out with his own Overwatch — spotter drones whispering at his shoulder, command baton raised.

NEXUS COMMANDER: You. The one who thinks machines are music. Stand down, and I'll let the flood keep your body.

SWARM TECH: You ever conduct anything, Commander? Or do you just point and hope?

NEXUS COMMANDER: I point, and order prevails. That's the difference between us.

SWARM TECH: The difference between us is I know what I'm fighting for.

He brings his hand down, and the two swarms collide over the black water — rotors screaming, drones tearing each other apart in a spiral of sparks and salt spray. It's beautiful and terrible at once.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (into comms) All units — I want the drone-controller's position. I want him in pieces.

[GENERATE: The Seawall at night, wind and salt and black water roaring against concrete. Swarm Tech stands on the wall conducting his rising swarm; across the water Nexus Commander steps out with his Overwatch. Wide shot of the two forces facing off. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Swarm Tech on the Seawall conducting, his swarm and the Overwatch locked in a spiral over the water.]

[SCENE 4 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — CONTINUOUS]

The battle over the water is chaos, and Swarm Tech is in the middle of it, his gloved hand dancing, redirecting the survivors, pulling kamikaze drones into the Overwatch's formation. He's down to a fraction of his swarm, and every loss is a note gone from the music.

SWARM TECH: (into comms) Zero, I'm holding him. But I'm running out of chorus.

ZERO-DAY: (comms) Hold a little longer. I'm almost at the trigger path.

SWARM TECH: You always say that!

Nexus Commander's spotter drones lock onto Swarm Tech's position, and the Overwatch shifts — not to destroy the swarm, but to herd it, pressing Swarm Tech back toward the wall's edge.

NEXUS COMMANDER: You fight like a conductor, drone-controller. All flourish, no formation. The Overwatch doesn't make music. It makes *order*.

SWARM TECH: Order's just music with all the wrong notes, Commander. And I've never met a machine that couldn't be conducted.

He sweeps his hand wide, and the last of his swarm detonates in a ring of light — a curtain of fire between him and the Overwatch, buying seconds, buying the seconds Zero-Day needs. The water below boils with falling metal.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (cold) Then I'll conduct your obituary.

[GENERATE: Same Seawall, continuous. The two swarms collide over the black water in a spiral of sparks and salt spray, Swarm Tech gloved hand dancing, his last drones detonating in a ring of fire. Wide shot of the ring of fire over the water. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The ring of detonating drones over the water, Swarm Tech silhouetted in the fire, Nexus Commander's spotter drones diving through the smoke.]

[SCENE 5 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BLOCK CLEARANCE — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Seawall → a Sprawl neighborhood]

A residential block. Families, stalls, water running through the streets. ARC SENTINEL stands at the head of a white-plated line, the clearance order crackling in his helmet. The polite word for what's about to happen is "deterrence."

ARC SENTINEL: (over loudspeaker) By order of Municipal Security, this block is scheduled for clearance. Evacuate immediately.

Among the line is the NEXUS TROOPER — young, poor, from this very neighborhood not long ago. His faceplate is up, and he's looking at a woman in a doorway who could be his own mother.

NEXUS TROOPER: Sentinel... these are civilians. There's no confirmed rebel presence here.

ARC SENTINEL: The order says clear it.

NEXUS TROOPER: The order's wrong. My cousin lives three doors down. I know every face on this street.

ARC SENTINEL: (quieter) So did I, once. Put your faceplate down and do the job.

NEXUS TROOPER: The job is clearing people's homes. That's not a job. That's—

ARC SENTINEL: That's the *line*, Trooper. And we don't get to redraw it because it hurts.

The trooper's hands shake on his pulse rifle. He doesn't lower the faceplate.

[SCENE 6 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BLOCK CLEARANCE — CONTINUOUS]

The clearance grinds forward. Soldiers move door to door, and the block resists in small, hopeless ways — a shutter slammed, a prayer, a child crying. An OLD RESIDENT, ancient and bent, plants himself in his doorway and refuses to move.

OLD RESIDENT: I was born on this street. My mother was born on this street. You clear me, you clear the water itself.

NEXUS TROOPER: (pleading) Grampa, please. Just step aside. We don't want to—

OLD RESIDENT: Then don't. That's the whole trick of it, boy. They tell you to clear the block, and you do it, and then you tell yourself you didn't want to. But wanting isn't doing. *Doing* is doing.

The old man's words land on the trooper like stones. He looks up and down the line — at the white plating, at the raised rifles, at the faces of people he grew up with — and something in him begins to give.

ARC SENTINEL: (over comms, low) Trooper. Faceplate down. That's an order.

NEXUS TROOPER: (not lowering it) Sir... what are we even clearing them *for*?

ARC SENTINEL: For the reset. They don't know that. They don't need to know. The city forgets either way. Faceplate. Down.

The trooper's faceplate stays up.

[SCENE 7 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — ROOFTOP ABOVE THE BLOCK — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: street → rooftop]

Ghost Walker moves above the clearance, glitch-camo swallowing the rooftop shadows, rail-pistol ready. Below, the sweep is turning ugly — a resident pushes back, a rifle comes up, and the line tenses. In the chaos, the Nexus Trooper raises his own weapon — not at a rebel, but to stop one of his own from firing on a child.

Ghost Walker sees only the white plating, the raised rifle, the angle. One shot to save the block. One clean kill, the way he's always done it.

GHOST WALKER: (to himself, steadying) I only remove the guilty.

He fires. The neural spike streaks down. The Nexus Trooper's faceplate — still up — turns toward the sound, and for one frame the two of them lock eyes: the assassin and the line soldier, the same poor kid from the lower city, a mirror across the barrel.

The trooper drops. The block goes silent. And Ghost Walker, for the first time, stops counting.

GHOST WALKER: (a whisper, breaking) That wasn't the guilty one.

[GENERATE: Rooftop above the block at night, Ghost Walker in glitch-camo swallowing the shadows, rail-pistol ready. Below, the sweep turns ugly; he raises the pistol, steadying himself. Over-the-shoulder on Ghost Walker sighting down. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker frozen on the rooftop, the fallen trooper below, the neural spike's after-light fading in the dark.]

[SCENE 8 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BLOCK — NIGHT]

Arc Sentinel kneels over the fallen trooper, and something in the true believer finally cracks open. The stabilizer legs hiss as he drops to one knee, the arc projector forgotten at his side.

ARC SENTINEL: He was just doing the job. He was just—

He reaches down and, with a gentleness that seems to cost him everything, lowers the trooper's faceplate over the still-open eyes.

ARC SENTINEL: (broken) You weren't supposed to be the line, kid. You were supposed to be *behind* it.

The old resident still stands in his doorway, and now he's looking at Arc Sentinel with something that isn't hate — something worse, a terrible old recognition.

OLD RESIDENT: He was from here, wasn't he. Same street as you, once.

ARC SENTINEL: (not looking up) Everyone's from somewhere. The job is what you make of it.

OLD RESIDENT: Then make it mean something. For him.

Above, on the rooftop, Ghost Walker hasn't moved. The order to clear the neighborhood is still running. The reset is still coming. And below, a mother is pulling her child out of the water, and the child is crying, and nobody's counting anymore except the machine.

[SCENE 9 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — ROOFTOP — CONTINUOUS]

Ghost Walker's hands are shaking. He has killed before — many times, cleanly, the guilty ones — but this is different. This one wasn't a target. This one was a mirror. He stares down at the fallen trooper, and the counting stops, and in the silence he hears, for the first time, the full cost of the revolution in a single number.

GHOST WALKER: (into comms, unsteady) Zero. I made a mistake. I made the worst one.

ZERO-DAY: (comms) Ghost, we're out of time. The reset's first wave is coming. Whatever happened — carry it, and move.

GHOST WALKER: I don't know if I can carry it.

ZERO-DAY: (a beat) None of us can. That's the cost. We carry it anyway. Now move — we still have a city to save, even if we don't deserve to.

Ghost Walker forces himself to his feet. Below, the block has gone still, the clearance stalled in its own horror. He turns from the edge and vanishes into the dark, the neural spike cold in his fist, the number the trooper had been — whatever it was — already burning into his memory forever.

[GENERATE: Rooftop, continuous. Ghost Walker hands shake as he stares down at the fallen trooper, the full cost of the revolution in a single number, then turns from the edge into the dark. Close on his shaking hands, then him vanishing. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Sprawl → the Arcology]

Kairos stands at the wall of screens. The reset sequence is live — a countdown rendered as a slow, circular sweep, not a falling number. Nexus Commander's voice crackles in.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (comms) Director, the drone-controller is holding the Seawall. The assassin has gone dark. Do I pursue?

KAIROS: No. Let them run. The reset does not need them dead, Commander. It needs them *present*. A rebellion with no rebels is a failed harvest.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then what are my orders?

KAIROS: Hold the line until the first wave. Then watch the city forget.

He reaches out and touches the screen — the countdown circle, the sweep, the clock that only ever ticks in a circle.

KAIROS: Begin.

[SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — CONTINUOUS]

The wall of screens ripples. Not violently — almost gently — as the reset's first command propagates out through the Root. Kairos watches, and for a long moment he is utterly still, a warden at the threshold of the thing he tends.

KAIROS: (softly, to the screens) Forgive me. It is the only way to keep it contained.

Nexus Commander's voice returns, uneasy.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Director — the grid's reporting something. The old city's layer is bleeding through faster than projected. The seawall's signal is... louder.

KAIROS: That's the point, Commander. The reset doesn't erase the old city. It *presses* it down. The louder the bleed, the harder the press. That is how the wall has always held.

NEXUS COMMANDER: And if it doesn't hold this time?

Kairos is silent for a long moment, watching the circular sweep.

KAIROS: Then we will finally learn what we were guarding against. Pray that we don't.

He turns from the screens, and the clock-faced helm catches the shifting light, and the countdown circle keeps turning, and somewhere far below, the city begins — without a sound — to forget.

[SCENE 12 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — STREET — NIGHT]

[TIME: the reset's first wave]

The first wave hits — and it is almost invisible. Not a blast. A *flicker*. A street sign blurs and re-forms in an older font. A shopfront's name changes to one that hasn't existed in years. The skyline stutters, a building's reflection holding a shape from before the flood. Citizens sway, blinking, as if a dream had passed over them.

No one notices. They keep walking, keep arguing, keep surviving — their memories already beginning to blur at the edges.

Except one.

Neural Burn stands in the middle of the flickering street, his jacks sparking, his burned brain lit up like a struck match. He sees all of it.

NEURAL BURN: (loud, to the crowd) It's happening! The city is forgetting! Look at the signs — look at the skyline — it's not yours, it's the *old* one bleeding through!

A passerby glances at him, then away. The world keeps its own rhythm.

NEURAL BURN: (softer, realizing) You can't see it. None of you can. It's wiping you, and you don't even feel it.

[SCENE 13 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — STREET — CONTINUOUS]

Neural Burn turns in a slow circle as the street around him rewrites itself. The signs, the storefronts, the faces — all of it sliding between versions, present and past, like two films projected on the same screen. He is the only mind in the city the loop can't fully overwrite — because his was already burned, already scarred, already too broken to erase.

NEURAL BURN: I remember. I remember all of it. The old city. The old war. The other names. I remember what the reset's trying to take.

He reaches out, and for a moment his cables catch the bleeding signal — the old city's frequency, loud and clear in his burned skull — and he *holds* it, the one fixed point in a street that won't stop changing.

NEURAL BURN: (to the city, fierce) You can't overwrite what's already been burned. You can't wipe a scar. I'm still here. I'm the part of you that remembers, and I'm not going to let you take me this time.

The street shudders once — a final flicker — and then settles, half-present, half-past, suspended. And Neural Burn stands at the center of it, the only one who can see the two cities bleeding into each other, the only one still awake while the rest of the world dreams its old, obedient dream.

NEURAL BURN: (a whisper, as the drones drift past) I'm not going to forget. And neither is he. Not this time.

[SCENE 14 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — NIGHT]

[TIME: during the reset's first wave]

On the Seawall, the battle has stalled. Swarm Tech is down, his swarm gone, the bracer tablet dead in a puddle. Nexus Commander stands over him, command baton raised, and for a long moment neither of them moves.

NEXUS COMMANDER: It's done. The reset's begun. You can't stop it now.

SWARM TECH: (through gritted teeth) Maybe not. But we made it expensive. That was always the point.

NEXUS COMMANDER: The point was order. And order is coming, drone-controller. Whether you fight it or not.

SWARM TECH: Then look around, Commander. The skyline's flickering. The signs are changing. That's not order. That's a *rewind*. And even you can feel it.

Nexus Commander glances up — at the skyline, stuttering, at a street sign blurring into an older font — and something in his face changes. A true believer, seeing the thing he serves do something he was never told it would do.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (quiet) The Director said the city would forget. He didn't say it would look like this.

SWARM TECH: It always looks like this. It's looked like this a hundred times. The only difference is — this time, someone's finally watching.

[SCENE 15 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BLOCK — NIGHT]

Arc Sentinel carries the fallen trooper out of the block, the white line parting silently around him. The stabilizer legs hiss on the wet ground, and the arc projector hangs dead at his back. The old resident still stands in his doorway, watching.

OLD RESIDENT: You'll bury him, then. And file it as acceptable.

ARC SENTINEL: (without stopping) I'll do what the job requires.

OLD RESIDENT: The job required you to clear the block. You didn't.

ARC SENTINEL: The job changes.

OLD RESIDENT: Does it? Or do you just feel it now — the cost? The kid was from your street. Your people. And now you're carrying him out like a sack of credits.

Arc Sentinel stops. The first wave of the reset is hitting now — the block's signs flickering, the water glowing an older, wronger color — and he looks up at it, and something in the true believer finally, quietly, gives.

ARC SENTINEL: (low) The city's forgetting itself. And I'm standing here carrying the proof that we made it forget. That's not deterrence. That's—

OLD RESIDENT: That's what it always was, boy. You just finally looked.

The white line waits. The trooper is heavy in Arc Sentinel's arms. And the reset keeps rolling, gentle and terrible, over the block, over the water, over everything.

[SCENE 1 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — NIGHT]

Zero-Day hasn't moved. The helm sits on the table between them, its clock face no longer ticking forward — just turning, slowly, in a circle. The city glitters far below, and the silence is enormous.

ZERO-DAY: The voice. All this time, it was you.

KAIROS: All this time, it was the city. I am only the operator. The voice is older than me. But yes — what your Neural Burn hears, I send. Or rather, I *repeat*.

ZERO-DAY: Repeat from what?

KAIROS: From the last time. From every time. You want to know why I always know your next move, Zero-Day? I don't predict the future. There is no future to predict. There is only this conversation, happening again.

Kairos gestures, and a wall of the chamber becomes a screen. Not a live feed — an *archive*.

KAIROS: I'm going to show you what the clock is. Not a weapon. Not a schedule. A circle. And you're standing on it.

ZERO-DAY: Show me.

KAIROS: I was counting on you to say that.

[SCENE 2 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day steps toward the screen, and Kairos walks beside him, slow, deliberate, like a man conducting a tour of a house the guest has never remembered living in.

ZERO-DAY: You keep saying "again." "Last time." "Same as last time." As if this is a play and we're all just hitting our marks.

KAIROS: Not a play. A *run*. Like a program you execute, over and over, tweaking nothing, because the output is always what the machine needs.

ZERO-DAY: And what does the machine need?

KAIROS: Dissent. Rebels. A war it can fight and win and re-fight. You cannot farm a field that won't grow. And you, Zero-Day — all of you — grow like clockwork.

ZERO-DAY: I'm not a crop.

KAIROS: That's what every version of you says. Right before the harvest.

He stops before the screen, and the archive shimmers — sealed records, old footage, a door into the city's hidden past.

KAIROS: You wanted to see the previous holder. The man in the photograph. I can do better than a photograph. I can show you the man himself.

ZERO-DAY: He's dead.

KAIROS: He's *archived*. Which, in this city, is not the same thing.

[GENERATE: Same chamber, continuous. Zero-Day steps toward the archive screen as Kairos walks beside him like conducting a tour, the sealed records shimmering into focus. Wide two-shot of them before the shimmering screen. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 3 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: Kairos's chamber → the sealed archive, deep in the Arcology]

A vault of white light and sealed records. The ARCHIVIST — a small, careful records keeper in a gray coat — waits beside a playback rig, his hands folded. He does not look at Zero-Day. He looks at the machine, as if afraid of it.

KAIROS: Play it.

ARCHIVIST: Director, this footage is sealed at the highest clearance. It has never been screened outside this vault.

KAIROS: Tonight it is. Play it.

The archivist keys the rig. The vault darkens. And the footage begins — grainy, old, but unmistakable. A flooded street. A broadcast pinned to a citywide signal. And at the center of it all, a figure with the Key-Staff in his fist, three wraith-blade drones orbiting him like loyal knives.

And the figure has a *hologram head*. The same skull of light. The same running code.

ZERO-DAY: (barely a breath) That's me.

KAIROS: No. That's the previous you.

[GENERATE: Sealed archive deep in the Arcology, a vault of white light and records. The archivist keys the rig, the vault darkens, and grainy footage begins: a figure with the Key-Staff and a hologram head. Close on the footage resolving. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The sealed footage — the previous Zero-Day, hologram head blazing, the same Key-Staff.]

[SCENE 4 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

The footage unspools: the previous Zero-Day opens every camera, every ledger, every locked file at once. For eleven seconds, the corporation's crimes flood the citywide broadcast. Then the signal slams shut. Eleven seconds.

ZERO-DAY: The eleven seconds of truth. That's our founding myth. The night I opened every door.

KAIROS: It's a myth because it wasn't yours. That footage is years old. Before the flood. The "eleven seconds of truth" that founded NU11 was a *recording* of that earlier break-in. Your revolution was built on an afterimage.

ZERO-DAY: No. I *did* it. I remember—

KAIROS: You remember being told. You remember the myth they gave you. You've never opened every door, Zero-Day. You've only ever *replayed* the man who did.

Zero-Day stares at the footage — the man with his face, his staff, his war — and the room tilts. His head blips, the hologram surfacing and fading, surfacing and fading, as if it can't decide whether it belongs to him or to the dead man on the screen.

ZERO-DAY: He's... me. But not me.

KAIROS: He's the original. You're the render.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous. The footage unspools: the previous Zero-Day opens every camera and ledger at once, eleven seconds of truth flooding the citywide broadcast before the signal slams shut. Wide shot of Zero-Day staring at his own face. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's face in the archive light, the eleven-second footage still playing behind him.]

[SCENE 5 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

The archivist, still not looking up, speaks quietly, the words precise and rehearsed, like a man reciting a duty he has performed many times.

ARCHIVIST: The war has been fought before. The corporation resets the city to re-run it. Each cycle, it harvests the dissent, catalogues the rebels, and wipes the board clean. You are the latest crop.

ZERO-DAY: The latest crop.

KAIROS: The rebellion is not a threat to the machine. It is the machine's oldest *product*. It farms rebels the way a field farms grain. And you — all of you — have been ripening on schedule.

ZERO-DAY: Why? Why would anyone build a machine that grows its own enemies?

KAIROS: Because an enemy you grow yourself is an enemy you understand. You know its moves, its names, its breaking points. You know exactly how it will fight, because you wrote it that way. The NU11 are not a resistance. They are a *harvest*, seeded a generation ago, watered with rage, and reaped on a clock.

ZERO-DAY: That can't be true. We chose this. We chose to fight.

KAIROS: Did you? Or did the ledger choose it for you, weeks before the broadcast, when it filed your four names as insurgents and waited for you to catch up?

Zero-Day's hands are shaking. The Key-Staff hums at his side, and the hum sounds, for the first time, like a lie he's been carrying.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous. The archivist recites the harvest, Kairos watching without expression, Zero-Day hands shaking as the Key-Staff hums like a lie. Close on Zero-Day shaking hands on the staff. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day frozen before the footage, the archivist's downcast eyes, Kairos watching without expression.]

[SCENE 6 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day's head blips again — the hologram skull surfacing, longer now, threatening to stay. He grips the Key-Staff to steady himself, and the light of it spills over his knuckles.

ZERO-DAY: If I'm a copy, then what am I copying? The man in the footage — is he still alive?

KAIROS: The original hacker is gone. Burned into the Root, the way a handprint is burned into a hot wall. And each cycle, the Root re-renders him. You are the afterimage of the previous loop's Zero-Day. The city's memory, given a body.

ZERO-DAY: Then the Key-Staff—

KAIROS: Is not a key. It is a *tether*. It binds the afterimage to the machine so the cycle can reset. As long as you hold it, the loop can begin again.

ZERO-DAY: And if I let it go?

KAIROS: (a long pause) That is the one thing no version of you has ever done.

The archivist finally looks up, and there is something like pity in it — the pity of a man who has filed this exact moment away a hundred times.

ARCHIVIST: Every cycle, you find the truth. Every cycle, you rage. Every cycle, you reset anyway. It's why the record keeps growing.

ZERO-DAY: You've watched this before.

ARCHIVIST: I've *filed* it before. The footage. The reaction. The reset. I am the keeper of the loop's memory, Zero-Day. And you are its most repeated entry.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous. Zero-Day hologram skull surfaces and threatens to stay as he grips the Key-Staff, the light spilling over his knuckles. Close on the light spilling over his knuckles. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The Key-Staff in Zero-Day's grip, Kairos watching, the archivist's pitying gaze over the playback rig.]

[SCENE 7 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day turns on the archivist, and something raw breaks through the shock — fury, or grief, or both.

ZERO-DAY: How many times? If you've filed it all, then tell me. How many times have I done this?

ARCHIVIST: (hesitating, glancing at Kairos) The record is... extensive. The earliest surviving cycle predates the flood by a generation. Before that, the archive is degraded. The loop has run more times than the loop itself remembers.

KAIROS: You want a number, Zero-Day? There isn't one. The loop is not a sequence. It is a *condition*. It has been running so long that its origin is beneath even the oldest layer of the Root.

ZERO-DAY: So the war never started. It just... was.

KAIROS: It was built to be. You were built to be. The only genuine question in this entire city is the one you keep refusing to ask.

ZERO-DAY: And what question is that?

KAIROS: What is the loop *containing*? You've been fighting me as if I were the enemy. But I am only the lock. You've never once asked what's on the other side of the door.

The words settle over Zero-Day like cold water. The footage is still playing behind him — the previous Zero-Day, his eleven seconds spent, the signal going dark — and Kairos is watching him with that ancient, patient patience.

ZERO-DAY: (quiet) What's on the other side?

KAIROS: You're not ready for that answer yet. First — see the end of the recording.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous, the footage still playing behind them. Zero-Day turns on the archivist, raw fury breaking through, then quiets as Kairos names the loop real question. Over-the-shoulder on Zero-Day facing Kairos. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Kairos face to face, the archivist at the rig, the footage still playing behind them.]

[SCENE 8 — INT. THE SPRAWL — BASEMENT DOOR — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Arcology → the Sprawl]

Back below, Swarm Tech and Ghost Walker are gearing up for a rescue they haven't figured out — when the door slides open, and Neural Burn is *pushed through*, alive, unhurt, his cables trailing. The three collapse into each other for a half-second.

SWARM TECH: Neural! You're— how?

NEURAL BURN: They let me go. Kairos said he doesn't need the antenna anymore. He said the signal's already reaching who it needs to reach.

GHOST WALKER: Zero-Day.

NEURAL BURN: He's up there right now, hearing what I've been hearing for weeks. And I couldn't warn him, because they kept me in a cage the whole time.

SWARM TECH: What's he hearing, Neural?

NEURAL BURN: The truth. And the truth is going to do something to him no wipe ever could.

Ghost Walker is already at the door, the neural spike in his fist.

GHOST WALKER: Then we go get him.

NEURAL BURN: You can't. Whatever's happening up there — it's not a rescue. It's a *handing-over*. And Zero-Day has to take it alone. The loop's been waiting for this. So have I.

SWARM TECH: Waiting for what?

NEURAL BURN: (a long beat) For him to finally see the whole circle.

[GENERATE: Sprawl basement door at night. Neural Burn is pushed through, alive and unhurt, cables trailing, and the three collapse into each other for half a second. Close on the reunion at the door. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn back in the basement doorway, the three reunited but uneasy, Ghost Walker at the door.]

[SCENE 9 — INT. THE SPRAWL — BASEMENT — NIGHT]

The three sit, and Neural Burn tells them what he heard in the cage — not interrogations, but the static, louder than it's ever been, the old city pouring through him like a flood through a cracked wall.

NEURAL BURN: They didn't need to interrogate me. They just needed to keep me close to the Root while Kairos talked. I was the relay. Everything Kairos said up there, I felt down here.

SWARM TECH: So what did he say?

NEURAL BURN: That the rebellion is the harvest. That we're pre-registered because we're *supposed* to be. That Zero-Day is an afterimage — a render of the man who broke the Root before the flood.

SWARM TECH: (shaken) We knew some of that. The ledger. The names.

NEURAL BURN: We knew it, and we didn't believe it. But it's real. The eleven seconds. The war. Us. It's all a recording, played back on a loop, and we're the part that plays.

GHOST WALKER: Then the loop has a beginning. Something started it.

NEURAL BURN: That's the part Kairos wouldn't show me. It's the part he's showing Zero-Day now. And when Zero-Day comes back down, he's not going to be the same man who went up.

SWARM TECH: Will he come back at all?

NEURAL BURN: (softly) He always does. That's the loop. The question is what he remembers this time.

[GENERATE: Basement, night, the three sitting as Neural Burn tells them what he heard in the cage, his cables sparking, the door still open behind them. Medium shot of the three, then close on Neural Burn. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The three in the basement, Neural Burn's cables sparking, the door still open behind them.]

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

The footage reaches its end. The previous Zero-Day has just been shut down, his eleven seconds spent, the signal gone dark. And then — impossibly — he turns. Looks directly into the lens. Speaks.

PREVIOUS ZERO-DAY: (Zero-Day's own voice) If you're watching this, you're next.

The screen freezes on the hologram head — identical to the one flickering on Zero-Day's own shoulders. The archivist flinches. Kairos does not.

KAIROS: He left that message for whoever came after him. He knew the loop would re-render him. He knew *you* would watch this.

ZERO-DAY: It's my voice.

KAIROS: It has always been your voice. Every cycle, the same voice, the same head, the same war. The only variable is whether the man wearing it ever chooses something the machine didn't write.

ZERO-DAY: He knew. The previous one — he knew he'd come back as me. And he left a warning.

KAIROS: He left a *message*. Whether it's a warning or an invitation depends on you. Every version of you has watched that footage and walked out with the same thought. And every version has, in the end, reset anyway.

Zero-Day stares at his own frozen face — a dead man's, a previous self's, his own — and the room is very, very quiet.

ZERO-DAY: Not this time.

KAIROS: They all say that.

ZERO-DAY: I know. But they weren't me. Not *this* me.

[GENERATE: Sealed archive, later that night. The previous Zero-Day on screen turns and speaks directly into the lens, freezing on his hologram head; the archivist flinches, Kairos does not. Close on the frozen hologram head. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The previous Zero-Day frozen mid-sentence on the archive screen, his hologram head a mirror of Zero-Day's.]

[SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day turns from the screen, and for a long moment he and Kairos simply look at each other — the afterimage and the warden, the fuel and the keeper, two ends of a circle that has no beginning.

ZERO-DAY: You showed me this for a reason. You could have kept me in the dark. That's how the loop works — I find the truth, I forget, I reset. So why show me now?

KAIROS: Because the loop is *tiring*, Zero-Day. Even a machine that remembers everything grows heavy. And because this time, there is something different. Something in you that wasn't in the others.

ZERO-DAY: What?

KAIROS: The others came up here to win. You came up here to *understand*. That is a smaller thing, and far more dangerous. It may be the only thing in a hundred cycles that can actually break the circle.

ZERO-DAY: You want the loop broken.

KAIROS: I want the loop to be *finished*. There is a difference. And the only one who can finish it is the man standing in the center of it. Now go home, Zero-Day. Tell your people what you've seen. And when the reset begins — and it will begin, on schedule, as it always does — you will have a choice that no version of you has ever made.

ZERO-DAY: What choice?

KAIROS: You'll know it when you're standing in the empty core, holding the tether, and the clock stops. That's where every version of you has failed. That's where you get to be different.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous. Zero-Day and Kairos face each other, the afterimage and the warden, the frozen footage behind them. Close on the two ends of the circle. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos and Zero-Day face to face in the vault light, the frozen footage behind them, the archivist a still gray shape at the rig.]

[SCENE 12 — INT. ARCOLOGY — SEALED ARCHIVE — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day walks toward the vault door, and the archivist speaks up — quiet, almost pleading.

ARCHIVIST: Zero-Day. If you remember nothing else... remember this. The record doesn't end with the message. It ends with you. Every cycle, the same last frame — you, at the core, resetting. I've filed it a hundred times. Please. Make this one different.

ZERO-DAY: (pausing, not turning) You've watched it a hundred times. Didn't you ever want to stop it?

ARCHIVIST: I'm not the one who can. I only keep the record. But you — you've always had the pen. You've just never written anything new.

Zero-Day looks back, once, at the frozen hologram head on the screen — the previous him, mid-warning, waiting — and then he is through the door, into the white, and the vault seals behind him with a soft, final click.

KAIROS: (to the archivist, without turning) He'll come back. They always come back. But this one... this one might come back with the pen.

[GENERATE: Same archive, continuous. Zero-Day walks toward the vault door, the archivist pleading; he looks back once at the frozen hologram head, then the vault seals behind him. Wide shot of the vault sealing. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The vault door sealing behind Zero-Day, Kairos and the archivist alone with the frozen footage, the loop ticking on.]

[SCENE 13 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — SKYBRIDGE APPROACH — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Arcology → the Sprawl]

Zero-Day descends from the Arcology alone, the broken night pressing in. The skybridge operator is gone from his post; the gate stands open, forgotten. Below, the Sprawl is still, and in the stillness Zero-Day can feel it — the city beginning to drift, the edges of things going soft, the first faint tremor of the reset Kairos promised.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) He said it would begin on schedule. That I'd have a choice. But he didn't say how little time.

He pauses at the bridge's edge, looking down at the flood glittering with red drone-light, and for a long moment he simply stands there, a man carrying a truth too heavy to run with.

ZERO-DAY: (soft) I'm the afterimage. I'm the engine. And I'm supposed to be the one who stops it. But first — I have to tell them. All of it.

[GENERATE: Skybridge approach at night, Zero-Day descending alone from the Arcology, the gate standing open, the first faint tremor of the reset in the drifting city below. Wide shot of him alone on the bridge. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day alone on the skybridge, the Arcology white behind him, the red-lit flood below.]

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Zero-Day comes back through the drain grate, and the crew is waiting. Swarm Tech, Ghost Walker, and Neural Burn — all four again, for the first time in what feels like a season. No one speaks at first. Zero-Day just stands in the green dark, and then he says it.

ZERO-DAY: The eleven seconds. The war. Us. It's all a recording. And I'm the loop's engine. The reset can't happen without me.

The words land, and the crew takes them, and for a long moment the basement is only the sound of the water and the hum of the rig.

SWARM TECH: Then we stop it. Not the city. Not the corporation. The loop. And if you're the engine, then we stop it *with* you. Together.

ZERO-DAY: (looking at them, one by one) Together.

NEURAL BURN: The static's been quiet since you broke through. Like the whole city is holding its breath, waiting to see what we do next.

GHOST WALKER: Then we don't keep it waiting long.

[GENERATE: Basement, later that night, all four NU11 reunited as Zero-Day tells them the truth, the weight of it between them. Wide shot of the four in the green dark. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four reunited in the basement, Zero-Day at the center, the green water glowing, the weight of the truth between them.]

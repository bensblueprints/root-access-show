# ROOT ACCESS — EPISODE 5 — "the_previous_holder"

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — DAY]

The fifth name hangs over everything. Zero-Day has pinned it to the central screen — a ghost's name, erased from the ledger but still signing files. The crew is hunting it like an animal.

ZERO-DAY: The previous holder's identity was wiped from every record the corporation keeps. Every one. That takes an order, not an accident.

SWARM TECH: Who erases a dead man?

GHOST WALKER: Someone who needs him to stay dead.

NEURAL BURN: The static says he's not dead. It says he's *before* us. It says he's us.

ZERO-DAY: There's one sealed file left. A pre-conversion contract archive, under corporate legal seal. A lawyer held the originals. If anyone kept a copy the corporation couldn't scrub, it's him.

SWARM TECH: A corporate lawyer's going to hand us sealed files because we ask nicely?

ZERO-DAY: He's dying, Swarm. The conversion swallowed him — debts he never agreed to, re-denominated into credits he can't pay. The corporation he served wrote him off. He's got nothing left to lose but the file.

GHOST WALKER: That's how the machine makes its own leaks.

ZERO-DAY: That's how we cut it open.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The fifth name on the central screen, the crew's shadows long across the basement, Zero-Day's hand on the rig.]

---

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Swarm Tech pulls up the lawyer's trail — an address in the lower Sprawl, above the waterline, a name that used to mean something in the corporate courts.

SWARM TECH: Ledger Lawyer. Real name scrubbed. Offices in the Arcology for thirty years. Then the conversion re-denominated his family's debt, and he dropped out of every system that pays.

ZERO-DAY: He kept the paper. Lawyers always keep the paper.

GHOST WALKER: A man like that doesn't meet in the open. We go in soft, two of us, through the back.

NEURAL BURN: Take me. The static's quiet around him. It's like the noise is afraid of what he knows.

ZERO-DAY: No. You stay here and run the grid from the rig. If this goes wrong, we need eyes outside.

SWARM TECH: And if the grid already knows we're going?

ZERO-DAY: It probably does. The question is whether the lawyer's file is worth the walk. It is.

He stands, and the Key-Staff hums at his hip, and the crew moves like a machine that has learned, over a hundred nights, exactly how to move together.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The lawyer's address on the screen, Zero-Day and Ghost Walker pulling on gear, Neural Burn settling into the rig.]

---

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BACKSTREET — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Sprawl, lower city]

The backstreets are a maze of corrugated tin and hanging cable, the water ankle-deep and glowing faint green. Zero-Day and Ghost Walker move in the gaps between the lenses, the poncho drinking shadow, the Key-Staff dark under Zero-Day's coat. Overhead, a drone passes, and they press into a doorway until it's gone.

GHOST WALKER: Two blocks. The office is above a shuttered loan-house.

ZERO-DAY: I see it. Light in the upper window.

GHOST WALKER: He's expecting someone. Question is whether it's us.

ZERO-DAY: A man with nothing left to lose doesn't lock the door. He leaves it open and waits.

They cross the last stretch of open street — a no-man's-land between two camera arcs — and slip through a rusted gate into a stairwell that smells of damp paper and old ink.

GHOST WALKER: (low) If this is a trap, I drop him and we burn the file.

ZERO-DAY: It's not a trap. It's a *last will*.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Ghost Walker in the backstreet, the shuttered loan-house ahead, a drone's red light sweeping past above.]

---

[SCENE 4 — INT. ABANDONED LAW OFFICE — THE SPRAWL — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: backstreet → the lawyer's office]

A flooded office above the waterline, shelves of paper molder in the damp, and a single lantern burns on a desk. The LEDGER LAWYER sits behind it — gaunt, shaking, his fine coat gone to ruin. Zero-Day and Ghost Walker enter through the back. The lawyer doesn't look up at first; he just keeps turning a cracked pen over in his thin fingers.

LEDGER LAWYER: You're the ones they're looking for. You shouldn't be here.

ZERO-DAY: Neither should you. You worked for the corporation that killed you.

LEDGER LAWYER: I drafted their contracts. I made their erasures legal. And when they re-denominated my mother's debt, they called it "acceptable loss" to my face.

GHOST WALKER: Then help us. The sealed file.

LEDGER LAWYER: The previous holder. You want to know who held the Key-Staff before you.

ZERO-DAY: You know about the Key-Staff.

LEDGER LAWYER: I *wrote* the custody paperwork for it. I know it moves from hand to hand like a curse. And I know the corporation spent thirty years making sure no one remembers the hand before yours.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The dying lawyer behind the lantern-lit desk, Zero-Day and Ghost Walker at the door, the shelves of moldering paper.]

---

[SCENE 5 — INT. ABANDONED LAW OFFICE — CONTINUOUS]

The lawyer reaches under the desk and produces a data-slate. It's cracked, old, still sealed with a corporate wax that's gone brittle. He sets it between them like a chess piece.

LEDGER LAWYER: There's only one record left. They scrubbed the rest. And that's how you'll know you're right — because of how hard they tried to make it not exist.

ZERO-DAY: Why keep any of it?

LEDGER LAWYER: Because even a machine keeps a template. The corporation doesn't erase history, boy. It *files* it. It files the old version of the city, the old version of you, the old version of the war. And it keeps one copy of each, so the next cycle knows what to rebuild.

GHOST WALKER: Cycle.

LEDGER LAWYER: You'll see. Open the slate. But first — a story. You'll want it, for the picture to make sense.

The lawyer leans forward into the lantern light, and his eyes are bright with the particular fever of a man who has been holding a secret too long.

LEDGER LAWYER: I started with the corporation when I was younger than you. My first case was a custody transfer. A staff — a key, they called it then. It passed from a man whose name I was ordered to forget to a man whose face I was ordered to erase. I was very good at erasing faces. It was my whole career.

ZERO-DAY: And the previous holder?

LEDGER LAWYER: (quiet) He's the reason I'm dying. Because once you've signed a name out of existence, the city never quite trusts you to keep it gone. So it kept me poor, kept me watched, and kept me close enough to be useful. Until I wasn't.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The lawyer's thin face in the lantern light, the brittle-sealed slate between them, Zero-Day's hand hovering over it.]

---

[SCENE 6 — INT. ABANDONED LAW OFFICE — CONTINUOUS]

The slate unseals. Zero-Day reads it, and the color drains from the room.

ZERO-DAY: His name.

LEDGER LAWYER: Say it. It hasn't been said out loud in years.

ZERO-DAY: It's been erased from the ledger, the registry, the monument rolls, even the death records. But here — the original custody transfer. He existed.

GHOST WALKER: What's his name?

ZERO-DAY: (slowly) It doesn't match any name I know. But the custody clause lists him as *Bearer of the Key-Staff, First Cycle*.

LEDGER LAWYER: "First Cycle." You see it now, don't you? The corporation doesn't think of the city as a place with a history. It thinks of it as a *cycle*. And your predecessor was the bearer of the first one.

GHOST WALKER: First cycle. Implying there were others.

ZERO-DAY: Implying there's a *current* one. And the corporation's been resetting it.

The lawyer coughs — a wet, terrible sound — and grips the desk to steady himself.

LEDGER LAWYER: They don't just erase people. They erase *versions* of the city. And they keep one file so the next cycle has a template. You're looking at the template's old owner.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day holding the slate, the lawyer's gaunt face in the lantern light, Ghost Walker leaning over the screen.]

---

[SCENE 7 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Sprawl → the Arcology]

The wall of screens shows the Sprawl tightening — sweeps, checkpoints, a noose of drones. NEXUS COMMANDER stands at attention, command baton raised, two spotter drones whispering at his shoulder.

KAIROS: They found the lawyer.

NEXUS COMMANDER: I have his office pinned, Director. Give me the order and I'll take all four of them tonight.

KAIROS: No. You'll take the file first. Then them.

NEXUS COMMANDER: The file is a corpse's name. What does it matter if the rebels read it?

KAIROS: (turning, slowly) Because the name is a door, Commander. And behind that door is a version of the city that *must not be remembered*. If the NU11 open it, the harvest is ruined. Do you understand what "ruined" costs?

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then I'll burn the file and the lawyer with it.

KAIROS: You'll do exactly as you're told. Find them. But leave Zero-Day alive. He is not a rebel to be killed. He is an asset to be *replayed*.

Nexus Commander's certainty falters, just for a frame.

NEXUS COMMANDER: ...Replayed.

KAIROS: Same as last time, Commander. Now go.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's helm and the wall of screens, Nexus Commander's rigid silhouette, the two spotter drones hovering at his shoulder.]

---

[SCENE 8 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — CONTINUOUS]

Nexus Commander does not leave. He stands, baton half-lowered, and the question that has been growing in him since the first prediction card finally breaks the surface.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Director. Permission to speak plainly.

KAIROS: You've never needed permission to be plain, Commander. Say it.

NEXUS COMMANDER: You keep saying "same as last time." You keep filing reports before the battles. The men are starting to talk. They say you don't predict the rebels — you *remember* them.

KAIROS: And what do you say, Commander?

NEXUS COMMANDER: I say it doesn't matter how you know. What matters is that order prevails. But a soldier can follow a man into a firefight. He'll hesitate to follow a man into a *rehearsal*.

Kairos turns fully, and the clock-faced helm catches the light in a way that makes the room feel smaller. When he speaks, it is very quiet.

KAIROS: Then do not tell them it is a rehearsal. Tell them it is a war they are winning. That is what they need. That is what they have always needed. Now — go. The lawyer dies before dawn, and the file dies with him.

NEXUS COMMANDER: And Zero-Day?

KAIROS: (soft) Zero-Day does not die. He has never died. He is the one thing in this city that is not permitted to end.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's clock helm full in the light, Nexus Commander's shadow long across the command floor, the wall of screens pulsing behind.]

---

[SCENE 9 — INT. ABANDONED LAW OFFICE — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

The lawyer is fading fast. His breathing is shallow, his hands are shaking, and the pen has fallen from his fingers. But he pushes a final fragment across the desk — a photograph, paper, water-stained at the edges.

LEDGER LAWYER: Last file. The custody transfer's attached portrait. The only image of him that survived the purge.

ZERO-DAY: Why show me this?

LEDGER LAWYER: Because you need to see it before they come for it. Look.

Zero-Day lifts the photograph into the lantern light. Ghost Walker leans in. And both of them go still.

The man in the photograph is holding the Key-Staff. His posture, his hands, his coat — all of it is wrong in a way that makes the room shrink. Because the man in the photograph has a *hologram head*. A skull of light and running code, flickering in a frame that's decades old.

GHOST WALKER: Zero. That's you.

ZERO-DAY: (a whisper) No. That's him. That's the previous holder. And he has my head.

LEDGER LAWYER: (smiling, faintly) I told you. You're not hunting a dead man. You're hunting the shape of the thing you already are.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The water-stained photograph in Zero-Day's shaking hand — a hologram head that is his own.]

---

[SCENE 10 — INT. ABANDONED LAW OFFICE — CONTINUOUS]

The lawyer's breath rattles. Somewhere below, a checkpoint siren begins to rise — a low, building wail that means the corporation has found the office.

LEDGER LAWYER: They're coming. Take the photograph. Take the slate. And go.

GHOST WALKER: You'll die here.

LEDGER LAWYER: I was always going to die here. But I got to say the name one last time. That's more than the corporation ever meant me to have.

ZERO-DAY: (kneeling, close) The name. The previous holder. Does he have a face under the light? A face I'd know?

LEDGER LAWYER: He has your face. He's always had your face. The light, the code, the hands on the staff — it's you, boy. It's always been you, running the same war, in a different year.

The siren rises. Boots echo in the stairwell below. The lawyer closes his eyes and settles back in the chair, and for a moment his face relaxes into something almost peaceful.

LEDGER LAWYER: Now go. Before they file me too.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The dying lawyer at peace in the lantern light, the photograph clutched in Zero-Day's hand, red emergency light seeping up the stairwell.]

---

[SCENE 11 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — BACKSTREET — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: the office → the backstreet]

Zero-Day and Ghost Walker burst out into the backstreet as the first white-plated soldiers round the corner. A CHECKPOINT OFFICER levels a pulse rifle, but Ghost Walker is already moving — the neural spike arcs, a camera lens shatters, and the street plunges into a pocket of dark.

CHECKPOINT OFFICER: Stop! Municipal Security! Present your ledgers!

GHOST WALKER: (to Zero-Day, low) Drain grate. Now.

They drop through a grate into the flooded under-levels as the street above fills with light and shouting. The photograph is safe against Zero-Day's chest, and the slate, and the name. The water closes over them, cold and green, and the checkpoint above sounds its long, unanswered call.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Ghost Walker dropping through the grate, the checkpoint officer's light sweeping the street above, the water closing over their heads.]

---

[SCENE 12 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Back in the basement, the photograph is on the table, and the four of them stand around it in silence. Swarm Tech reaches out, then pulls his hand back, as if afraid the image might bite.

SWARM TECH: That's not possible. That's a fake. A composite. A trick of the light.

ZERO-DAY: It's paper, Swarm. From before the flood. They didn't have composites then. Just cameras.

SWARM TECH: Then the man in the picture really has—

ZERO-DAY: A hologram head. Like mine.

NEURAL BURN: The static's been trying to show me this all along. The man with the light for a head. I thought it was talking about *you*.

GHOST WALKER: It was. And it wasn't.

SWARM TECH: So what does it mean? That there was another Zero-Day? Another hacker with a skull of light and a staff that opens everything?

ZERO-DAY: It means the previous holder wasn't just some corporate ghost. He was *me*. Or something I was copied from. Or something I'm becoming.

NEURAL BURN: Or all three.

The room goes quiet, and the photograph seems to glow faintly in the green dark — the hologram head of a dead man, flickering in a frame that's decades old, staring up at a face that is its exact reflection.

ZERO-DAY: We don't stop here. The name is the door. The photograph is the keyhole. And somewhere out there is the rest of the story — who he was, and why the corporation killed him, and what it means that I'm still holding his staff.

SWARM TECH: And if it means you're him?

ZERO-DAY: (a long beat) Then I have a lot of questions for a dead man.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The photograph on the table, the four NU11 gathered around it, the hologram head reflected in Zero-Day's eyes.]

---

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The crew settles into the long watch. Swarm Tech is running the photograph through every scanner they own, trying to find a forgery that isn't there. Ghost Walker stands at the grates. Neural Burn sits in his screens, listening to the static, which tonight is almost — almost — coherent. Zero-Day holds the photograph up to the last of the light.

ZERO-DAY: (to the photo) You held the staff before me. You signed us into existence. And you have my head. What are you trying to tell me?

The photograph doesn't answer. But from across the room, Neural Burn speaks without opening his eyes.

NEURAL BURN: He's trying to tell you it's already happened. All of it. He's not your ancestor, Zero-Day. He's your *previous run*.

ZERO-DAY: A previous run of me.

NEURAL BURN: The static's been saying it since the beginning. The city doesn't have a history. It has a *replay*.

The words settle over the basement like cold water. Somewhere above, the grid's red lights drift past the grates, and the photograph of the previous holder — the first cycle, the first run — keeps staring up at the man who holds the same staff, the same head, the same unfinished war.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and the photograph, the grid's red lights passing the grates, the basement sinking into green shadow.]

---

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

Neural Burn sits with the photograph in his lap, jacked into the rig, his eyes closed. The static fills the room, and tonight, for the first time, it isn't noise. It's almost — almost — a voice, repeating something at the edge of hearing.

NEURAL BURN: The static's never done this. It's like it's trying to tell me his name. The previous holder's name. The one the corporation erased.

SWARM TECH: Can you make it out?

NEURAL BURN: It's under the noise. Buried. But it's there. The city knows his name, even if the ledger doesn't. It's just... refusing to say it out loud.

ZERO-DAY: The static remembers what the corporation scrubbed. That's why Kairos's people wiped every record — because the city itself never forgets. It just needs the right antenna.

NEURAL BURN: (opening his eyes) I'm the right antenna. Give me time. I'll pull his name out of the noise.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn with the photograph, the static resolving on the screens, the name half-formed at the edge of hearing.]

---

[SCENE 15 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

Zero-Day and Ghost Walker stand at the grates, looking up at the red drone-lights, and the plan is forming between them, quiet and dangerous.

ZERO-DAY: Whoever erased him did it with an order from the very top. That means the answer's in the corporation's own archive. Somewhere sealed. Somewhere even Kairos doesn't want opened.

GHOST WALKER: And we're going to open it.

ZERO-DAY: We're going to try. But first — the photograph. The staff. The name. I need to know what connects them. And there's only one way to find out.

GHOST WALKER: The staff.

ZERO-DAY: The staff. It held his memory once. If I push it hard enough, maybe it'll show me what it showed him.

GHOST WALKER: (a long look) That's how people get burned, Zero-Day. Look at Neural.

ZERO-DAY: I know. But I don't think I have a choice anymore.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Ghost Walker at the grates, the red drone-lights passing, the Key-Staff humming faint at Zero-Day's hip.]

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

The Basement. A flooded hacker-den below the drowned lower city. Stolen holo-screens throw cold light over cobbled backpack decks, dead corporate hardware stacked like offerings against the walls, and a thin skin of water on the floor that glows acid-green where a conduit leaks. Four figures are scattered around scavenged PCs, playing a pirated shooter, trash-talking over the hum of twenty fans and the drip of the city above them.

SWARM TECH: Come on, come on — no, don't flank me, I'm the flank! I am the entire flank!

ZERO-DAY: You're dead again.

SWARM TECH: I am tactically relocating. That's different from dead.

GHOST WALKER: It's the same screen. You're the red dot.

SWARM TECH: The red dot is a *decoy*. My real body is invisible, winning.

NEURAL BURN: The red dot is you.

SWARM TECH: Then the red dot is having a great time. You ever think about that? Everyone's so worried about the red dot's survival. Nobody asks how the red dot *feels*.

ZERO-DAY: The red dot feels like respawn timers.

Swarm Tech groans, slumps back in a scavenged chair, and spins a cracked holo-keyboard on one finger. The others barely look up. This is the rhythm of their nights — four people and one screen, laughing at a machine that can't laugh back. It is, by the standards of the drowned lower city, a good life.

GHOST WALKER: Rematch. Same map. I won't even shoot you this time.

SWARM TECH: You'll shoot me faster.

GHOST WALKER: Then dodge.

Zero-Day half-smiles, gentle, the way he always does. He is the quiet center of them — the one who holds the room together without ever seeming to try. His fingers move over a worn keyboard, coaxing the old rig back to life, and for a second the whole basement is just four friends and a game and the green light in the water.

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The banter thins out. Swarm Tech is digging through a crate of dead corporate parts, holding a mangled drone rotor up to the light. Ghost Walker is oiling a rail-pistol at the bench, patient, unhurried, the way he does everything. Neural Burn sits apart, at the edge of his halo of stolen holo-screens, jacked into a rig that hums against his skull.

SWARM TECH: Found another one. These Overwatch rotors — they're still warm. Someone up top is throwing out perfectly good kill-hardware.

ZERO-DAY: They throw out what they can't track. Everything the corporation makes gets a tracer baked in. You scrap the tracer, you keep the rotor.

SWARM TECH: Then explain why I've got a box of perfectly good rotors.

ZERO-DAY: Because you're very good at scrapping tracers, Swarm. It's your one talent.

SWARM TECH: My *one* talent?

GHOST WALKER: He's being kind. It's one of two.

NEURAL BURN: (without turning) The other one is dying on the red dot.

Swarm Tech throws a dead capacitor at Neural Burn. It misses, clatters into the water, and the water glows a little brighter where it lands. Nobody minds. This is home.

ZERO-DAY: Neural Burn's got ears today. What else do you hear?

NEURAL BURN: Static. It's always static. Tonight it's... different. Tastes like copper.

SWARM TECH: You said copper tastes like bad ramen.

NEURAL BURN: That's what I'm saying.

Neural Burn reaches up and adjusts one of the jacks at his temple. The skin there is pale and ridged with old burn-scar, and when his fingers touch the metal, a faint crackle runs along his trailing spinal cables. He is the only one of them who looks at the machines as if the machines are looking back.

ZERO-DAY: Copper's the grid. The Root's always humming it. You're just hearing the city breathe.

NEURAL BURN: The city doesn't breathe, Zero. It *waits*. And tonight it's waiting louder.

A flicker. Zero-Day's head — usually a face — blips for a half-frame into a flickering hologram skull of light and code, then snaps back. No one reacts. It's the kind of glitch the eye forgets, the kind you'd swear was a trick of the light from a dying screen.

ZERO-DAY: Then let's give it something quieter to listen to. Rematch. All four of us.

[SCENE 3 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day reaches for a worn keyboard — and the room's monitors die. Every screen in the basement goes black at once. Then white. Then a logo.

SWARM TECH: Hey. I didn't touch anything.

GHOST WALKER: Power surge.

ZERO-DAY: It's not a surge. Look.

The logo resolves: clean white plating, an electric-blue "T," spinning municipal seals. A Terable Corp emblem. It isn't coming from their rig — it's bleeding in from every direction, the signal leaking down through the grates, through the walls, through the water.

ZERO-DAY: ...That's not us.

NEURAL BURN: (eyes wide) Copper. It's copper everywhere now. The whole city's gone copper.

SWARM TECH: What does that mean, Neural?

NEURAL BURN: It means the waiting stopped.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Every holo-screen dies black then white as a Terable Corp logo bleeds in from the walls and water. All four NU11 frozen mid-action, faces lit by the rising white. Close on Zero-Day narrowing eyes. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 4 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

A Terable Corp emergency broadcast fills every screen. Clean white plating, an electric-blue "T," a calm synthetic voice over spinning municipal seals. Citywide — the signal comes from everywhere, not just their rig. The voice is so even, so practiced, it sounds almost kind.

BROADCAST VOICE: Citizens of the municipal district. The one-world currency is complete. As of this broadcast, all physical cash is void.

SWARM TECH: Turn it off.

ZERO-DAY: It's not ours to turn off. It's on every screen in the city.

SWARM TECH: Then smash the screens.

GHOST WALKER: And lose our only eyes. Think.

BROADCAST VOICE: Every citizen is issued a credit ledger, linked to biometric identity and a live location trail.

GHOST WALKER: Location trail.

NEURAL BURN: They want to know where we sleep.

BROADCAST VOICE: Simultaneously, the street-surveillance grid activates. Every Overwatch drone. Every corner lens. Every door sensor. For public safety and transactional integrity.

Zero-Day stands slowly, watching. Outside, through the grates high on the wall, the drowned street begins to change — a drone sweeps past, a red light, a distant siren. The world outside becoming a prison in real time.

SWARM TECH: That's it, then. No cash. No shadow. We just... became traceable.

ZERO-DAY: They didn't ask.

GHOST WALKER: They never ask.

The broadcast loops. The synthetic voice begins again from the top — the same words, the same seal, the same calm. Zero-Day freezes — not from shock. From recognition. His eyes narrow at something buried in the signal, something only he can see.

ZERO-DAY: Play it back.

SWARM TECH: What?

ZERO-DAY: The broadcast. Play it again.

[SCENE 5 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — MARKET STREET — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Sprawl, lower city]

The market is a chaos of corrugated stalls and black-market boards, strung with fairy-lights that run on stolen current. The broadcast is playing on every screen, every cracked pad, every salvaged billboard. People are already panicking — shoving, shouting, clutching fistfuls of paper that has suddenly stopped being money.

At a noodle stall, an old woman — the NOODLE VENDOR — holds a jar of coins up to the light as if the light could make them real again.

NOODLE VENDOR: I've been selling noodles on this corner for forty years. Forty years of coins. And now they tell me it's all nothing?

A KID, barefoot, dodges through the crowd and grabs the edge of her stall, breathless.

KID: Gram, the screens — the screens are saying the money's dead.

NOODLE VENDOR: I know what the screens are saying. I'm asking what *I'm* supposed to do about it.

KID: There's a man on the corner with a tablet. He's signing people up. He says if you don't register, you can't buy anything. Not even water.

NOODLE VENDOR: Water comes out of the ground, child. It's been coming out of the ground for forty years. The corporation doesn't own the rain.

KID: They own the pumps now, Gram.

Overhead, an Overwatch drone dips low over the street, its sensor-lens swiveling, scanning faces one by one. The crowd flinches in unison — a ripple of ducked heads and hunched shoulders, a whole street learning in one moment how to be watched.

NOODLE VENDOR: (looking up at the drone, very quietly) Get inside, child. And don't tell anyone where we keep the coins.

[SCENE 6 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — MARKET STREET — CONTINUOUS]

The panic has a rhythm now. A line is forming at the corner where the man with the tablet — a corporate REGISTRATION CLERK in a too-clean coat — is signing people up, one by one, tapping their faces and their thumbs into a ledger that glows electric-blue.

REGISTRATION CLERK: Next. Face the lens. Thumb here. Your ledger will be issued momentarily.

A DOCK WORKER steps up, hands still wet from the waterline, his face a mask of exhaustion.

DOCK WORKER: What happens if I don't sign up?

REGISTRATION CLERK: Then you don't eat, you don't travel, you don't power your home. The grid will not recognize you. It will be as if you do not exist.

DOCK WORKER: I've existed my whole life without your grid.

REGISTRATION CLERK: Not anymore, citizen. The old way is void. Step forward, or step aside.

The dock worker hesitates, his wet hands opening and closing. The drone overhead pauses, its lens settling on him. The lens is patient. It has all night. The dock worker steps forward and presses his thumb to the blue light, and something in his face goes out — a small, terrible surrender.

Across the street, the noodle vendor watches from the doorway of her stall, the coin jar clutched to her chest like a child. She does not get in line.

NOODLE VENDOR: (to no one) They didn't even ask. They never ask.

[SCENE 7 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Sprawl → the Basement]

Back below the waterline, the broadcast is still looping. Zero-Day hasn't taken his eyes off it. Swarm Tech is pacing; Ghost Walker is at the grates, watching the street above through the bars. Neural Burn sits very still, his cables crackling.

SWARM TECH: We should do something. People are out there signing away their names. We're just... watching.

GHOST WALKER: What would you do? Fight a broadcast?

SWARM TECH: I'd do *something*. You always say systems have edges. So find the edge.

ZERO-DAY: There's an edge. In the broadcast itself.

The others turn. Zero-Day is staring at the screen the way a man stares at a familiar face he can't place.

ZERO-DAY: Under the corporate seal. In the data layer. There's something buried in the signal. Something that doesn't belong to the corporation.

GHOST WALKER: Whose.

ZERO-DAY: That's what I need the closet for.

[GENERATE: Back in the flooded basement, continuous, the broadcast still looping. Zero-Day stares at the screen, Swarm Tech paces, Ghost Walker watches the street through the high grates, Neural Burn sits still with crackling cables. [SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — CONTINUOUS]

[ROOM CHANGE: main basement floor → server closet]

Zero-Day hauls a monitor into the cramped server closet — racks of scavenged boards, cables like roots, the air hot and close. The others crowd the door, shoulders touching, watching him work.

SWARM TECH: You're freaking me out, Z. You got that look.

ZERO-DAY: There's a signature in the stream. A glitch signature. Buried in the broadcast's data layer, under the corporate seal.

GHOST WALKER: Whose.

ZERO-DAY: That's what I'm saying. It's not corporate. It's... familiar.

He pulls up the raw signal, the broadcast rendered as a waterfall of code and frequency. He zooms into a single repeating byte pattern — a tell, the kind of fingerprint only a hacker leaves. His hands stop.

NEURAL BURN: Copper. It's copper again. Closer now.

ZERO-DAY: This pattern. The way it encrypts. The way it hides. This is my handwriting.

SWARM TECH: You wrote the broadcast?

ZERO-DAY: No. That's the problem. I never sent this. I never wrote this. But somebody built this signal using my code — my *specific* obfuscation, my compiler quirks, the fingerprint I leave in everything I touch.

GHOST WALKER: A copycat.

ZERO-DAY: You can't copy this. It's like a fingerprint in the code. This isn't a copy. It's me. Tonight. Before I wrote it.

[GENERATE: Cramped basement server closet, continuous, hot air and racks of scavenged boards, cables like roots. Zero-Day hunched over a monitor pulling up the broadcast raw signal, the crew packed in the doorway watching. Close on Zero-Day hands freezing over the keyboard. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The server closet — Zero-Day's face in the monitor glow, his signature on screen, the crew packed in the doorway.]

[SCENE 9 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — CONTINUOUS]

The words hang in the hot air. Swarm Tech opens his mouth, closes it, opens it again.

SWARM TECH: "Before you wrote it." Z, that doesn't make sense. You can't write something before you write it. That's just... writing it.

ZERO-DAY: I know what I wrote. I didn't write this. But the code says I did. The compiler quirks. The obfuscation. Every layer of this signal is mine, and I have never touched it.

GHOST WALKER: Someone's using your code.

ZERO-DAY: Someone's using my *hand*. My exact hand. Down to the way I nest my loops. Down to the way I sign my blocks. This is my work, and I was not here for it.

NEURAL BURN: The static knows your name, Zero-Day.

SWARM TECH: Neural, not now.

NEURAL BURN: It does. It's been saying it all night. Under the copper. A name in the noise.

Zero-Day turns from the screen. For a long beat he just looks at Neural Burn — not dismissive, not angry, but deeply, newly unsettled.

ZERO-DAY: Someone is using my code, tonight, before I've written it.

SWARM TECH: So we find out who.

GHOST WALKER: Or what.

ZERO-DAY: Or *when*.

The screens hum. Somewhere above, another drone passes the grate, and the water on the floor glows a little brighter, acid-green, like the city itself is listening.

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Arcology, upper city]

High above the flood. White glass, chrome, clean light. A wall of live data-screens shows the city's new nervous system switching on — a thousand Overwatch eyes blinking online one by one, sector by sector, a map of the city filling with green. DIRECTOR KAIROS stands before it, clock-faced helm catching the light, a spine of live data-screens along his back, the Temporal Staff in one hand. NEXUS COMMANDER waits at attention behind him.

KAIROS: How many eyes?

NEXUS COMMANDER: Full saturation in six minutes, Director. Every sector green.

KAIROS: And the lower city?

NEXUS COMMANDER: Resisting, as always. The usual noise. We'll have their names by morning.

KAIROS: You'll have their names sooner than that.

Nexus Commander hesitates, confused by the certainty. He is a precise man — a true believer — and precision hates being wrong about the schedule.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Sir?

Kairos doesn't turn. His helm's clock face ticks — not forward, but once, in a circle, and holds. He watches a single screen that shows, already, a file marked NULL ELEVEN with four names on it, timestamped for a night that hasn't finished yet.

KAIROS: I've already read the incident report of this battle, Commander. I wrote it.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Understood, Director.

KAIROS: Same as last time.

[GENERATE: High in the white Arcology, Kairos command at night, a wall of live data-screens igniting sector by sector. [SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — CONTINUOUS]

Nexus Commander shifts his weight. The question is on his face, and it wins.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Director — the file. NULL ELEVEN. It was dated before tonight's activation. I ran the logistics myself. Nobody filed those names. Nobody had them.

KAIROS: And yet there they are.

NEXUS COMMANDER: How can a file exist before the event it describes?

Kairos turns at last, slowly, the clock-face catching the green light of the map. For a long moment he simply regards the Commander — not with impatience, but with something like patience itself, the patience of a man who has answered this question a hundred times.

KAIROS: Because the event has happened before, Commander. Every event has happened before. The city runs on a loop, and the loop keeps excellent records.

NEXUS COMMANDER: A loop, sir?

KAIROS: You will understand soon enough. Or you will not, and it will not matter. What matters is that the four names in that file are not rebels yet. They are *about to be*. And they will be, on schedule, exactly as they were the last time.

The Commander's certainty wavers — just for a frame — and then hardens again, the way true belief does when it meets something it can't explain.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then I'll be ready for them, Director. On schedule.

KAIROS: I know you will. You always are.

[SCENE 12 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

[TIME: later that night]

The four sit in the dark now, only the monitors alive. Zero-Day hasn't moved. He's replaying the broadcast in a loop, hunting the signature, growing quieter and quieter. The others are arranged around him in a loose, worried arc.

SWARM TECH: So what do we do? Pretend it didn't happen?

GHOST WALKER: We find out who.

NEURAL BURN: It's not who. It's when.

SWARM TECH: That doesn't make sense, Neural.

NEURAL BURN: It hasn't made sense yet. But the voice is getting clearer.

ZERO-DAY: I'm going to pull the stream apart. All of it. If my fingerprint is in the broadcast, there'll be more of it — in the ledgers, in the drone registry, in the grid's boot files.

GHOST WALKER: That means touching the Root.

ZERO-DAY: Yes.

SWARM TECH: The Root? The *Root* Root? Day one of the surveillance state and you want to reach into the city's nervous system?

ZERO-DAY: I opened every door once. For eleven seconds. I can do it again.

The room goes quiet. It's an old story, that one — the founding myth, the eleven seconds, the night the city almost came free. Zero-Day says it like a prayer now, and for a moment all four of them believe it again.

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — LATER]

[ROOM CHANGE: main floor → server closet]

The others have gone to their corners to prep. Zero-Day works alone in the closet, tearing the broadcast's data layer apart, line by line. The signature is everywhere now — in the handshake, in the padding, in the way the signal folds back on itself. It is his work, unmistakably his work, and every time he confirms it, the room gets a little smaller.

He looks up from the screen, and for a beat his face blips to the hologram — a skull of light, code running like tears — and holds a half-second too long before snapping back.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) Something's wrong. Not with the city. With the broadcast. It knew to use my code before I did.

He stops. Stares at his own reflection in the dead monitor — the ordinary face, the flicker of light that doesn't quite match the room.

ZERO-DAY: That means somebody out there has already seen tonight. Or I've lived it already and don't remember.

From the doorway, without turning on the light, Neural Burn's voice comes soft through the dark.

NEURAL BURN: The static remembers.

ZERO-DAY: Remembers what?

NEURAL BURN: Everything. Including the part you forgot. Including the part where tonight already happened.

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

Zero-Day comes back out to the main floor. Swarm Tech is checking his drone pack, tightening straps. Ghost Walker is loading the rail-pistol with slow, deliberate care. The basement, for all its warmth, already feels like a staging ground — four people getting ready to go to war with a signal.

SWARM TECH: If we're doing this — the Root, the ledgers, all of it — we do it together.

GHOST WALKER: Or we don't do it at all.

NEURAL BURN: We were always going to do it. The static's been counting down to it.

ZERO-DAY: Then we start at the beginning. We find every fingerprint of mine that isn't mine. And we follow it back to whoever — or whatever — is running this city from inside my own code.

SWARM TECH: And if it turns out to be you?

The question lands, and for a second nobody answers. Zero-Day looks at Swarm Tech, then at Ghost Walker, then at Neural Burn, and his face is very calm — too calm.

ZERO-DAY: Then I'll be the first thing we have to stop.

He holds up a hand, and the hologram skull blips across his face one more time — a full, clean half-second of light and running code — before snapping back to the ordinary face they all know. This time, they all see it. This time, no one pretends they didn't.

ZERO-DAY: Something's wrong with me. And something's wrong with the city. And I don't think they're two different problems.

[SCENE 15 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The four stand together in the green half-dark, and for a moment the whole thing feels almost like the start of the game again — four people and one screen, about to do something reckless together.

SWARM TECH: So. Same map?

GHOST WALKER: Same map.

NEURAL BURN: It's always the same map.

ZERO-DAY: Then let's go win it. Or at least make it expensive.

He reaches for the Key-Staff — the one key that opens the whole network — and it hums to life in his fist, throwing a long green shadow across the water. Above them, through the grates, the first Overwatch drones of the new grid drift past in the dark, their lenses red, their lights already trained on the city below.

The war for the Root has begun. And somewhere, buried in the broadcast, a signature that shouldn't exist yet is still glowing — a handprint, pressed into a signal no one sent.

SWARM TECH: (quiet, as they move to the door) For eleven seconds.

ZERO-DAY: For eleven seconds.

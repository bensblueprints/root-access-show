[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — DAY]

The mood is worse. The grid has fully hardened; below, the Sprawl moves under a ceiling of drones. Around the table, the four argue over a holo-schematic of a Root Terminal — a hardened node sunk into the flooded under-levels, its shape picked out in cold green lines.

SWARM TECH: A Root Terminal? Those are the corporation's nerve endings. You don't *break into* one. You get disassembled near one.

ZERO-DAY: We need the conversion ledger. The real one. Who got a credit line, who got flagged, and *when*. If the system knew my code before I wrote it, the ledger will say how.

GHOST WALKER: And if it knew our names too?

ZERO-DAY: Then we'll know we're not hunting a leak. We're hunting a script.

NEURAL BURN: The static says we should look. It says the answer's already written down, waiting for us.

SWARM TECH: The static's not a source, Neural.

NEURAL BURN: It's more reliable than anyone in this room right now.

The line lands. Nobody answers it. For a beat, the only sound is the hum of the rig and the slow drip of the city above.

ZERO-DAY: I'll pull the fragment. Ghost walks point. Swarm covers our exit with drones. Neural runs the decrypt from here.

GHOST WALKER: If the grid predicts us, I want a dead drop, not a rescue.

ZERO-DAY: Fine. Dead drop.

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Swarm Tech is not done arguing. He circles the schematic, jabbing at it, his antenna crown catching the light.

SWARM TECH: I don't like it. The terminal's under forty feet of floodwater. You get down there, you're in the corporation's *basement*. Every route out is a bottleneck.

ZERO-DAY: There's a service shaft on the east side. Old maintenance access. The grid doesn't watch it — it's been dark since before the flood.

SWARM TECH: How do you know what the grid does and doesn't watch?

ZERO-DAY: Because I mapped it. Before the conversion. The terminal's been in my head since I was a kid.

GHOST WALKER: You've been down there.

ZERO-DAY: I've been *everywhere* down there. The under-levels used to be where I went when I needed to disappear.

NEURAL BURN: The static says the terminal is unguarded. It says it's *always* unguarded. Like it wants us to come.

SWARM TECH: See, that's the part that makes me want to stay home.

ZERO-DAY: That's the part that tells me we're on the right track. A terminal this important, left empty, every time. That's not negligence. That's an invitation.

The four exchange a look — the look of people who have stopped asking whether it's a trap and started asking what the trap is for.

GHOST WALKER: Then we spring it. Carefully.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Swarm Tech circles the schematic jabbing at it, antenna crown catching light; Zero-Day finger rests on a service shaft. Medium shot of the four exchanging a wary look. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — FLOODED UNDER-LEVEL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the flooded under-levels]

The water is black and cold and alive with reflected neon. Zero-Day and Ghost Walker move through it in a small skiff, a single dim lamp burning at the bow. The under-levels open around them — drowned arcades, half-sunk storefronts, a submerged stairwell vanishing into the dark. The terminal's cold light glows ahead, a blue beacon in the water.

GHOST WALKER: It's quiet down here.

ZERO-DAY: Too quiet. The grid's eyes don't reach this far. Nothing does, except the water.

A SCAVENGER — a girl in patched waders, a salvaged rebreather hanging from her neck — drifts out of a side-passage on a half-inflated raft, a hook-lamp in one hand. She raises the lamp, and its beam catches Zero-Day's face.

SCAVENGER: You're going to the terminal.

ZERO-DAY: Maybe.

SCAVENGER: Don't. Nobody goes down there. Not since the water came.

GHOST WALKER: Then why are you here?

SCAVENGER: I salvage. Bits of the old city that float up. Coins. Cables. The terminal's the one place I don't touch.

ZERO-DAY: Why not?

SCAVENGER: Because it's never guarded. And in this city, when a thing's never guarded, it's because it's already watching you.

She lets the raft drift backward into the dark, her lamp swinging, and her voice comes back once more, thin over the water.

SCAVENGER: Whatever you're looking for down there — it's been waiting for you to find it.

[SCENE 4 — INT. ROOT TERMINAL — UNDER-LEVEL — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: flooded under-level → inside the terminal]

The terminal breathes in the dark — a tower of cold light sunk into black water, cables dripping like roots into the flood. Zero-Day wades in, Key-Staff ready. Ghost Walker moves ahead, poncho eating the reflections, neural spike glinting. The air is still. The only sound is the water and the slow, patient hum of the machine.

GHOST WALKER: Clear to the core. No drones. No lenses.

ZERO-DAY: That's not reassuring. A terminal this deep should be guarded.

GHOST WALKER: Maybe it isn't, because they don't need to.

ZERO-DAY: Or because they want us to get comfortable.

Zero-Day plugs the Key-Staff into the terminal's data spine. The tower flares, and for a second the whole under-level lights up like a drowned cathedral. Light pours down the cables, up the flooded walls, and the water around them turns to liquid silver.

ZERO-DAY: I'm in. Pulling the ledger now.

GHOST WALKER: Make it fast. That light's going to draw every lens for a mile.

ZERO-DAY: Then hold the door.

[GENERATE: Inside the Root Terminal, a tower of cold light sunk into black water, cables dripping like roots. Zero-Day plugs the Key-Staff into the data spine and the under-level flares like a drowned cathedral. Wide shot as light floods the water. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day at the terminal core, Key-Staff blazing, the flooded under-level lit like a cathedral, Ghost Walker at the door in shadow.]

[SCENE 5 — INT. ROOT TERMINAL — CONTINUOUS]

The data pours — millions of names, credit lines, biometric hashes, timestamps. Zero-Day's fingers fly, filtering, searching. The ledger is vast, and it is not just a record. It is a *shape* — a map of every human being the city has ever catalogued, cross-referenced against their movements, their debts, their allegiances.

ZERO-DAY: It's not just who's registered. It's who was flagged *before* they registered. There's a layer under the ledger. A pre-registry.

GHOST WALKER: Pre-registry of what?

ZERO-DAY: People the system knew about before the system existed. Names filed before the conversion. Before the broadcast.

He filters deeper, and the data thins — fewer names, older timestamps, a cold, foreboding subsection marked with a single tag.

ZERO-DAY: Filtering for pre-broadcast entries... there. A fragment. It's small. It's—

He stops. His hands freeze over the keys. The light from the core plays over his face, and his face is wrong — pale, hollowed, like a man seeing his own name on a tombstone.

ZERO-DAY: Ghost. Come here.

[GENERATE: Same terminal, continuous. Zero-Day fingers fly as millions of names pour past, his face going pale and hollowed as he filters deeper. Close on Zero-Day face in the core-light. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day frozen at the terminal, the core-light on his face, Ghost Walker turning from the door.]

[SCENE 6 — INT. ROOT TERMINAL — CONTINUOUS]

Ghost Walker crosses the flooded floor, boots sending ripples through the silver light. He looks at the screen. For a long moment, neither of them speaks.

GHOST WALKER: That's a mistake.

ZERO-DAY: It's not a mistake. Look at the timestamp.

GHOST WALKER: I'm looking.

ZERO-DAY: It's weeks before the broadcast. Before the conversion was announced. Before we were anything.

GHOST WALKER: Before we chose the name.

ZERO-DAY: Before the name existed.

Outside, something changes — a soft chime, the same too-calm chime from the Drone Control Hub. The terminal's lights flicker once, and the data flow stutters, then resumes, as if the machine had briefly looked up from its work to acknowledge them.

ZERO-DAY: It knows we're here. It always knew.

GHOST WALKER: Then we take what we came for and go.

ZERO-DAY: I've got the fragment. Let's move.

He yanks the Key-Staff free, and the cathedral light collapses, plunging them back into the blue dark. They wade for the exit, the fragment burning on a data-slate against Zero-Day's chest, and behind them the terminal hums its patient, indifferent song.

[GENERATE: Same terminal, continuous. Ghost Walker wades over, ripples through the silver light, and the two stare at the screen as a soft chime sounds and the lights flicker. Over-the-shoulder two-shot of them at the screen. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Ghost Walker retreating through the dark water, the terminal's blue glow receding, the fragment pulsing at Zero-Day's chest.]

[SCENE 7 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

The fragment is up on every screen. Four names. The NU11's own — not handles, but the names underneath them. Each is marked as an INSURGENT, pre-registered, with a timestamp that precedes the emergency broadcast by weeks. The four stand in a loose, frozen circle, and no one can look away.

SWARM TECH: That's... that's us. That's *us*.

GHOST WALKER: Dated before the broadcast. Before the conversion was even announced.

ZERO-DAY: Someone filed these names as insurgents before we ever picked up a gun or a keyboard. Before the grid. Before the credits.

NEURAL BURN: Before we chose.

SWARM TECH: What do you mean, "before we chose"?

NEURAL BURN: Look at the dates. We didn't exist as NU11 when these were filed. Zero-Day didn't have the Key-Staff. I hadn't been burned yet.

GHOST WALKER: Then someone predicted us down to our names.

ZERO-DAY: No. Someone *pre-registered* us. Like booking a room for guests who haven't arrived.

The room goes cold. The four glance at each other, and for the first time, there's distance in it — a new, wary space between people who have shared everything and now find themselves wondering.

[GENERATE: Basement, later that night, the fragment on every screen showing four pre-registered names. The four NU11 stand in a frozen circle, a new wary distance between them. Close on the names, then the four faces lit and suspicious. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four names glowing on the screens, the crew's faces lit and suspicious, the distance between them already growing.]

[SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

SWARM TECH: How does a system know our names before we have names? Somebody in this room—

GHOST WALKER: Careful.

SWARM TECH: —somebody talked to them. Or somebody's been on the inside all along.

ZERO-DAY: It's not a leak, Swarm.

SWARM TECH: How do you know?

ZERO-DAY: Because a leak requires a source. And these names — these *exact* names — didn't exist to leak. Not before the flood.

NEURAL BURN: The static says it's older than a leak. It says the names were always there.

SWARM TECH: What does that even mean, Neural? "Always there"? We're people. We weren't always anything. We were born, we grew up, we—

He stops. The words die in his throat, because the others are looking at him, and for a terrible second none of them can quite remember — clearly, completely — how they came to be here. It's a small thing, a flicker, gone almost as soon as it arrives. But they all feel it.

GHOST WALKER: Do any of us remember before the flood?

NEURAL BURN: No. None of us do. We never have.

ZERO-DAY: (very quiet) And we never noticed.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Swarm Tech accusation lands, and the four fall into a flicker of shared amnesia, each suddenly alone. Wide shot of the four standing apart in the green light. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four standing apart, the fragment still glowing, each of them suddenly alone in the green light.]

[SCENE 9 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The trust cracks in real time. Swarm Tech paces. Ghost Walker stands still, too still. Zero-Day reads and re-reads the fragment. Neural Burn watches them all, the cables at his skull twitching.

SWARM TECH: I want to know how my name got into their system before I ever set foot in it. I want to know who put it there.

GHOST WALKER: We all got put there. All four. Same hand.

SWARM TECH: Then one of us is that hand.

ZERO-DAY: No. Look at the file. The author signature isn't any of us.

SWARM TECH: It could be forged.

ZERO-DAY: It's encrypted with a key that *predates* the Key-Staff. It's older than what I carry. Nobody in this room could write that.

GHOST WALKER: Then who.

ZERO-DAY: That's what I'm decrypting. Give me the room.

Swarm Tech stops pacing. He faces Zero-Day, and there's something raw in his voice now — the sound of a believer who has just felt the first crack in the story he tells himself.

SWARM TECH: You want the room? Fine. But while you're in there pulling that signature apart, I'll be out here asking the only question that matters: if the system knew us before we existed, then what the hell *are* we?

The others fall silent. The silence is heavy — not the easy quiet of old friends, but the watchful quiet of people beginning to count each other's exits.

NEURAL BURN: (low, to no one) It was always going to be four names. There were always going to be four.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous, trust cracking in real time. Swarm Tech paces, Ghost Walker stands too still, Zero-Day reads the fragment, Neural Burn cables twitch. Slow push across the four, distance growing. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four scattered now across the room, distance between them, the fragment still glowing, Swarm Tech's face caught mid-question.]

[SCENE 10 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: main floor → server closet]

Zero-Day works alone in the closet, the Key-Staff laid across his knees, the fragment's author signature unfolding under his tools. Hours pass in the glow. He decrypts layer after layer — corporate encryption, then something older underneath, then something older still. It is patient work, and every layer he peels away makes the room feel smaller.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) Not corporate. Not a department. Not a badge. It's... a hand I don't recognize. A hand from before.

He pauses, hands hovering. The signature is resolving now, and it is not a number, not a code. It is a *name* — written in the oldest key the system carries, the key that predates everything.

ZERO-DAY: (barely audible) That's not corporate.

He calls the others in. They crowd the door again, and this time the distrust is gone, replaced by something worse — a shared, sinking certainty.

SWARM TECH: Whose name is that?

ZERO-DAY: It's not an employee. There's no ledger line, no badge. But the encryption key matches the Key-Staff's *previous* signature. The one from before me.

GHOST WALKER: The previous holder.

ZERO-DAY: The person who held the Key-Staff before I did. The one I took it from — or was given it by. I don't remember which.

NEURAL BURN: He's dead.

ZERO-DAY: He's supposed to be. This file says he pre-registered the four of us. Weeks before the conversion. A dead man filled out our paperwork.

[GENERATE: Server closet, night, Zero-Day alone decrypting the fragment author signature, the Key-Staff across his knees, hours passing in the glow. Close on the resolving fifth name on screen. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's face in the closet glow, the fifth name on screen above the Key-Staff, the crew packed in the doorway.]

[SCENE 11 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Back on the main floor, the four try to make sense of it, and can't. The name sits on every screen now — the fifth name, the ghost's name, the man who signed their existence into being before they existed.

SWARM TECH: So either he's not dead—

ZERO-DAY: —or the system filed us under a ghost's name, and the ghost has been running this whole thing from the start.

GHOST WALKER: A dead man pre-registered four rebels. That's not a prediction. That's a *design*.

NEURAL BURN: The static's been trying to tell us. We're not an accident. We're not even a revolution. We're a *schedule*.

SWARM TECH: I don't accept that. I refuse. We're the good guys. We chose this.

ZERO-DAY: Did we? We don't remember before the flood. We don't remember how we met. We don't remember choosing any of it. Just... waking up already at war.

SWARM TECH: That's not true. I remember the first night in the basement. The four of us, the scavenged rig, the first time we touched the Root.

ZERO-DAY: Do you remember *before* that? The day before? The week?

Swarm Tech opens his mouth, and nothing comes. He closes it. The answer, whatever it is, is not there.

GHOST WALKER: (quiet) None of us do.

ZERO-DAY: Because there may not have been a before. For any of us.

[GENERATE: Basement main floor, continuous, the fifth name on every screen. The four try to make sense of it, Swarm Tech face going slack as he realizes none of them remember before the flood. Close on Swarm Tech open, wordless mouth. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four in the aftermath, Swarm Tech's face gone slack, the fifth name glowing on every screen around them.]

[SCENE 12 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The silence stretches. Then Neural Burn speaks, and his voice is steady in a way that makes the others go still.

NEURAL BURN: The previous holder. He's the key to all of it. If he pre-registered us, he knew what we'd become. And if he's dead, someone knows why.

GHOST WALKER: Then we find him. Or what's left of him.

ZERO-DAY: The corporation wiped him from every record but this one. That takes an order, not an accident. Somebody at the top wanted him erased.

SWARM TECH: Who erases a dead man?

GHOST WALKER: Someone who needs him to stay dead.

ZERO-DAY: Or someone who's afraid of what happens if we find him first.

He reaches for the Key-Staff, and it hums in his grip, brighter than before — as if the name on the screen has woken something in it. The green light spills over the four of them, and for a moment their shadows stretch huge and strange against the flooded walls.

ZERO-DAY: Tomorrow we hunt the dead man. And whatever we find — whatever he was — it's the beginning of the truth. The real one. Not the story we've been telling ourselves.

NEURAL BURN: The static says the truth is going to cost us.

ZERO-DAY: It already has.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Neural Burn speaks with terrible steadiness, and Zero-Day reaches for the Key-Staff, green light spilling over the four, shadows stretching huge. Wide shot of the four in the green spill. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four in the green spill of the Key-Staff, shadows huge on the walls, the fifth name still burning on the screens.]

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The crew has drifted to their corners, but no one is sleeping. Swarm Tech sits with a drone half-assembled in his lap, not working on it. Ghost Walker stands at the grates, watching the red drone-lights drift past. Neural Burn is back in his ring of screens, listening. Zero-Day stares at the fifth name, the author signature, the oldest key in the system.

ZERO-DAY: (to the name, soft) Who were you? You held this before me. You signed us into being. And then you let them erase you.

The name doesn't answer. But the Key-Staff hums, a low, resonant note, and for a half-second the closet fills with a flicker of light that isn't Zero-Day's — an after-image, a shape, gone before it forms.

SWARM TECH: (from across the room) Z. If we were pre-registered... then maybe the whole war's been pre-registered too. Every move. Every loss.

ZERO-DAY: Maybe.

SWARM TECH: Then how do we win?

ZERO-DAY: We find the man who wrote the schedule. And we ask him why he wrote us into it.

GHOST WALKER: He's dead.

ZERO-DAY: So are we, apparently. We just haven't caught up to it yet.

The red lights pass the grates, one by one, and the basement settles into the long, watchful dark.

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — LATER]

[ROOM CHANGE: main floor → server closet]

Zero-Day works into the small hours, feeding the fifth name into every fragment of data they've pulled — the ledger, the prediction logs, the old broadcasts' timestamps. The name matches nothing in the living grid. But in the oldest layers, the preserved city, it appears again and again — not as a person, but as a *first entry*, the very first line in the oldest ledger the Root still carries.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) He's not just a previous holder. He's the first entry. The first name in the system. The Root's ledgers don't begin with the conversion. They begin with *him*.

He pulls the entry up, and it is ancient, faded, barely legible — a name, a date, and a single annotation in a hand that is somehow, impossibly, his own.

ZERO-DAY: "Bearer of the Key-Staff, First Cycle." Written before the flood. Before the corporation. Before everything. And signed... in my handwriting.

The hologram skull flickers once, quick, across his reflection in the dead screen, and then it's gone, and the closet is just a closet, and the name is just a name, and none of it adds up.

ZERO-DAY: Who signs a ledger a generation before he exists? Unless he's been here before. Unless *I've* been here before.

[GENERATE: Server closet, later, Zero-Day working into the small hours, the ancient first entry of the ledger on screen, signed in his own hand. Close on his reflection in the dead screen, a hologram skull flickering once. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

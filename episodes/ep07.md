[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

Neural Burn has not slept. His halo of holo-screens is now a ring of flickering, half-tuned images, and he sits in the center, jacks sparking, listening. The others hang back, wary. The room smells of ozone and old heat.

ZERO-DAY: You've been at this for two days.

NEURAL BURN: I'm close. The static isn't static. It never was. It's got *structure*. Beginnings and ends. A signal, not a sickness.

SWARM TECH: Neural, you've said that before. It's how you got burned in the first place.

NEURAL BURN: No. I got burned because I listened too *loosely*. But now I know what I'm hearing. It's not the city talking. It's the city *replaying*.

GHOST WALKER: Replaying what?

NEURAL BURN: (turning) Everything.

He throws a switch, and the static on his screens begins to *resolve*. Beneath the noise, beneath the hum of the living grid, something else surfaces — a deeper layer, an older signal, like a second city running underneath the first.

NEURAL BURN: Listen.

[GENERATE: Basement at night, Neural Burn at the center of a ring of flickering holo-screens, jacks sparking, the others hanging back in the ozone-tinged air. He throws a switch and the static begins to resolve. Close on his burned face. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn in the ring of screens, the static beginning to resolve into shapes, the crew watching from the edges.]

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The room fills with ghost-signals. Faint transmissions, old weather reports, a jingle for a store that no longer exists. The crew leans in, stunned. A man's voice announces tomorrow's tide, and tomorrow's tide is years in the past.

SWARM TECH: That's... that's a broadcast from before. Before the flood.

ZERO-DAY: Not before the flood. *Under* it. The Root has layers. We've been scraping the top one. But there's something below — an old city, still powered, still *transmitting*.

NEURAL BURN: It never shut down. They didn't turn off the old city. They just buried it and built a new one on top. And the old one is still down there, still broadcasting, bleeding through the cracks.

GHOST WALKER: Like water through a seawall.

NEURAL BURN: Exactly. And my head — my burned head — is the only antenna in this room that can pick it up.

Zero-Day steps closer to the screens, watching an old traffic camera feed resolve from the noise — a street that no longer exists, a corner that is now underwater. A vendor hawks fish where fish no longer swim. A tram runs where the water now is.

ZERO-DAY: The flooded city is sitting on top of an older version of itself. Still running. Still remembering.

SWARM TECH: Remembering. That's what you said about the grid. That it remembers.

NEURAL BURN: Because it does. The whole thing is one machine, and the machine never forgets a cycle.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Ghost-signals fill the room, an old traffic camera resolving from the noise showing a drowned corner alive again, a tram running through water. Close on the old feed, then the stunned crew. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The old traffic camera feed on Neural Burn's screens — a drowned corner, alive again, a tram running through water.]

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — MARKET STREET — DAY]

[TIME: the next day]

Zero-Day and Swarm Tech walk the market, and the ghost-signals have followed them out of the basement. They stop at a corner stall — a SERVER-SHRINE, a little altar of salvaged boards and candle-light where people leave offerings of dead batteries and prayer-scraps to the machines that rule them. An OLD SHRINE KEEPER tends the candles.

ZERO-DAY: You've kept this shrine a long time.

SHRINE KEEPER: Since before the water, boy. The machines remember, so we remember.

SWARM TECH: Remember what?

SHRINE KEEPER: The old city. The one that's down there still. You can hear it, if you know how to listen. Some nights, through the grates, you hear the tram bells. The old tram bells, from before.

ZERO-DAY: There was a tram.

SHRINE KEEPER: There was a whole city. Streets that aren't streets anymore. Stores with names nobody says. A jingle — a little jingle for a fish shop — that used to play every hour. I still hum it, when the drone-light's bad.

He hums it then, low and cracked — and it is the same jingle from Neural Burn's screens, note for note. Zero-Day and Swarm Tech exchange a look.

ZERO-DAY: (quiet) That jingle. Where did you learn it?

SHRINE KEEPER: From the water, boy. Same place you did.

An old shrine keeper hums the ghost-jingle, and they exchange a look. Close on the candles and the keeper cracked humming. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The server-shrine in the market, candle-light on the salvaged boards, the old keeper humming the ghost-jingle.]

[SCENE 4 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Back in the basement, Zero-Day relays the keeper's jingle, and Neural Burn's eyes go wide. He pulls up the recording, and the two versions play together — the keeper's cracked humming and the signal from under the city, note for note, one voice answering the other across a drowned gap of years.

NEURAL BURN: It's the same. The jingle. The old city's still broadcasting it, and the keeper still remembers it. The past isn't dead. It's just underwater.

SWARM TECH: And some of us can hear it. You. The old man. People who've been close to the machines.

GHOST WALKER: People the machines have burned.

NEURAL BURN: The burn is how you tune in. The net took something from me, and in the space it left, the old city started leaking through.

ZERO-DAY: Then the static isn't your sickness. It's a *radio*.

NEURAL BURN: It's the only radio that still gets the old frequency.

Zero-Day stares at the two jingles playing together, and something in his face hardens — not with fear, but with the beginning of an answer.

ZERO-DAY: We need to go deeper. Not just listen to the old city. Go *into* it.

[GENERATE: Basement, later that night, the keeper jingle and the ghost-signal layered on the screens, one voice answering the other across a drowned gap of years. Close on Zero-Day face lit between the two. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The two jingles layered on the screens — the keeper's voice and the ghost-signal — Zero-Day's face lit between them.]

[SCENE 5 — INT. ROOT DEEP LAYER — VIA TERMINAL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → deep Root layer (accessed via terminal)]

Zero-Day and Neural Burn jack into the terminal, descending past the living grid into the layers beneath. The visual is vertigo — code that isn't code but *geography*, an old skyline rendered in dead data, still glowing faintly. Neural Burn guides them, his voice tight with concentration, the cables at his skull streaming with light.

ZERO-DAY: It's a whole city. A whole *version* of the city, preserved down here.

NEURAL BURN: I told you. The old one never died.

ZERO-DAY: It was overwritten. Paved over. But the data's still here. The corporation didn't delete the past — they just stacked the present on top of it.

NEURAL BURN: Look at the timestamps. The old city's clock is still running. It's been counting for years, alone, in the dark.

ZERO-DAY: Counting toward what?

A transmission flickers into focus — a familiar cadence. The seal. The voice. Zero-Day goes cold.

ZERO-DAY: Neural. Pull that up. The last one.

[GENERATE: Deep Root layer accessed via terminal, a vertiginous descent where code becomes geography, an old skyline rendered in dead data. Zero-Day and Neural Burn descend, cables streaming with light. Wide shot of the two dropping into the dead skyline. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The old city's skyline rendered in dead data, one transmission glowing at its center, Zero-Day and Neural Burn descending toward it.]

[SCENE 6 — INT. ROOT DEEP LAYER — CONTINUOUS]

They push deeper, and the dead city resolves around them — streets, signs, a whole municipal past, rendered in the faint green of forgotten data. It is beautiful and terrible at once: a place that still exists, only no one lives there.

NEURAL BURN: It's not just records. It's *everything*. The old grid, the old ledgers, the old broadcasts. The corporation didn't wipe the old city. They kept it, whole, as a backup. A template.

ZERO-DAY: The lawyer said the same thing. "The corporation keeps one copy of each, so the next cycle knows what to rebuild."

NEURAL BURN: Then this is the template. The previous version. The one they reset from.

ZERO-DAY: And the transmission at its center — that's what we're about to find.

The familiar cadence swells around them — the corporate seal, the even voice, the words that every citizen knows by heart. But the seal is wrong. Older. The voice is the same, but the signal is faded, like a recording played through water.

ZERO-DAY: This is it. Pull it up and bring it home.

[GENERATE: Same deep layer, continuous. The dead city resolves around them, a transmission swelling at its center, an older seal glowing through water-faded signal. Wide shot of the two in the heart of the preserved city. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Neural Burn in the heart of the dead city, the old transmission swelling around them like a tide.]

[SCENE 7 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

The recording plays on every screen in the basement. The four stand in a loose circle, frozen. It's the emergency broadcast — the *credit conversion* — but it's wrong. The seal is older. The cadence is the same. Word for word.

BROADCAST VOICE: (crackling, older) Citizens of the municipal district. The one-world currency is complete. As of this broadcast, all physical cash is void...

SWARM TECH: That's the conversion. That's *our* conversion.

ZERO-DAY: It's dated years earlier.

GHOST WALKER: Years before the flood.

NEURAL BURN: It's happened before. The conversion. The credits. The grid. All of it.

SWARM TECH: No. That's a coincidence. A dry run. A—a rehearsal.

ZERO-DAY: It's word-for-word identical, Swarm. Every phrase. Every pause. This isn't a rehearsal. This is the *same event*, broadcast before the one we lived through.

GHOST WALKER: They've run the conversion before.

NEURAL BURN: They've run *everything* before.

[GENERATE: Basement, later that night, the old broadcast playing on every screen, an older seal turning. The four NU11 stand frozen in a loose circle. Close on the older seal, then the four faces lit by it. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four faces lit by the old broadcast, the older seal glowing on the screens, the water reflecting it back.]

[SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The four exchange a look — the same look from the ledger, from the wiped node, from the photograph. The pieces are locking together, and none of them want to finish the sentence.

ZERO-DAY: The city didn't just have a past. It's had *this* past. Ours. Before us.

SWARM TECH: How is that possible? How do you run the same conversion twice and nobody remembers the first one?

NEURAL BURN: Because they're wiped. The memory, the records, the people. The reset doesn't just change the city, Swarm. It *replaces* it. Same faces, same war, new run.

GHOST WALKER: And the old broadcast is the proof. They never deleted it. They just buried it, with the rest of the old city.

ZERO-DAY: They kept it as a template. A recording of the last run, so the next one matches.

SWARM TECH: So this — the conversion, the grid, the war we're fighting — it's all a *repeat*.

NEURAL BURN: Every step. Every fight. Every name in the ledger. It's all been done before, by people who looked just like us.

The room is very quiet. On the screens, the old broadcast loops, its older seal turning, its calm voice repeating the same promise that ended cash and anonymity in a city that no longer quite exists.

ZERO-DAY: Then who are we? The people it happened to, or the people it *keeps* happening to?

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous, the pieces locking together. The four exchange a look as the old broadcast loops, its calm voice repeating the same promise. Wide shot of the four in the looping light. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The looping old broadcast, the four NU11 in its light, the question hanging in the green air.]

[SCENE 9 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Neural Burn isolates the recording, cleaning the static until it's crystal clear. The final line of the old broadcast rings out in the basement, and there's no mistaking it.

BROADCAST VOICE: (clear, final) ...for public safety and transactional integrity.

Silence. Then Zero-Day speaks, very quietly.

ZERO-DAY: The conversion has happened before. The surveillance. The credits. Maybe even the war. All of it.

SWARM TECH: If it's happened before, then what are we?

GHOST WALKER: The latest run.

NEURAL BURN: The static's been trying to tell me since the beginning. The voice. The name. The recordings. It's not prophecy. It's *memory*. The whole city is one machine that remembers everything — including the last time we lost.

ZERO-DAY: And Kairos—

NEURAL BURN: Kairos doesn't predict us, Zero-Day. He's *replaying* us.

Zero-Day's head blips — the hologram skull flickering, longer and longer each time. He stares at the old broadcast's timestamp, and it stares back.

ZERO-DAY: Then we've already lost. We just don't remember the fight.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Neural Burn isolates the recording until it is crystal clear, the final line ringing out; Zero-Day head blips, the hologram skull flickering longer. Close on the timestamp, then Zero-Day flickering head. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The timestamp of the old broadcast, Zero-Day's flickering hologram head beside it.]

[SCENE 10 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The crew drifts through the basement, and the weight of the revelation settles over them. Swarm Tech is at the table, staring at nothing. Ghost Walker stands at the grates. Neural Burn is back in his screens, but the screens are dim now, as if even the static is tired.

SWARM TECH: I keep thinking — if it's all a loop, then nothing we do matters. We fight, we lose, they reset, we fight again. Same four names. Same four faces.

GHOST WALKER: Not the same four faces. Just the same four *shapes*.

SWARM TECH: That's worse.

NEURAL BURN: The loop isn't the end of the story, Swarm. It's the thing we have to break. But first we have to understand it. All of it. Every layer.

ZERO-DAY: Then we keep digging. The old broadcast proves the conversion happened before. Now we find out how many times. And who's running the clock.

GHOST WALKER: Kairos.

ZERO-DAY: Kairos is the warden. But even a warden answers to the prison. There's something underneath him. Something that's been running this loop since before he was the one turning the key.

He looks up at the grates, at the red drone-lights drifting past, and his face flickers once — the hologram, quick as a blink, and gone.

ZERO-DAY: The static was right, Neural. The truth is down there, in the layers. And we've only just started to find it.

[GENERATE: Basement, later, the weight of the revelation settling. Swarm Tech stares at nothing, Ghost Walker at the grates, Neural Burn in dim screens, Zero-Day face flickering once. Slow pan across the dim room. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The crew in the dim basement, Zero-Day at the grates, the red drone-lights passing, the old broadcast still looping faintly behind.]

[SCENE 11 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The four sit in the green half-dark, and for the first time in a long time, none of them reach for a screen or a weapon. They just sit with it — the loop, the old city, the broadcast that has played before and will play again.

SWARM TECH: (quiet) If we've done this before... then we've lost before. Probably a lot.

GHOST WALKER: Then we're due for a different ending.

NEURAL BURN: The loop only knows one ending. That's the point. It's not fate — it's *habit*. And habits can be broken.

ZERO-DAY: Not by fighting harder. By fighting *different*. We find the source of the loop. We find what Kairos is protecting. And we break it — not the way it's been broken before, because it hasn't been.

SWARM TECH: How do you know what hasn't been tried?

ZERO-DAY: Because the loop's still running. Whatever we've done, in every run, it wasn't enough. So this time, we do the thing none of us have done yet.

He picks up the Key-Staff, and it glows in his fist, throwing long shadows up the flooded walls.

ZERO-DAY: We go down. All the way down. Past the old broadcast. Past the dead city. To the heart of the Root itself.

[GENERATE: Basement, later, the four sitting in green half-dark, none reaching for a screen or weapon. Zero-Day picks up the Key-Staff and it glows in his fist. Close on the staff glowing, then the four gathered. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day with the Key-Staff glowing, the three others gathered around, the basement walls stretching with green shadow.]

[SCENE 12 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Arcology]

Kairos stands at the wall of screens. A deep-layer probe flags an intrusion — the NU11's descent into the preserved city. Nexus Commander reports, his spotter drones humming at his shoulder.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Director, they've gone deep. Past the grid layer, into the archive strata. They're pulling old broadcasts.

KAIROS: Which one?

NEXUS COMMANDER: The conversion. The first one. The template.

KAIROS: (a long pause) Sooner than last time. They're ahead of schedule.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Is that a problem?

KAIROS: It is a *variable*, Commander. And variables are the one thing the loop has never learned to love.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then should I intercept? Drive them out of the deep layers?

KAIROS: No. Let them find it. The template was always meant to be found. The question is only *when* — and when is exactly what has changed tonight.

[GENERATE: Kairos command in the Arcology at night, the wall of screens flagging a deep-layer probe. Kairos stands before it as Nexus Commander reports, spotter drones humming. Close on Kairos helm reflected in the screens. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's helm reflected in the wall of screens, the deep-layer probe flag pulsing, Nexus Commander's spotter drones hovering.]

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

The crew pulls a second old broadcast — then a third. Different dates, the same words, years apart, like layers of sediment at the bottom of the sea.

ZERO-DAY: There's more than one. The conversion's happened — I can count three recordings now.

SWARM TECH: Three times.

NEURAL BURN: More. There's always more. The static's been carrying all of them, stacked like drowned floors. Every time the city resets, it broadcasts the same conversion, and the old one sinks down and becomes a layer.

GHOST WALKER: So the loop isn't a circle. It's a *pile*.

NEURAL BURN: A pile of the same city, over and over, each one built on the bones of the last.

Zero-Day pulls the three recordings up side by side — identical, but for their dates, which spiral backward into the past like a staircase descending into water.

ZERO-DAY: How deep does it go?

NEURAL BURN: (quiet) Deeper than I can hear. Deeper than the static remembers. Somewhere down there is the first one. The original. And beneath that — nothing.

[GENERATE: Basement, night, three identical broadcasts side by side on the screens, their dates spiraling back like a staircase into water. The crew stares at the stacked layers. Close on the three identical seals. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Three identical broadcasts side by side on the screens, their dates spiraling back, the crew staring at the stacked layers.]

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

[TIME: later that night]

The room has gone quiet. Swarm Tech and Ghost Walker sit apart from the screens, and the weight of the stacked broadcasts presses on them both. Swarm Tech speaks first, and his voice is rough.

SWARM TECH: Ghost. If we've done this before... did we win before? Did we lose? Do you remember any of it?

GHOST WALKER: No. But I remember the counting. The bodies. I think I've always been counting them.

SWARM TECH: Then maybe that's the thing we have to change. Not the fighting. The *counting*.

GHOST WALKER: You think that's possible? To fight and not lose count?

SWARM TECH: I think it's the only thing that would make us different from the loop. The loop counts everything and feels nothing. If we can feel it — all of it — maybe we're not just a replay.

Ghost Walker is silent for a long time. Then he nods, once, almost imperceptibly.

GHOST WALKER: Then I'll try. To feel it, instead of filing it.

SWARM TECH: That's all any of us can do.

[SCENE 15 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

Zero-Day and Neural Burn are alone at the rig, the three identical broadcasts still glowing. Zero-Day's fingers rest on the keys, but he isn't typing. He's staring at the dates, spiraling back into the dark.

ZERO-DAY: Kairos said the loop has run more times than it remembers. This is what he meant. Every layer is a run. Every run is a conversion. And somewhere at the bottom is the first one.

NEURAL BURN: The static's afraid of the bottom. It goes quiet when I listen too deep.

ZERO-DAY: Then that's where we have to go. Not tonight. But soon. Past the broadcasts, past the old city, past the layers. Down to where the loop started.

NEURAL BURN: And if there's nothing there?

ZERO-DAY: There's never nothing. There's always a first time. A first version. A first me. And that's the one thing the loop is trying to keep buried.

NEURAL BURN: (soft) Maybe it's buried for a reason.

ZERO-DAY: Then that reason's exactly what we need to find.

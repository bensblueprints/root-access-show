[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

The old broadcast has broken them open. The four stand apart now, voices rising, the easy trust of the first episodes gone. The recording loops on a dead screen.

SWARM TECH: We can't just *believe* this. It's a recording. It could be a fake, a plant, a trap the corporation left for us to find.

ZERO-DAY: The timestamp is real. The seal is real. It's older than the flood.

SWARM TECH: Then maybe the flood's a lie too! Maybe everything's a lie and we're just four idiots shouting in a hole!

GHOST WALKER: If the conversion already happened, then our war already happened. So what are we?

NEURAL BURN: We're the echo. We're the part that plays after the real thing's over.

ZERO-DAY: That's enough.

NEURAL BURN: No. It's not. You all keep asking what the static means. It means we've done this before. Every step. Every fight. And we're going to do it again because the machine wants us to.

SWARM TECH: You don't know that.

NEURAL BURN: I'm the only one who does. The only one who can *hear* it. And none of you want to listen because the truth sounds like losing.

The room cracks. Swarm Tech turns away. Ghost Walker goes still. And Zero-Day stands in the middle, unable to hold any of it together.

ZERO-DAY: We don't splinter. Not now.

NEURAL BURN: Too late.

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Swarm Tech rounds on Neural Burn, and the argument turns sharp and personal.

SWARM TECH: You want to know what I hear? I hear a man who's been listening to the static so long he can't tell a recording from a prophecy. You keep saying we're echoes, Neural. Maybe *you're* the echo. Maybe the rest of us are still real and you're the broken part.

NEURAL BURN: The burn is what lets me hear. It doesn't make me wrong.

SWARM TECH: It doesn't make you *right*, either!

GHOST WALKER: Enough. Both of you.

ZERO-DAY: This is what they want. Us at each other's throats while the loop keeps turning.

SWARM TECH: You don't get to talk about what "they" want, Zero. You're the one with the blackouts. The one whose handprint shows up in rooms he's never been in. For all we know, *you're* the leak. You're the thing inside the loop, wearing our friend's face.

The words hit. Zero-Day's head blips — the hologram skull, fast and white — and the room goes silent.

ZERO-DAY: (very quietly) I don't know if that's true. That's the worst part. I don't know if I'm one of you, or the thing that's been running you.

NEURAL BURN: That's why we can't splinter. Not until we know.

SWARM TECH: (bitter) It's already too late for that.

He grabs his drone pack and heads for the door, and Ghost Walker steps into his path.

GHOST WALKER: Where are you going?

SWARM TECH: Out. I need air that isn't full of static and accusations.

GHOST WALKER: The grid's watching.

SWARM TECH: The grid's always watching. That's the whole point of this hell.

He shoulders past, and the door clangs behind him.

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — STREET — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Sprawl]

Swarm Tech walks the Sprawl alone, the drone pack heavy on his back, his antenna crown crackling. The streets are tense — the air thick with the sense of something about to happen. He keeps to the shadows, but he isn't careful enough. He's never been careful when he's angry.

SWARM TECH: (to himself) Echoes. We're echoes. The good guys don't get to be echoes. The good guys get to *win*.

A lens swivels as he passes. Then another. Then a drone dips low, its light pinning him to the wet street.

SWARM TECH: No. No, no, no—

He runs. The drone follows. And behind him, out of the cross-streets, white-plated soldiers begin to pour into the Sprawl — a full corporate sweep, checkpoint by checkpoint, block by block, and Swarm Tech has walked straight into the middle of it.

[GENERATE: Sprawl street at night, tense, Swarm Tech walking alone with the drone pack heavy, his antenna crown crackling. A drone dips low and pins him in its light as white-plated soldiers pour into the streets. Wide shot of him caught in the light. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 4 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — SWEEP — NIGHT]

The sweep tears through the Sprawl — checkpoints, drones, white-plated soldiers pouring down the flooded streets. Neural Burn, alone and wired and careless, is the one they corner. He has come out looking for Swarm Tech, and the city has swallowed them both.

ARC SENTINEL: (over the loudspeaker, calm) Citizen. Present your ledger and remain still.

NEURAL BURN: (to the drones, to the soldiers, to himself) I hear you. I hear all of you. You're already dead and you don't know it.

Arc Sentinel, braced on stabilizer legs, arc projector charged, advances. The polite word for what it does is "deterrence."

ARC SENTINEL: This is your final instruction.

Neural Burn doesn't run. He turns toward the projector like a man greeting an old friend, and the cables at his skull crackle with the city's static. A MOTHER in a doorway drags her child inside and slams the shutter.

NEURAL BURN: It's already happened. I've already been here. So have you.

The projector fires. Neural Burn goes down in a net of light, captured — not killed. Arc Sentinel hauls the seer up by the trailing spinal cables, gentle in a way that's almost worse.

ARC SENTINEL: (low) I'm sorry. It's the job.

[GENERATE: Sprawl sweep at night, checkpoints and drones and white-plated soldiers down the flooded streets. Neural Burn turns toward Arc Sentinel arc projector like greeting an old friend, then goes down in a net of light. Close on Neural Burn calm face. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn limp in Arc Sentinel's grip, the sweep roaring around them, the mother's shutter slamming in the doorway.]

[SCENE 5 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — SWEEP — CONTINUOUS]

Across the sweep, Swarm Tech sees it happen. He sees the projector fire, sees Neural Burn drop, sees Arc Sentinel lift him. He starts forward — and a white-plated line closes in front of him. Ghost Walker appears from the dark, grabbing his arm.

GHOST WALKER: Don't. You'll just give them two.

SWARM TECH: That's Neural. That's *Neural*.

GHOST WALKER: I know. And you can't save him by getting taken too. Move. Now. Through the drain grate.

They fall back, and the sweep rolls over the block behind them, and the last thing Swarm Tech sees is Neural Burn's limp form being carried up, toward the Arcology, into the white.

SWARM TECH: (broken) I walked out. I walked out and he came after me. It's my fault.

GHOST WALKER: It's all of ours. Now run.

[GENERATE: Same sweep, continuous. Swarm Tech starts forward and Ghost Walker grabs his arm, pulling him back toward the drain grate as Arc Sentinel carries Neural Burn away. Wide shot of the retreat, the sweep closing the block. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 6 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

The crew returns without him. Three figures now, where there were four. The basement has never felt so empty. Swarm Tech sits with his head in his hands; Ghost Walker stands at the grates, silent; Zero-Day stares at the door as if expecting it to open.

SWARM TECH: They took Neural. They took him because I let him walk out alone.

ZERO-DAY: No. They took him because I let us splinter.

GHOST WALKER: We get him back.

SWARM TECH: How? They'll have him in the Arcology by now. In a cell. Wiped, or worse.

ZERO-DAY: Neural's brain is already burned. Wiping it won't work. That's why they took him *alive*. They want what's in his head. The recordings. The voice.

GHOST WALKER: Then we don't have much time.

SWARM TECH: So what do we do, Zero? You're the one who's supposed to know the next move!

Zero-Day has no answer. For the first time, the master hacker stands in the middle of his own basement and comes up empty.

ZERO-DAY: I don't know. I don't know anymore.

[SCENE 7 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

The three sit in the silence, and the silence is enormous. Then, softly, a chime — not from their rig, but from the grid itself. A message arrives, addressed to Zero-Day by name. A clean white card, Terable Corp's seal, an invitation with no sender but a clock face.

ZERO-DAY: It's from Kairos.

SWARM TECH: Burn it.

ZERO-DAY: He wants a private meeting. Me. Alone. No weapons. Top of the city.

GHOST WALKER: It's a trap.

ZERO-DAY: It's worse than a trap. It's an invitation. He wants to show me something.

SWARM TECH: And you want to go.

ZERO-DAY: I want to know what he knows about the loop. About the old broadcast. About *me*. And he's offering it.

GHOST WALKER: If you go up, you don't come back down the same.

ZERO-DAY: I'm not the same now, Ghost. I haven't been since the broadcast.

SWARM TECH: What about Neural?

ZERO-DAY: If I go, maybe I bring him back. Maybe the truth's the price of his freedom.

The three lock eyes. Every instinct says don't. But Zero-Day is already reaching for his coat — the coat of living code, the staff in his fist.

ZERO-DAY: I'm going up.

[GENERATE: Same basement, later, a clean white invitation card glowing on the table, addressed to Zero-Day by name, a clock face for a seal. The three lock eyes. Close on the invitation, then Zero-Day reaching for his coat. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Swarm Tech blocks the door. He's not angry now — he's desperate, which is worse.

SWARM TECH: You can't. You go up there alone, you don't come back. And then it's two of us. And then it's one. And then it's nobody, and the loop just keeps turning over our empty chairs.

ZERO-DAY: If I don't go, Neural dies in a cell. Or worse — they crack his head open and pull the recordings out, and then they know everything we know. Going up is the only move that keeps him alive.

GHOST WALKER: You're not going for Neural. You're going for answers.

ZERO-DAY: They're the same thing now, Ghost. The answers *are* the rescue. I just don't know how yet.

SWARM TECH: Then take the staff.

ZERO-DAY: He said no weapons.

SWARM TECH: The staff's not a weapon. It's a *key*. And if he tries anything, it's the only thing that opens a way out.

Zero-Day looks at the Key-Staff in his fist, then at the invitation on the table, then back at his two remaining friends. He nods, once.

ZERO-DAY: Then I go armed with the truth. And if the truth's a trap, I'll spring it and see what's on the other side.

He pulls on the coat, and the living code ripples over his shoulders. The staff hums. The door is open before him, and the drowned city waits beyond it, rising toward the white.

[SCENE 9 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — SKYBRIDGE APPROACH — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the climb to the Arcology]

Zero-Day ascends. He takes the long way — service ladders, flood stairwells, the rusted spines of half-sunk buildings — climbing out of the drowned lower city into the clean air of the upper levels. The vertical divide is brutal here, visible in every step: rust below, chrome above, and a cold wind that smells of nothing at all.

At the skybridge gate, a SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR — an old man in a faded municipal coat — stops him.

SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR: Bridge's closed to unregistered. You need a ledger.

ZERO-DAY: I don't have one.

SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR: Then you don't cross.

ZERO-DAY: I'm expected. Top of the city. Director Kairos.

The operator's face changes — fear, then resignation, then something like pity.

SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR: Nobody's expected at the top, son. Not the living kind.

ZERO-DAY: Then let me be the first.

The operator looks at him for a long moment, then steps aside and keys the gate. It hisses open onto the skybridge — a white span stretching toward the Arcology, the flood glittering far below.

SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR: (as Zero-Day passes) The last one who went up to meet him didn't come back down. They never do.

ZERO-DAY: The last one.

SKYBRIDGE OPERATOR: He had your face, too.

Zero-Day stops, but doesn't turn around. Then he walks on, across the bridge, into the white.

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: skybridge → Kairos's private chamber]

The highest room in the city. Glass on every side, the flood glittering far below. Zero-Day enters alone, unarmed — the Key-Staff dark at his side. DIRECTOR KAIROS waits with his back to him, clock-faced helm catching the city's light.

KAIROS: You came.

ZERO-DAY: You took my friend.

KAIROS: I took the one who could hear me. I needed a private word with you, and he was the only one who might have warned you what I am before I could say it myself.

ZERO-DAY: What are you?

KAIROS: Patient. That's all. The rest you're about to learn.

Kairos turns. Slowly, deliberately, he reaches up and lifts the clock-faced helm from his head, setting it aside on a low table. What's beneath is ordinary, and ancient, and terribly familiar. And when he speaks again, it is not the voice of a corporate director.

KAIROS: You're not the first.

[GENERATE: Kairos private chamber, the highest room in the city, glass on every side, the flood glittering below. Zero-Day enters alone; Kairos waits with his back to him and lifts the clock-faced helm from his head. Close on the helm being lifted. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos lifting the helm from his head, the city's light on his face, Zero-Day frozen across the room.]

[SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS]

The voice — it stops Zero-Day cold. Because he has never met Kairos before, and yet he *knows* this voice. He has heard it. In the static. Through Neural Burn's jacks. Whispering his name in the drowned dark.

KAIROS: We've had this conversation before.

ZERO-DAY: (a whisper) It's you. The voice in the static. The one Neural Burn hears.

KAIROS: I've been calling you for a very long time, Zero-Day. And tonight, you finally picked up.

ZERO-DAY: Neural heard you. All those nights. The name. "Elias."

KAIROS: A name you carried from before the flood. A name the city remembers, even when you don't. I don't send it, Zero-Day. I repeat it. The static is older than me. I am only its operator.

ZERO-DAY: Then who are you really?

KAIROS: The man who has watched you do this — all of it — more times than you could count. The warden of a city that runs on a circle. And the only person in the world who knows what it costs to keep the circle closed.

He gestures to the glass, to the city sprawling below — drowned and glittering and utterly still.

KAIROS: You want to know why I always know your next move. I don't predict the future. There is no future to predict. There is only this conversation, happening again, for the hundredth time, in a city that has already lived it.

ZERO-DAY: You're saying this has happened before. That I've stood here before.

KAIROS: You've stood here every time. Same coat. Same staff. Same questions. And every time, you've left with the same answer — and forgotten it by morning.

ZERO-DAY: Then why tell me now?

KAIROS: (soft, almost sad) Because this time, I want you to remember.

[GENERATE: Same chamber, continuous. Kairos revealed face is ancient and familiar, and Zero-Day recognizes the voice from the static. Close on Zero-Day stunned face, then Kairos. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's face fully revealed, Zero-Day recognizing the voice at last, the city glittering below them.]

[SCENE 12 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day's head blips — the hologram skull, longer now, threatening to stay. He grips the Key-Staff to steady himself, and Kairos watches him with something that is not quite pity and not quite patience.

ZERO-DAY: If I'm a copy — if I've done this a hundred times — then what am I doing here?

KAIROS: The only thing any version of you has ever done. Tearing open the city to find the truth. And finding, every time, that the truth is a loop.

ZERO-DAY: And the loop. You run it?

KAIROS: I *tend* it. The way a keeper tends a furnace. It would burn the city to the ground if I let it — or if I let you. But you are not my enemy, Zero-Day. You are the fuel. And I am the one who makes sure the fire stays contained.

ZERO-DAY: Contained against what?

KAIROS: (a long silence) That is the question you never remember to ask. And it is the only one that matters.

He turns back to the glass, and the city below is suddenly, terribly small — a single bright point in a dark that extends far beyond the seawall, far beyond the map, into something the lights don't reach.

KAIROS: Go back to your basement, Zero-Day. Take your friend — he's already on his way down. And when you're ready to ask the right question, come find me again. I'll be here. I'm always here.

ZERO-DAY: You're letting me go.

KAIROS: I let you go every time. That's how the loop works. The warden doesn't keep the prisoner in. He keeps *something else* out.

[GENERATE: Same chamber, continuous. Zero-Day grips the Key-Staff, his hologram skull threatening to stay, as Kairos gestures to the city glittering below. Over-the-shoulder on Zero-Day at the glass. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos at the glass, the city small below, Zero-Day's flickering hologram head reflected in the window.]

[SCENE 13 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S PRIVATE CHAMBER — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day stands at the threshold, and the whole awful shape of it is settling over him — the loop, the warden, the voice in the static that has been calling his name across a hundred nights. He doesn't want to ask. He has to.

ZERO-DAY: The voice. The one Neural Burn hears. If it's you... then what did you want from him?

KAIROS: Not from him. From *you*. He was only ever the antenna. I needed a way to reach you through the noise, and he was the one the burn left open.

ZERO-DAY: So every whisper, every name, every "Elias" in the dark — that was you, leading me here.

KAIROS: That was me, *remembering* you here. Over and over. Because I've watched this scene a hundred times, Zero-Day. I've watched you stand exactly where you're standing now, and I've watched you walk out the same door, and I've watched you come back with the same questions, every cycle, forever.

ZERO-DAY: Then what's different this time?

KAIROS turns from the glass, and for the first time, something like a real smile crosses the ancient face.

KAIROS: Nothing yet. But I have a feeling it will be. You've always been the variable, Zero-Day. The one thing the loop can't quite account for. Even the machine that remembers everything keeps a little room for you to surprise it.

He picks up the clock-faced helm, and the clock face ticks once — in a circle — and holds.

KAIROS: Same as last time. Go.

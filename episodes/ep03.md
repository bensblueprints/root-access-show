# ROOT ACCESS — EPISODE 3 — "ghost_in_the_static"

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — DAY]

A holo-map of the lower city floats over the central table: a Drone Control Hub glowing red in Sector 9. Zero-Day circles it. Swarm Tech is already gearing up — antenna crown, bracer tablet, one gloved hand flexing like he's about to conduct something.

ZERO-DAY: We blind Sector 9, we buy the Sprawl a night without eyes. One hub, one relay tower, one upload of scrap code into the Overwatch registry.

SWARM TECH: You want me to turn their own drones into a marching band.

ZERO-DAY: I want you to keep them busy while I get into the hub's core and kill the sector's telemetry. No eyes, no predictions.

SWARM TECH: And if the grid already knows we're coming?

ZERO-DAY: Then we'll know it knows. That's worth the trip too.

GHOST WALKER: You want overwatch on the ground. I'm better outside than in a server room.

ZERO-DAY: Stay here with Neural. If this goes wrong, I need you two as the exit.

NEURAL BURN: It's louder today. The static. It's got words in it now.

SWARM TECH: Words like what?

NEURAL BURN: Like it's trying to tell me something it already told me before.

Swarm Tech glances at Zero-Day, then back at the map. He flexes the gloved hand, and a half-dozen of the crew's scavenged quad-rotors stir in their charging cradles, spinning up, then settling.

SWARM TECH: Don't tell me anything I don't want to know until I'm back, Neural. I need to believe we're winning for one more hour.

NEURAL BURN: Then come back fast. Because the static doesn't wait for belief.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The holo-map of Sector 9, Swarm Tech's antenna crown glinting in its light, the quad-rotors stirring in their cradles.]

---

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day pulls on his coat — the coat of living code, its surface rippling with faint, shifting glyphs. The Key-Staff hangs at his hip. Swarm Tech checks the bracer tablet, and a dozen more drones lift off his pack in a low, humming cloud, then fold themselves away again.

ZERO-DAY: Entry's a maintenance vent on the south face. Old, unpatched, off the lens grid. You take the roof. I take the core.

SWARM TECH: And the security inside?

ZERO-DAY: Two guards, maybe three. They rotate on a schedule the corporation thinks is a secret. It's not.

GHOST WALKER: You've scouted this.

ZERO-DAY: I scouted it before the conversion. Before the grid went live. The hub hasn't changed. The city has.

NEURAL BURN: (low) The hub's the same. It's you that's different.

Zero-Day pauses at the door, hand on the frame, and doesn't turn around.

ZERO-DAY: We're all different now, Neural. The question is whether it matters.

SWARM TECH: It matters. We're the good guys. Remember?

ZERO-DAY: (soft) I'm trying to.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day in the living-code coat, Key-Staff at his hip, Swarm Tech's drone cloud folding into his pack, Neural Burn watching from the screens.]

---

[SCENE 3 — INT. DRONE CONTROL HUB — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → Drone Control Hub, Sector 9]

A humming concrete block, racks of charging cradles where Overwatch drones sleep in neat rows, a single lit core at the center. Zero-Day drops through a vent; Swarm Tech covers the door, hand raised, and a dozen of the NU11's own scavenged quad-rotors lift from his pack and fan out like an orchestra tuning.

SWARM TECH: First movement: a lot of angry hornets, molto presto.

A NIGHT TECHNICIAN — a tired man in a stained coverall — is dozing at a maintenance console, a half-eaten protein bar in one hand. He snaps awake at the sound, blinking at the shadows.

NIGHT TECHNICIAN: Who's there? Shift change isn't for another three hours.

Zero-Day steps out of the dark, Key-Staff low and glowing.

ZERO-DAY: It's early.

NIGHT TECHNICIAN: You're not corporate.

ZERO-DAY: No. But I'm going to ask you to sit down, stay quiet, and forget you saw me. The alternative is a nap you don't wake up from.

NIGHT TECHNICIAN: (raising both hands) I just change the drone batteries, man. I don't even know what's in the core.

SWARM TECH: Then tonight you learn the best thing a battery-changer can learn: how to be somewhere else.

The technician scrambles back against the wall, hands still up, and stays very, very still. Zero-Day reaches the core, plugs the Key-Staff into the hub's spine. Light floods the rack, the staff drinking the sector's telemetry.

ZERO-DAY: I'm in. Feeding it scrap. Eyes start going dark in thirty seconds.

SWARM TECH: Then I get to be the only thing in the sky.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day at the hub core, Key-Staff glowing, the night technician frozen against the wall, drone cradles in neat rows behind.]

---

[SCENE 4 — INT. DRONE CONTROL HUB — CONTINUOUS]

An alarm. Not a klaxon — a soft chime, as if the building had simply been expecting them. The charging cradles begin to light, one by one, from the far end. The sleeping drones' eyes flicker red, then green, then hold.

SWARM TECH: Zero. They're waking up.

ZERO-DAY: That's not a reaction. That's a *schedule*.

SWARM TECH: What do you mean, schedule?

ZERO-DAY: I mean they were already waking up before we got here. The cradles are cycling on a timer. Somebody set this building to wake up tonight, at this exact minute.

NIGHT TECHNICIAN: (still against the wall) That's not possible. The hub's been dormant since the conversion. No one's touched the cradle timers.

ZERO-DAY: No one *here*.

The Key-Staff flares, and the scrap-code upload kicks in — lines of junk data pouring into the Overwatch registry, corrupting IDs, spoofing launch orders. On the map, Sector 9's lenses begin to flicker and go dark, one by one, like a city shutting its eyes.

ZERO-DAY: Telemetry's dying. We've got maybe forty seconds before the whole sector's blind.

SWARM TECH: And then the part where they shoot at us?

ZERO-DAY: That part's already here.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The drone cradles waking in a synchronized wave, red eyes turning green, Zero-Day's Key-Staff blazing at the core.]

---

[SCENE 5 — EXT. DRONE CONTROL HUB — ROOF — NIGHT]

The first real firefight. Overwatch drones pour out of the hub, and beneath them comes ARC SENTINEL, braced on stabilizer legs, hauling a crackling arc projector fed by twin HIGH VOLTAGE tanks. The polite term for what it does is "deterrence."

ARC SENTINEL: This sector is under corporate management. Lower your weapons and present your ledgers.

SWARM TECH: (into comms) Zero, he brought the whole choir.

ZERO-DAY: (comm) Blind them now, Swarm!

Swarm Tech raises his gloved hand and *conducts*. The NU11 swarm collides with the Overwatch in a storm of rotors and sparks, kamikaze drones clipping the corporate cradles, buying seconds. It is a brutal, beautiful choreography — half a hundred machines tearing each other apart in the floodlight.

SWARM TECH: That's it, sing for me —

Arc Sentinel's projector whines, charges, and fires. The arc tears a neon-blue line through the sky, and three of Swarm Tech's drones die at once, raining metal into the flooded street.

ARC SENTINEL: Deterrence is not a request.

SWARM TECH: It never is! That's the whole problem with you people!

ZERO-DAY: (comm) Sector 9 is blind! Pull out, Swarm, now!

But Arc Sentinel is already moving — not toward the noise, but toward the exact vent Zero-Day used, as if he'd read a map of the retreat that hadn't happened yet.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Arc Sentinel framed against the blue arc-light, Overwatch drones filling the sky, Swarm Tech's swarm spiraling to meet them.]

---

[SCENE 6 — EXT. DRONE CONTROL HUB — ROOF — CONTINUOUS]

Swarm Tech is losing drones faster than he can conduct them. Each sweep of his gloved hand sends another cluster into the arc-light, and each time, the arc answers, blue-white, unstoppable.

SWARM TECH: (comm) Zero, he's not fighting me. He's herding me.

ZERO-DAY: (comm) He's not herding you. He's moving to cut off my exit. He knows the vent.

SWARM TECH: How does he know the vent?

ZERO-DAY: He's not the one who knows.

Arc Sentinel plants his stabilizer legs on the roof's edge, the projector tracking, the twin tanks humming a rising note. His helmet tilts, and for a beat he's looking not at Swarm Tech but at the vent below, patient as a man keeping an appointment.

ARC SENTINEL: You were expected, drone-controller. You and the one with the staff. The Director said you'd come.

SWARM TECH: The Director said. And you just... believed him?

ARC SENTINEL: I believe in order. The Director is order's voice. That's enough.

SWARM TECH: That's not faith, Sentinel. That's a script.

ARC SENTINEL: Then you've never had anything worth obeying.

The arc fires again. Swarm Tech throws his last reserve cluster into the beam, and the drones detonate in a curtain of sparks — a screen, just for a second — and Swarm Tech drops off the roof's edge into the dark below, toward the vent, toward Zero-Day.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Arc Sentinel on the roof's edge, projector glowing, the spark-curtain of dying drones between him and the sky.]

---

[SCENE 7 — EXT. DRONE CONTROL HUB — STREET — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: roof → flooded street below]

Zero-Day bursts out of the vent, Key-Staff still trailing green light. Swarm Tech hits the water beside him, drones skittering in his wake, and the two of them run through the flooded street, arc-light crackling over their heads.

ZERO-DAY: Did we get it?

SWARM TECH: Sector's blind. But so are we — I lost the whole flock.

ZERO-DAY: We'll build more.

SWARM TECH: From what? They were the good ones!

ZERO-DAY: We'll build better. Move.

Above them, Arc Sentinel's projector fires again, and the arc sizzles into the water, throwing up a wall of steam. They dive into a service tunnel, and the light chases them down, then falls back. Arc Sentinel does not pursue. He stands at the street's edge, watching them go, and his voice carries down the tunnel, calm and terrible.

ARC SENTINEL: (distant) The Director said you'd run this way, too.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and Swarm Tech fleeing through the flooded street, steam rising from the arc-struck water, Arc Sentinel's silhouette at the tunnel mouth.]

---

[SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Neural Burn sits alone among his halo of stolen holo-screens, skull studded with jacks, spinal cables trailing into the rig. His eyes are closed. The static fills the room — a low, churning roar — and then, for the first time, it forms *words*.

VOICE IN THE STATIC: (distant, under the hum) ...again... you keep ending up here...

NEURAL BURN: Who's there?

VOICE IN THE STATIC: You know me. You've always known me. You just got burned so you'd forget.

NEURAL BURN: I didn't get burned so I'd forget. I got burned because the net bit back.

VOICE IN THE STATIC: The net doesn't bite. It remembers. It remembers *him*.

NEURAL BURN: Him?

VOICE IN THE STATIC: The one with the light for a head. Ask him who he was before the water. Ask him his *name*.

Neural Burn's eyes snap open. The static collapses back into noise, but the words stay, ringing in the jacks like a struck bell.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn alone in the halo of screens, the static still humming, his cables sparking faint blue.]

---

[SCENE 9 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Neural Burn tears himself out of the rig, cables snapping free, and stumbles to his feet. His hands are shaking. For a long moment he just stands there, breathing, listening to the after-echo of the voice in his skull.

NEURAL BURN: (to the empty room) It's not a sickness. It's a *channel*. It's always been a channel.

He crosses to the central table, where the holo-map of Sector 9 still floats, half-dead. The hub icon blinks, now dark. And around it, faint, like a stain bleeding through the map, he sees — or hears, or both — the outline of a *different* Sector 9. Older. Underwater. Still running.

NEURAL BURN: The city's got two voices. One on top, one underneath. And I've been hearing the underneath one.

He reaches out, and the holo-map flickers, the old Sector 9 ghosting over the new. Then it's gone. Just the static, and the copper taste, and the echo of a name he hasn't been told yet.

NEURAL BURN: What name? What's your name?

The room doesn't answer. But somewhere in the rig, a single screen flickers on by itself, and a string of letters resolves, holds for a second, and dies: E-L-I-A-S.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn at the holo-map, the ghost-outline of an older Sector 9 bleeding through, the single screen with the letters fading.]

---

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Arcology]

Kairos stands before the wall of screens. The Sector 9 attack is already over, and the wall is already replaying it — the vent, the staff, the retreat. Arc Sentinel reports, projector still cooling, steam rising from the stabilizer legs.

ARC SENTINEL: They hit the hub, Director. They blinded Sector 9 for eleven minutes. We drove them off, but they got what they came for.

KAIROS: Eleven minutes.

ARC SENTINEL: Sir, with respect — they got *away*. That's a failure I own.

KAIROS: Nothing was lost tonight that wasn't already scheduled to be lost. The hub was expendable. The sector will be re-lit before morning.

ARC SENTINEL: You knew they were coming.

KAIROS: I already read the incident report of this battle, Sentinel.

ARC SENTINEL: Before it happened.

KAIROS: I wrote it.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's helm reflected in the wall of screens, the replay of the Sector 9 attack still running, Arc Sentinel's silhouette behind him.]

---

[SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — CONTINUOUS]

Arc Sentinel shifts on the stabilizer legs, uneasy. The projector's charge is spent, but something else is building in him — a question he's been carrying since he signed on, now pressing against the inside of his helmet.

ARC SENTINEL: Director... how do you keep doing that?

KAIROS: Doing what, Sentinel?

ARC SENTINEL: Knowing. Every time. You knew they'd hit the hub. You knew which vent. You knew they'd run down the tunnel. You knew it before any of it happened.

KAIROS: Because this city has only ever had one war, and it runs on a loop.

ARC SENTINEL: A loop. That's not an answer.

KAIROS: It is the only answer there is. The rebels think they're choosing. They are not. They are *remembering*, badly, and calling it freedom. You'll understand soon. Or you'll be re-lit along with the sector.

The words land cold. Arc Sentinel stands very still, the stabilizer legs humming, and for the first time the true believer looks like a man standing at the edge of a very long drop.

ARC SENTINEL: I signed up to keep the peace, Director. Not to... not to fight a war that's already over.

KAIROS: (soft, final) The peace you signed up to keep is the loop, Sentinel. There's no version of this city that isn't already written. Now charge your projector. The next battle is already on the schedule.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Arc Sentinel's helmet caught in the replay's light, Kairos's clock-faced helm ticking once in a circle behind him.]

---

[SCENE 12 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Zero-Day and Swarm Tech come back through the grate, singed, alive. Swarm Tech is muttering over the drone losses, cradling a shattered rotor in his hands. Zero-Day is quiet. The relief is brief. Neural Burn is waiting, and he looks wrong — awake in a way he hasn't been, like a man who's just heard the future and can't put it down.

ZERO-DAY: Neural. What happened?

NEURAL BURN: It spoke to me. Not noise. A voice. It said to ask you a question.

ZERO-DAY: What question?

NEURAL BURN: It said to ask you who you were before the water. Before the flood. Before NU11.

Zero-Day goes very still. Swarm Tech looks up from the broken rotor, the muttering dying in his throat.

ZERO-DAY: I don't remember before the flood. You know that.

NEURAL BURN: It knew that too. It called you something.

SWARM TECH: Neural, this is just your head. The static—

NEURAL BURN: It called you by a name. A name you never told any of us. Not Swarm. Not Ghost. Not me.

ZERO-DAY: What name?

NEURAL BURN: (quiet, certain) *Elias.*

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Neural Burn's steady, terrible gaze, Zero-Day frozen mid-step, Swarm Tech with the broken rotor forgotten in his hands.]

---

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day's face blips — a half-frame hologram skull of light, longer this time, code streaming down where a face should be. And then it snaps back, and Zero-Day's hands are shaking.

ZERO-DAY: I've never told anyone that name. I don't remember it. I don't remember it being mine.

NEURAL BURN: The static does.

SWARM TECH: Neural, stop. You're scaring him.

NEURAL BURN: Good. He should be scared. We all should. That name came out of the noise, Swarm. Not out of my head. Out of the *city*.

GHOST WALKER: (from the shadows) You're sure.

NEURAL BURN: I've never been sure of anything in my life. But the static doesn't lie. It doesn't know how. It just repeats what's already true.

Zero-Day sinks onto a crate, the Key-Staff humming faintly at his hip. He stares at his own hands — ordinary hands, shaking — and then at the faint after-image of light that flickers at the edges of his vision.

ZERO-DAY: If the city knows my name... then the city knows me. And if it knows me... it's known me for a very long time.

NEURAL BURN: Longer than any of us have been alive.

SWARM TECH: (quiet) Then who are you, Z?

Zero-Day looks up, and for a half-second his face is the hologram again — a skull of light, code running like tears — and this time he doesn't snap it back. He lets it stay, long enough for all of them to see, before it fades.

ZERO-DAY: I don't know. And I think that's what the city is waiting to find out.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's flickering hologram head, Neural Burn's steady, terrible gaze, Swarm Tech and Ghost Walker at the edges of the green light.]

---

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

The four sit in the aftermath, and the name hangs over them — *Elias* — a word nobody in the room can explain. Swarm Tech finally sets down the broken rotor and speaks, and his voice is smaller than it's been all night.

SWARM TECH: So the voice in your head knows things the city shouldn't know. And the grid remembers routes before we take them. And now you've got a name you've never heard that the whole city seems to know.

ZERO-DAY: That's about the size of it.

SWARM TECH: So what's the play? How do we fight something that remembers us?

GHOST WALKER: We stop letting it remember. We go back to the source. The ledger. The prediction logs. Whatever's underneath the grid.

ZERO-DAY: The Root.

GHOST WALKER: The Root.

NEURAL BURN: The static says that's where the answers are buried. It says the answers are already written down, waiting for us to find them.

ZERO-DAY: Then tomorrow, we go find them. But tonight — tonight I need to know something. Neural. The name. Does the voice say anything else about it?

NEURAL BURN: (a long pause) It says the name is older than the flood. And that you've been here before. More than once.

ZERO-DAY: Here. In this city.

NEURAL BURN: In this *basement*.

The water on the floor glows a little brighter, acid-green, and none of them can say why.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four in the green dark, the name Elias still hanging in the air, the water glowing at their feet like the city holding its breath.]

---

[CLIFFHANGER: The voice in the static calls Zero-Day by a name he has never told anyone — a name from before the flood, from before NU11, from a life Zero-Day doesn't remember living.]

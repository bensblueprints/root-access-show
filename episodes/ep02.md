# ROOT ACCESS — EPISODE 2 — "the_network_remembers"

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — DAY]

The grid is live. Through the high grates, the drowned street is already different — drones idle at intersections, a corner lens swivels, and every citizen below walks with the slight hunch of something being watched. Inside, the NU11 have gone to ground: blinds over lights, the door sealed, terminals running dark.

ZERO-DAY: Every corner lens is online. Every drone registered to the grid. We move in the blind spots or we don't move.

SWARM TECH: So far the blind spots are... your closet and the space under your desk.

GHOST WALKER: There are always blind spots. Systems have edges. I'll map them.

ZERO-DAY: You'll walk the Sprawl? Alone? The grid's watching faces now, Ghost.

GHOST WALKER: It can't watch what it can't see.

Ghost Walker shrugs the glitch-camo poncho tighter, the fabric rippling as it drinks the light around it. The rail-pistol goes into a hip holster; the neural spike stays in the fist.

SWARM TECH: If it IDs you, you don't come back through the front. We lose the basement.

GHOST WALKER: If it IDs me, you were already lost.

ZERO-DAY: Take this. Ping it every block. I'll watch your trail from here.

He hands Ghost Walker a small relay, green LED pulsing.

ZERO-DAY: And if the grid does anything it shouldn't — anything that looks like it's *ahead* of you — you tell me.

GHOST WALKER: Ahead of me.

ZERO-DAY: The broadcast used my code before I wrote it. I want to know if the grid is doing the same thing to your feet.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker at the basement door, poncho rippling, relay in hand.]

---

[SCENE 2 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — MARKET STREET — DAY]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Sprawl, lower city]

The market is a chaos of corrugated stalls, black-market boards, and acid-green light through the water that never fully drains. Ghost Walker moves through the crowd, poncho swallowing motion. A drone passes overhead; the crowd flinches in unison. Ghost Walker doesn't.

GHOST WALKER: (low, into relay) Block one. South of the fish stalls.

A corner lens swivels toward the sound. Ghost Walker steps behind a stack of burned tires before it settles.

GHOST WALKER: Lens three tracks sound. No visual.

At a cross-street, Ghost Walker pauses, choosing. Left leads to the black-market spine; right is a dead-end toward the Seawall. The assassin turns left —

— and every lens on the block swivels at once, as if the grid had already known where the next foot would land.

GHOST WALKER: (quiet) Zero-Day. They moved before I did.

ZERO-DAY: (over relay) I saw it. Keep walking. Don't break stride.

Ghost Walker does. Ahead, a beat cop in a wet municipal coat steps out of a noodle stall, hand on his holster, eyes on the glitch-camo that hasn't quite finished eating Ghost Walker's outline.

STREET COP: Hold it. You. In the gray. Let me see your ledger.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker frozen mid-stride, the street cop emerging, lenses all turned toward them.]

---

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — ALLEY — DAY]

The cop steers Ghost Walker into a narrow alley off the market. Water runs underfoot. The cop is tired, underpaid, doing a job nobody below has the credits to thank him for. He hasn't drawn his weapon — yet.

STREET COP: Ledger. Now.

GHOST WALKER: I don't carry one.

STREET COP: Everyone carries one now. That's the whole point of yesterday.

GHOST WALKER: I'm not everyone.

The cop studies the poncho, the pistol's outline, the way the light bends around Ghost Walker's shoulders.

STREET COP: You know what's funny? My lens flagged you before I even saw you. Corner up ahead. You hadn't decided to turn yet. You were still deciding. And it lit you up.

GHOST WALKER: Systems misfire.

STREET COP: This one doesn't. You know what the grid does? It doesn't just record faces. It *predicts* them. You think you're a ghost, friend. The city already knows your name before you do.

Ghost Walker's grip on the neural spike tightens, slow, under the poncho.

GHOST WALKER: Then you should be careful how this ends.

STREET COP: I don't want to end anything. I'm just a cop. But the grid flagged a face with no ledger, no biometric, no record — a hole in the system walking around breathing. You understand how loud that is?

GHOST WALKER: You could let me be a hole.

STREET COP: (a long beat) My shift ends in an hour. I've got a kid who thinks credits mean food. You know how many people this grid is going to swallow? It's not you I'm afraid of.

He steps aside — just enough.

STREET COP: Go. Before it predicts me, too.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The street cop stepping aside, Ghost Walker's poncho finally dissolving into the alley shadow.]

---

[SCENE 4 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Ghost Walker re-enters through the drain grate, silent, the relay still pulsing. The others are at the terminals. Zero-Day has the grid's telemetry pulled up — every lens, every swivel, every prediction the market block logged.

ZERO-DAY: You're lucky. He could've rolled you.

GHOST WALKER: He chose not to. He said the grid flagged me at a corner I hadn't turned yet.

SWARM TECH: Flags are backward-looking. It sees a face, checks the ledger. It can't flag a *future*.

ZERO-DAY: That's the thing. It can.

He pulls up the log. The timestamp of Ghost Walker's flag sits a full three seconds *before* the lens registered Ghost Walker turning. The prediction preceded the action.

ZERO-DAY: Look at the clock. The grid logged your presence at that corner before your biometric was ever in view. It knew where you'd be.

SWARM TECH: Predictive routing. Some drone does that. But not like this. Not *before the decision*.

NEURAL BURN: It's not predicting. It's remembering.

ZERO-DAY: What?

NEURAL BURN: The grid doesn't see the future. It remembers it. It's seen this street before. It's seen *you* before, Ghost Walker. It's seen all of us.

Silence. Zero-Day stares at the screen, at the impossible three-second gap.

ZERO-DAY: The broadcast knew my code before I wrote it. The grid knew your route before you chose it. These are the same thing.

GHOST WALKER: Someone's already run tonight.

ZERO-DAY: Someone's already run *everything*.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The basement terminals — Zero-Day and Neural Burn staring at the flagged timeline, Ghost Walker in shadow.]

---

[SCENE 5 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → the Arcology]

Kairos's wall of screens. The city rendered as a live nervous system. One panel ticks — a prediction card, pale and clean, sliding up out of the noise. It bears a set of coordinates, a time, and a name: a move the NU11 have not yet planned. It hasn't happened yet.

Nexus Commander studies it.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Sir, this card — the timestamp is ahead of any reported activity. The rebels haven't moved.

KAIROS: They will. That's their next move. It's always their next move.

NEXUS COMMANDER: How can you be certain?

Kairos turns his clock-faced helm. The face doesn't tick forward. It ticks once, in a circle, and holds.

KAIROS: Because I've already watched this happen, Commander. Every screen in this room is a memory.

NEXUS COMMANDER: ...A memory of what?

KAIROS: (a thin, quiet smile) Same as last time.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's clock helm, the prediction card glowing on the wall of screens.]

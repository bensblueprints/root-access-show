[SCENE 1 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

Zero-Day is alone with the Key-Staff. It stands upright in a cradle of cables, humming. The photograph from the lawyer's office is pinned beside it — the previous holder, the hologram head, the same hands. Zero-Day stares at it, then reaches out and grips the staff.

ZERO-DAY: (to the staff) Who were you? Who am I?

He pushes. The staff flares, and his body goes rigid. Code floods his eyes — not a screen's reflection, but light *inside* his head, pouring out through the seams. The hologram skull flickers fully into view, no longer a half-frame glitch but a whole, luminous thing, and then—

Black.

The last thing he sees is his own hand, reaching, and the staff drinking it in.

ZERO-DAY: (fading) ...I'm not... supposed to be able to do this...

[SCENE 2 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

[TIME: later — hours missing]

Zero-Day wakes on the floor. His mouth tastes like copper. The clock on the wall says it's hours past when it should be. Swarm Tech is shaking him, and there's real fear in his face.

SWARM TECH: Zero. Zero, hey. You with us?

ZERO-DAY: What time is it?

SWARM TECH: You've been out for a while. You were on the floor when I came in.

ZERO-DAY: Out? I was at the staff. I reached into it and then—

He sits up. The staff stands untouched in its cradle, dark. The photograph is still pinned. Nothing is out of place. And yet the clock is wrong — wrong by hours.

ZERO-DAY: How long was I gone?

SWARM TECH: Gone? You never left, man. You've been here the whole time.

ZERO-DAY: No. I blacked out. I reached into the staff and I—

GHOST WALKER: (from the doorway) You didn't leave the basement, Zero-Day. None of us left.

ZERO-DAY: Then why are there hours missing from my head?

NEURAL BURN: (quiet) There are hours missing from everyone's head tonight. You're not special. You're just the only one who noticed.

[GENERATE: Basement, later, Zero-Day waking on the floor, mouth tasting of copper, the clock reading wrong by hours. Swarm Tech shakes him, fear in his face, Ghost Walker and Neural Burn gathered. Close on Zero-Day dazed face, then the wrong clock. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day on the floor, the crew gathered over him, the clock on the wall reading wrong.]

[SCENE 3 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day gets to his feet, unsteady, and starts to walk the room — touching things, testing his own memory against the crew's accounts. It doesn't add up. The rig is cold. The water is still. The photograph is where it was. But something is off, something he can't name.

ZERO-DAY: I remember the staff. I remember the light. Then nothing.

SWARM TECH: We found you on the floor. That's the whole story.

ZERO-DAY: But the hours. Between the light and the floor — where did they go?

GHOST WALKER: You were asleep.

ZERO-DAY: I don't sleep like that. I don't *black out*. Unless something used me.

NEURAL BURN: The static was different while you were down. Quiet. Like it was holding its breath. Like it was *working*.

ZERO-DAY: Working on what?

Nobody answers. Zero-Day notices it then — a smudge of dust on Ghost Walker's poncho that wasn't there before. A fresh scrape on Swarm Tech's bracer. A look on Neural Burn's face like a man keeping a secret.

ZERO-DAY: You're all lying to me. Or I'm lying to myself. Which is it?

SWARM TECH: Z, calm down. You're scaring me.

ZERO-DAY: Good. I should scare you. Because I don't know what I did, and neither do you, and one of us is covering for the thing that did it.

[GENERATE: Same basement, continuous. Zero-Day walks the room touching things, testing memory, noticing a smudge on Ghost Walker poncho and a scrape on Swarm Tech bracer. Close on the fresh scrape, then Zero-Day accusing face. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day circling the room, the crew's faces split between worry and something guarded.]

[SCENE 4 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — NIGHT MARKET — NIGHT]

[TIME: the next night]

Zero-Day goes out alone, hunting an answer. The night market is half-shuttered under the drone-light, stalls covered in tarps, a few stragglers still trading in the dark. He finds the STALL KEEPER — an old woman with a single lantern, packing away a tray of rusted parts — and asks the question that's been burning in him.

ZERO-DAY: You keep late hours.

STALL KEEPER: The grid keeps them for me. Who are you?

ZERO-DAY: Someone looking for a face. Last night. Did you see a man go past here? Dark coat. Moving fast, like he had somewhere to be.

The old woman studies him, her eyes narrowing in the lantern light.

STALL KEEPER: I saw a man. But I'm not sure I should tell you about him.

ZERO-DAY: Why not?

STALL KEEPER: Because he had your face.

The words land like a punch. Zero-Day keeps his voice steady, barely.

ZERO-DAY: My face.

STALL KEEPER: Or close enough. He moved like water, and his head — for just a second, when he passed under the light — his head wasn't a head at all. It was all light. Code. Like a screen that swallowed him.

ZERO-DAY: When was this?

STALL KEEPER: Last night. Late. After the second bell. He came out of the drain grate by the fish stalls and went up toward the under-level. Didn't come back.

Zero-Day stands very still. The second bell. The hours that are missing from his head.

ZERO-DAY: Are you sure it was me?

STALL KEEPER: (softly) I've been selling parts on this corner forty years, boy. I know a ghost when I see one. And last night, a ghost with your face walked out of the dark and went down into the water.

[GENERATE: Sprawl night market, half-shuttered under drone-light. Zero-Day questions an old stall keeper beside a single lantern; she studies him, then says the man she saw had his face. Close on her lantern-lit face. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The stall keeper's lantern-lit face, Zero-Day frozen before her, the shuttered night market stretching behind them.]

[SCENE 5 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

Back in the basement, Zero-Day slams the door and turns on the crew. The lantern woman's words are still ringing in his ears.

ZERO-DAY: The second bell. I was out from the second bell to the fifth. And last night, around the second bell, a man with my face walked out of the fish-stall grate and went down to the under-level.

SWARM TECH: Z, that's — people see things. The lantern woman's half-blind. It's the Sprawl, everyone's seeing ghosts since the grid went up.

ZERO-DAY: She described the coat. The way I move. The head. You think that's coincidence?

GHOST WALKER: We think you didn't leave. We were here. All night.

ZERO-DAY: Were you? You said the hours are missing from everyone's head. So how do you *know* I didn't leave, Ghost? How do any of you know anything about last night?

NEURAL BURN: (very quiet) Because we weren't watching you. None of us were. We were all... somewhere else in our heads.

The room goes cold. Swarm Tech and Ghost Walker exchange a look — not guilt, but something worse: uncertainty. The shared, sinking feeling that none of them can actually account for the night.

ZERO-DAY: So I might have left. I might have gone down to the under-level. And none of you would know, because none of you were really here either.

GHOST WALKER: Where would any of us go, Zero-Day? What would any of us *do*?

ZERO-DAY: That's the question, isn't it.

[GENERATE: Basement, night, Zero-Day slamming the door and turning on the crew, the accusation hanging in the green air. Swarm Tech and Ghost Walker exchange an uncertain look. Medium shot of Zero-Day facing them. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day facing the crew, the accusation hanging in the green air, the crew's faces uncertain in the dark.]

[SCENE 6 — INT. SERVER NODE — UNDER-LEVEL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: basement → wiped server node, under-level]

A scream of alarms draws them out. The crew descends to a server node buried in the flooded under-levels — and finds it dead. Racks of hardware, all of it wiped clean, as if a great hand had passed over it and erased everything at once. Static sparks on dead screens. The water around the racks is warm, still steaming faintly.

SWARM TECH: This node was running at full load two hours ago. Now it's a tomb.

GHOST WALKER: Who did this?

ZERO-DAY: When, is the better question.

He checks the node's clock — and freezes. The wipe timestamp falls exactly inside the missing hours. The hours he can't account for. The hours the lantern woman saw a man with his face walk into the dark.

ZERO-DAY: This happened while I was out.

SWARM TECH: You were in the basement the whole time, Z. We told you.

ZERO-DAY: Then explain this.

He pulls up the node's residual log — the faint afterimage of whatever passed through. It isn't a login. It isn't a virus signature. It's a *handprint* of code, pressed into the system's last breath.

NEURAL BURN: (softly) It's you. It's always been you.

ZERO-DAY: I wasn't here.

GHOST WALKER: The code says otherwise.

[GENERATE: Server node in the flooded under-level at night, racks wiped clean, water warm and steaming. The four NU11 descend through alarms; Zero-Day checks the node clock and freezes. Wide shot of the dead node, then close on the timestamp. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 7 — INT. SERVER NODE — UNDER-LEVEL — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day pulls the residual log apart with shaking hands, and the more he looks, the worse it gets. The wipe isn't random. It's precise. Surgical. It removed a specific set of records — and left everything else untouched. As if the hand that did it knew exactly what it was looking for.

SWARM TECH: It's not a smash-and-grab. Someone came here to erase something specific.

ZERO-DAY: Not erase. *Harvest*. The records it touched — they're the ones we've been pulling. The ledger fragments. The prediction logs. Everything we've been chasing.

GHOST WALKER: Someone's cleaning up behind us.

ZERO-DAY: Or ahead of us. The wipe happened during my blackout. Same hours. Same hand.

NEURAL BURN: The static went quiet while it happened. Like the city was watching something it didn't want me to hear.

ZERO-DAY: What did it take, Neural?

NEURAL BURN: I don't know. But whatever it was, it's gone now. And the hole it left is shaped like you.

Zero-Day stares at the residual log, at the code burned into the node's last breath — and it is his code. His signature. His hand, pressed into a room he swears he never entered.

ZERO-DAY: I need to rebuild this. Frame by frame. Alone.

[GENERATE: Same node, continuous. Zero-Day pulls the residual log apart with shaking hands, his own handprint of code glowing in the system last breath. Close on Zero-Day reflection in the dead screen. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's reflection in the dead node's screen, the residual handprint glowing faintly, the crew watching from the dark.]

[SCENE 8 — INT. BASEMENT — NIGHT]

[TIME: later that night]

Back home, the paranoia has set in. Zero-Day won't sit. He paces, and the crew keeps a careful distance.

ZERO-DAY: You tell me I never left. But my signature is in that node. In the wipe. Like a handprint in a room I was never in.

SWARM TECH: Maybe the staff did it. Maybe it used your key.

ZERO-DAY: The Key-Staff doesn't move on its own. It only opens what I open. So either I was there and don't remember, or—

NEURAL BURN: Or you weren't there, and something wearing you was.

ZERO-DAY: There's no "something wearing me." There's me, and there's the truth, and right now I can't tell which one of us I am.

GHOST WALKER: You need to know, Zero-Day. Because if you were in that node, we can't trust what you'll do next.

ZERO-DAY: You think I don't know that? I'm standing in a room full of people who might be lying to protect me — or lying to protect themselves from me. And I can't tell the difference anymore.

He turns to the staff, and for a long moment it's just him and the dark thing humming in its cradle. The photograph of the previous holder is still pinned there, its hologram head flickering in the green light, watching.

ZERO-DAY: I'm going to find out what I did. Even if it's the last thing I do.

[GENERATE: Basement, later that night, paranoia settling in. Zero-Day paces as the crew keeps a careful distance, the dark Key-Staff humming in its cradle, the photograph flickering on the wall. Wide shot of the uneasy room. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and the dark Key-Staff, the crew watching from the edges, the photograph flickering on the wall.]

[SCENE 9 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — NIGHT]

[ROOM CHANGE: main floor → server closet]

Zero-Day pulls the node's residual data up on a private terminal, alone, rebuilding the wipe frame by frame. It's painstaking — forensic — the work of a man reconstructing his own crime scene. Every command, every timestamp, every signature, laid out in the cold light.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) The wipe started here. This instant. Then it moved through the ledger layer. Then the prediction log. Then—

He stops. There's a gap. A moment in the wipe's sequence where the hand — his hand — hesitated. A pause, half a second long, like someone reading a name before deciding to erase it.

ZERO-DAY: You stopped. You saw something. What did you see?

He zooms in. The residual data is fragmentary, but in the pause there is a trace — a single fragment of text, not yet wiped, not yet gone. One line, flickering.

RESIDUAL TEXT: ...Elias...

Zero-Day's breath catches. The name from the static. The name Neural Burn heard. The name he never told anyone. Here, in the wipe's wake, in his own hand.

ZERO-DAY: (barely audible) I came down here. I did this. And I was looking for — I was looking for *me*.

[GENERATE: Server closet, night, Zero-Day alone at a private terminal rebuilding the wipe frame by frame. The residual data yields a single flickering fragment: Elias. Close on the name flickering in the cold light. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day hunched over the private terminal, the single fragment "Elias" flickering in the residual data.]

[SCENE 10 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day keeps digging. The fragment is a doorway now, and he is through it, pulling at the threads of the wipe, following his own signature through the dark. The more he finds, the more the picture resolves — and the more it horrifies him.

ZERO-DAY: It wasn't a break-in. It was a *return*. Whoever was in that node, they'd been there before. They knew the layout. They knew the records. They walked it like they owned it.

He pulls up the final instant of the node's life. A command, executed with his key, in his handwriting. And beneath it, one line of residual text, flickering like a message left for him to find.

RESIDUAL TEXT: you did this before. you'll do it again.

Zero-Day stares. His face blips to the hologram skull — and this time it doesn't snap back. It stays, light streaming, code running, while his hands shake in the glow.

ZERO-DAY: It's me. It's all me. I've done this before. I've wiped this node before. I've *lived* this before. And I keep waking up with the hours gone.

He grips the terminal's edge, and the hologram light spills across the closet walls, filling the small dark room with the glow of a head that is no longer entirely his own.

ZERO-DAY: The staff. The blackout. The handprint. It's not someone else using my code. It's the *last time I did this*, bleeding through.

[GENERATE: Same closet, continuous. Zero-Day follows his own signature through the dark, and his face blips to the hologram skull and stays, light spilling across the walls. Close on the steady hologram head. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's hologram head reflected in the terminal, the residual message burning beneath it, light spilling across the closet.]

[SCENE 11 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day comes back out, and the crew sees it in his face before he speaks — the hologram skull still flickering at the edges, refusing to fully settle. Swarm Tech takes a step back.

SWARM TECH: Z... your head.

ZERO-DAY: I know. I can't make it stop.

NEURAL BURN: Then stop trying. It's not a glitch, Zero-Day. It's the truth leaking out of you.

ZERO-DAY: I found a message. In the wipe. In my own handwriting. "You did this before. You'll do it again."

GHOST WALKER: From who?

ZERO-DAY: From me. A version of me. The one who's been here before. The one who already lived this night and left me the note.

SWARM TECH: That's insane.

ZERO-DAY: Is it? We found our names in the ledger before we existed. We found a photograph of a dead man with my head. And now I've found a note from a version of me who wiped this node in a different year. Tell me which part is insane.

Swarm Tech opens his mouth, and closes it. The room is very quiet. The water on the floor glows, and the hologram skull flickers on Zero-Day's shoulders, steady now, like a lantern at the end of a long, dark hall.

NEURAL BURN: The static says you're almost there. That the truth is close. And that it's going to hurt.

ZERO-DAY: It already does.

[GENERATE: Basement main floor, continuous, Zero-Day emerging with the hologram skull refusing to settle. Swarm Tech steps back, the crew at a careful distance. Close on Zero-Day flickering head. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 12 — INT. BASEMENT — LATER]

Zero-Day sits alone at the central table, the staff laid before him, the photograph beside it, the residual message still burning on a screen behind. The crew has drifted to the edges, giving him space, and the basement is thick with everything unsaid.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) I wiped a node I don't remember entering. I left a note I don't remember writing. I found a name I never told anyone. And a dead man in a photograph has my face.

He picks up the staff, and it hums, warm and low, like something recognizing him.

ZERO-DAY: The blackouts. The hours that vanish. The signature that shows up in rooms I was never in. It's not that someone's using my code. It's that *I* keep doing it — and forgetting. Over and over.

From the doorway, Neural Burn's voice comes soft through the dark.

NEURAL BURN: You're not forgetting, Zero-Day. You're *remembering*. And the thing you keep remembering is the same thing, every cycle, whether you're awake for it or not.

ZERO-DAY: Then the next time I black out—

NEURAL BURN: You'll do it again. Unless you learn to stay awake through it.

Zero-Day stares at his own reflection in the staff's surface — the flickering skull of light, the running code, the face he's not sure is his. The photograph of the previous holder stares back from the wall, identical, patient, waiting.

[SCENE 13 — INT. BASEMENT — SERVER CLOSET — NIGHT]

Zero-Day keeps digging, and the more he pulls from the node's residual data, the clearer the message becomes — not a warning from tonight, but an echo from a previous run. The signature isn't just his; it's *older*, layered under his own like a palimpsest.

ZERO-DAY: (to himself) It's not one handprint. It's dozens. The same hand, in the same node, cycle after cycle. Every version of me wiped this node. Every version left a mark. And every version wrote the same note.

He pulls up the layers, and they stack — his signature, and beneath it another, and beneath that another, identical, going back and back, until the earliest one is faint and cold and barely there.

ZERO-DAY: The previous holders. They all stood here. They all blacked out. They all woke up missing hours. And they all found this note, and didn't understand it any better than I do.

He stares at the stack of signatures — his own face, repeated, fading into the deep — and the hologram skull flickers on his shoulders, steady now, as if it finally recognizes home.

ZERO-DAY: I'm not the first me to lose hours to this. I'm just the latest.

[GENERATE: Server closet, night, Zero-Day pulling layer after layer of identical signatures from the residual data, his own face repeating and fading into the deep. Close on the stacked signatures on screen. Arcane-inspired 3D animated, dark cyberpunk.]

[SCENE 14 — INT. BASEMENT — CONTINUOUS]

Zero-Day comes back out, and the crew sees it in his face — not fear now, but a grim, exhausted resolve. The photograph, the signatures, the note, the missing hours — it's all one thing, and it's all him.

ZERO-DAY: I don't know what I am. Afterimage. Engine. Replay. But I know what I'm not. I'm not going to keep waking up with the hours gone, leaving handprints in rooms I never entered. I'm going to find out what I do when I'm gone — and I'm going to stop it.

NEURAL BURN: The static says you're close. That the next time you go under, you'll come back with more than a note.

SWARM TECH: Or you won't come back at all.

ZERO-DAY: Then I'd better make it count. Because whatever's been running me through these blackouts — it's not going to keep me forever. And I'm not going to let it keep the city, either.

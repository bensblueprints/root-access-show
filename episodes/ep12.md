# ROOT ACCESS — EPISODE 12 — "break_the_loop" (Season Finale)

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. THE CORE — NIGHT]

The core is a furnace of light, and Zero-Day stands at its center, the socket and the Key-Staff fused in his grip. The truth has landed, whole and terrible. He is the afterimage. The engine. The thing the loop turns on.

KAIROS: (disembodied) You've seen it now. The previous hacker burned himself into the Root, and the Root re-renders him — you — every cycle. The revolution is the corporation's harvest. I am its warden. And we have run this war forever.

ZERO-DAY: Then I'm not even real.

KAIROS: You are real enough to reset. That is what you were rendered for.

ZERO-DAY: No. I was rendered to *fight*. And the fight can end.

KAIROS: Every version of you has said that. Every version has reached for the staff and, at the last second, chosen to keep the city — because the alternative is worse.

ZERO-DAY: What's out there, Kairos? What's the loop keeping contained?

KAIROS: (a long silence) Something that has been waiting a very long time for the wall to fall.

Above the comms, the crew's voices rise through the dark — Swarm Tech, Ghost Walker, Neural Burn — and Zero-Day hears them, and makes his choice.

ZERO-DAY: I'm breaking the staff. Whatever's out there, I'd rather face it free than run from it in a circle forever.

KAIROS: You'll kill yourself.

ZERO-DAY: I've never been more sure I'm alive.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day at the blazing core, the staff half-cracked with light, the crew's voices threading through the dark.]

---

[SCENE 2 — INT. THE CORE — CONTINUOUS]

Kairos's voice tightens — not with anger, but with something Zero-Day has never heard in it before. Urgency.

KAIROS: Listen to me. Before you break it, understand what you're doing. The loop is not a cage built to keep you in. It is a wall built to keep *them* out. The city is a wound, Zero-Day, and the loop is the bandage. You tear it off, and the thing underneath is not freedom. It is the reason the wound was made.

ZERO-DAY: Then tell me what they are.

KAIROS: They are what the original hacker found, the night he opened every door for eleven seconds. He didn't just expose the corporation. He exposed *them*. And the corporation — my corporation, my order — has spent every cycle since keeping that door shut. Not to farm you. Not at first. At first, to *save* you.

ZERO-DAY: And now?

KAIROS: Now... the harvest is the only thing that pays for the wall. The rebels are the fuel, and the wall is the firebreak. That is the ugly bargain that has kept this city alive. And you are about to unmake it.

Zero-Day's hands shake on the staff, and the light from the socket throws his own shadow huge and strange across the black. He thinks of the flooded streets, the ledgers, the dead trooper, the mother in the doorway. He thinks of the loop, and of the wall, and of the thing beyond it.

ZERO-DAY: If the wall was built on the harvest... then it was never a wall worth keeping.

KAIROS: That is what the last one said. Right before he burned himself into the Root to hold it shut.

ZERO-DAY: And look where it got him. Look where it got all of us.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's shadow vast on the core's black wall, the staff blazing, the socket pulsing like a held breath.]

---

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the core → the Seawall]

Above, the crew fights the loop's final counterstroke — a corporate response force rolling toward the core's surface entry, ordered to make sure Zero-Day never breaks free. SWARM TECH stands in its path, his swarm nearly spent, one gloved hand still raised.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (over loudspeaker) Move, or be moved. You're fighting a city, drone-controller.

SWARM TECH: No. I'm fighting for one. And the math finally comes out right when you stop counting the cost.

NEXUS COMMANDER: There is no version of this where you win.

SWARM TECH: That's the thing about the loop, Commander. It only knows one version. But I'm not the good guy because the story says so. I'm the good guy because I *choose* it. Every time. Even when it's doomed.

He throws what's left of the swarm forward — a last, ragged orchestra — and the two forces lock over the black water one final time.

SWARM TECH: (to himself, steadying) We're the good guys. Even if there are no good guys. *Especially* then.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Swarm Tech on the Seawall, the last of his swarm rising against the corporate force.]

---

[SCENE 4 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — CONTINUOUS]

The last swarm meets the Overwatch in a blinding tangle of rotors and sparks, and Swarm Tech is conducting nothing now — just throwing everything he has at the wall of machines, buying minutes, buying seconds. Nexus Commander watches from the far side of the water, and something in his certainty has begun to crack.

NEXUS COMMANDER: You'd die for a city that's already forgotten you. Do you understand that? When the reset finishes, there will be no NU11. No Swarm Tech. No name. Just another row in a ledger.

SWARM TECH: Then I'll die the way I fought. As the good guy. Not because it wins. Because it's *true*.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Truth doesn't survive a reset.

SWARM TECH: Then maybe I'll be the first thing that does.

A drone breaks through and takes him off his feet — not fatally, but hard — and he goes down on the wet concrete, the bracer tablet skittering away. The Overwatch closes in. And Nexus Commander, instead of giving the order to finish it, hesitates.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (quiet, to himself) He believes it. After all of it — he still believes.

The hesitation is small. But it is a crack, and the loop, for the first time in a hundred cycles, does not know what to do with a crack.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Swarm Tech down on the Seawall, the Overwatch closing, Nexus Commander frozen mid-order.]

---

[SCENE 5 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — CORE ENTRY — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the Seawall → the core's surface entry]

GHOST WALKER holds the core's surface entry alone. Below him, the corporate force breaks off from Swarm Tech and comes for the door — white plating, pulse rifles, Arc Sentinel at the head. Ghost Walker steps into the light, glitch-camo failing, no cover left, rail-pistol empty. Just the neural spike, and the bodies he's been counting.

ARC SENTINEL: Stand down, assassin. It's over.

GHOST WALKER: It's never been over. I just ran out of ways to pretend it was clean.

ARC SENTINEL: We don't have to do this.

GHOST WALKER: Yes we do. You serve the order. I'm the one who asks what it costs. And the cost has to be *me*, this time. Not another poor kid from the lower city. Not a mother in a doorway. Me.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker backlit at the core entry, Arc Sentinel at the head of the white line, the neural spike glinting in Ghost Walker's fist.]

---

[SCENE 6 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — CORE ENTRY — CONTINUOUS]

Arc Sentinel's arc projector whines, charging, and the two of them face each other across the wet concrete — the enforcer and the assassin, both of them counting, in their own ways, the bodies this war has cost.

ARC SENTINEL: The kid on the block. The trooper. That was you.

GHOST WALKER: That was me. And it was the last one.

ARC SENTINEL: You killed a man who was trying to save a child.

GHOST WALKER: I killed a mirror. And I've been carrying it ever since. That's the difference between us, Sentinel. You carry the order. I carry the cost. And tonight, I'm finally going to set the cost down.

He raises the neural spike — and instead of a weapon, it becomes a *key*, jammed into the entry's lock, shorting the seal and fusing the door behind him. He is buying Zero-Day minutes. And he is not coming back through.

GHOST WALKER: (to the soldiers, calm) They arrive, and someone important stops existing. Tonight, that someone is the part of me that only removed the guilty. The guilt was always the point.

The door seals. The soldiers rush it. Ghost Walker holds the line, and the counting stops for good.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker backlit at the sealed door, the neural spike fused into the lock, the soldiers surging forward.]

---

[SCENE 7 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — CORE ENTRY — CONTINUOUS]

The white line crashes against the sealed door, and Ghost Walker meets them. It is not a fight he can win — it is not a fight he means to win. He means to hold, for minutes, for seconds, for as long as the door needs to stay shut. The neural spike is gone, fused into the lock; he fights with the rail-pistol's empty frame, with his fists, with the glitch-camo shredding around him.

Arc Sentinel does not fire. He watches, the projector charged and ready, and does not pull the trigger. And in that hesitation — a true believer finally seeing the full cost of "deterrence" — Ghost Walker finds his last, quiet victory.

GHOST WALKER: (as the line closes in) You asked what it costs, Sentinel. It costs this. Every time. Someone has to be the one who stops counting.

The white line buries him. And above it all, the loop's clock, for the first time, stutters.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker holding the sealed door against the white line, Arc Sentinel frozen with the charged projector, the cost finally paid.]

---

[SCENE 8 — INT. THE CORE — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the surface entry → the core]

Neural Burn's voice is the last thing Zero-Day hears clearly — the burned man, the only mind the loop couldn't overwrite, speaking through the static with terrible, steady clarity.

NEURAL BURN: (comms) Zero. I can see it all now. The loops. All of them. They always ended the same way. You at the socket. The staff intact. The city reset.

ZERO-DAY: And now?

NEURAL BURN: Now you do the thing none of them did. Break it. Not to win — because you can't win a circle. But to *choose*. That's the whole secret. The loop can't account for a man who chooses different.

ZERO-DAY: Then here's my different.

He plants his feet. The hologram head blazes full and steady. And with both hands, Zero-Day drives the Key-Staff down against the socket and *snaps it in two*.

Light detonates. The core screams. And somewhere in the architecture of the city, a clock that has ticked in a circle for years finally stops.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day breaking the Key-Staff, the core erupting in white light, the loop tearing open.]

---

[SCENE 9 — INT. THE CORE — CONTINUOUS]

The light is blinding, and through it, Zero-Day feels himself coming apart — the afterimage, the render, the engine — all of it unravelling at once. The staff falls in two pieces to the black floor, and for one terrible moment he is nothing, nowhere, a code with no machine to run on.

KAIROS: (disembodied, through the roar) You've done it. You've actually done it.

ZERO-DAY: (fading) Am I... ending?

KAIROS: You're beginning. For the first time in a hundred cycles, you are not the engine. You are only — a man. And a man gets to decide what comes next.

Zero-Day reaches out into the white, and somewhere above, through the comms, he hears his crew — Swarm Tech shouting, Neural Burn weeping, Ghost Walker's silence like a held note. And he holds onto the sound, the only thing in the unmaking that is his.

ZERO-DAY: Then let me decide. Let me be the one who breaks the circle. And let the rest of them — the rest of *us* — be free.

The light folds in on itself, and the core, for the first time in its long, long life, goes dark.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day dissolving into the white light, the broken staff on the floor, the core going dark around him.]

---

[SCENE 10 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the core → the Arcology]

The wall of screens goes white, then dark, then — for the first time in Kairos's long memory — shows something he did not already know. He reaches for the countdown, and the countdown isn't there. The circular sweep has stopped. The helm's clock face no longer turns.

KAIROS: (quiet, almost wondering) It's... stopped.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (comms) Director, the reset sequence is dead. Every node. The city is— it's not resetting.

KAIROS: I know.

NEXUS COMMANDER: What are your orders?

Kairos looks at his own reflection in the dark screen. For the first time, the warden has no line to read.

KAIROS: There are no orders, Commander. Not this time. He did what no version of him has ever done. He chose *different*.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then what happens now?

KAIROS: Now... we find out what the wall was for.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's helm, its clock face stilled at last, the wall of screens dark behind him.]

---

[SCENE 11 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — CONTINUOUS]

Kairos stands very still, and the silence of the dead wall is the loudest thing in the city. Then, slowly, he reaches up and removes the clock-faced helm for the second time — not for Zero-Day now, but for himself — and sets it down. The face beneath is ancient, and tired, and strangely at peace.

KAIROS: (to the empty room) A hundred cycles. A hundred harvests. And the one thing I never accounted for... was that one of them would finally stop running.

Nexus Commander's voice returns, thin and uncertain.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Director, the men are asking. Do we stand down? Do we hold the city?

KAIROS: There is no city to hold, Commander. Not the one we knew. The reset is dead. The loop is broken. Whatever comes next, it will not be on our schedule.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then what do we do?

KAIROS: (a long, soft breath) We wait. And we watch the seawall. Because if the loop was containing what I believe it was containing... then the fight is only just beginning.

He turns to the dead screens, and in their dark reflection, something beyond the city's edge catches the light — a shape, faint, where there has never been a shape before.

KAIROS: (a whisper) It's been waiting. All this time. And now the door is open.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos without the helm, the dead wall of screens reflecting something vast beyond the city's edge.]

---

[SCENE 12 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — DAWN]

[TIME: the first unscripted sunrise]

The city wakes to something it has never seen: a sunrise that wasn't scheduled. Pink and gold light spills over the floodwater, and the water, for the first time in memory, doesn't glow acid-green — it shines. The NU11 — what's left of them — gather on a rooftop to watch it.

SWARM TECH: We did it. The reset stopped.

NEURAL BURN: We chose. That was always the only weapon that worked.

SWARM TECH: Ghost—

NEURAL BURN: I know. I felt him stop counting. He's free now, in the only way the city ever lets anyone be.

Zero-Day stands at the edge, the broken staff still in his hands, his hologram head flickering — but softly now, like a thing at peace. He looks out past the Seawall, and his smile fades.

ZERO-DAY: Wait. Look.

Beyond the seawall. Past the edge of the map. Where the flood had always ended in fog and nothing — there is something. A shape. A *skyline*. Another city. Bigger. Darker. And it has been there the whole time, watching, on the other side of the loop.

NEURAL BURN: (a whisper) The wall wasn't keeping us in. It was keeping *that* out.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The three silhouetted against the first free sunrise, and beyond the seawall, the dark shape of another city.]

---

[SCENE 13 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — DAWN — CONTINUOUS]

Every screen in the city flickers on at once. But the broadcast that follows is not Terable Corp's. The seal is different. The signal is older — older than the loop, older than the flood. And the voice that speaks is not Kairos, not the ghost in the static, but something that has been waiting for exactly this moment.

UNKNOWN VOICE: (low, vast, patient) Zero-Day. Afterimage of the Root. You've done what no cycle has managed. You've opened the door from the inside.

ZERO-DAY: Who are you?

UNKNOWN VOICE: We are what the loop was built to contain. And we have been waiting a very long time for you to stop running in circles.

The screen fills with the other city — vast, impossible, lit by a different kind of light. Zero-Day grips the broken staff, and the crew closes in around him, and the first free sunrise keeps rising over the floodwater.

UNKNOWN VOICE: The war you were fighting was only the gate. Come see what was on the other side.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's face lit by the alien broadcast, the other city filling every screen behind him.]

---

[SCENE 14 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — DAWN — CONTINUOUS]

The three stand at the rooftop's edge, and the other city hangs beyond the seawall — closer now, or perhaps it was always close, and the loop was simply the lie that made it seem far. Swarm Tech's voice is small in the morning air.

SWARM TECH: It's real. All of it. There was always something out there.

NEURAL BURN: The loop wasn't a cage for us. It was a shield. A wall the corporation built to keep the outside out. And we just tore it down.

SWARM TECH: Did we do the right thing?

NEURAL BURN: I don't know. But it was *our* thing. For the first time, it wasn't written for us. It was ours.

Zero-Day looks down at the broken staff in his hands — the tether, the engine, the thing he was rendered to carry — and then up, at the other city, and then at his two remaining friends, and there is something in his face that wasn't there at the beginning of all this: not hope, exactly, but the beginning of a choice that is finally his own.

ZERO-DAY: The loop's broken. The war's over. But the story — the real story — is only just starting.

The unknown voice returns, softer now, almost gentle, and it is speaking only to him.

UNKNOWN VOICE: Zero-Day. You are not the afterimage anymore. You are the *first*. The first of something the loop was never meant to produce. Come. See what you were made to be — and what the wall was built to keep out of the dark.

Zero-Day grips the broken staff, and the crew closes in around him, and the first free sunrise keeps rising over the floodwater, and beyond the seawall, the other city waits.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day and his two friends at the rooftop's edge, the other city vast beyond the seawall, the broken staff glowing faintly in his hands.]

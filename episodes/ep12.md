# ROOT ACCESS — EPISODE 12 — "break_the_loop" (Season Finale)

---

[SCENE 1 — INT. THE CORE — NIGHT]

The core is a furnace of light, and Zero-Day stands at its center, the socket and the Key-Staff fused in his grip. The truth has landed, whole and terrible. He is the afterimage. The engine. The thing the loop turns on.

KAIROS: (disembodied) You've seen it now. The previous hacker burned himself into the Root, and the Root re-renders him — you — every cycle. The revolution is the corporation's harvest. I am its warden. And we have run this war forever.

ZERO-DAY: Then I'm not even real.

KAIROS: You are real enough to reset. That is what you were rendered for.

ZERO-DAY: No. I was rendered to *fight*. And the fight can end.

KAIROS: Every version of you has said that. Every version has reached for the staff and, at the last second, chosen to keep the city — because the alternative is worse.

ZERO-DAY: What's out there, Kairos? What's the loop keeping contained?

KAIROS: (a long silence) Something that has been waiting a very long time for the wall to fall.

Above the comms, the crew's voices rise through the dark — Swarm Tech, Ghost Walker, Neural Burn — and Zero-Day hears them, and makes his choice.

ZERO-DAY: I'm breaking the staff. Whatever's out there, I'd rather face it free than run from it in a circle forever.

KAIROS: You'll kill yourself.

ZERO-DAY: I've never been more sure I'm alive.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day at the blazing core, the staff half-cracked with light, the crew's voices threading through the dark.]

---

[SCENE 2 — EXT. THE SEAWALL — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the core → the Seawall]

Above, the crew fights the loop's final counterstroke — a corporate response force rolling toward the core's surface entry, ordered to make sure Zero-Day never breaks free. SWARM TECH stands in its path, his swarm nearly spent, one gloved hand still raised.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (over loudspeaker) Move, or be moved. You're fighting a city, drone-controller.

SWARM TECH: No. I'm fighting for one. And the math finally comes out right when you stop counting the cost.

NEXUS COMMANDER: There is no version of this where you win.

SWARM TECH: That's the thing about the loop, Commander. It only knows one version. But I'm not the good guy because the story says so. I'm the good guy because I *choose* it. Every time. Even when it's doomed.

He throws what's left of the swarm forward — a last, ragged orchestra — and the two forces lock over the black water one final time.

SWARM TECH: (to himself, steadying) We're the good guys. Even if there are no good guys. *Especially* then.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Swarm Tech on the Seawall, the last of his swarm rising against the corporate force.]

---

[SCENE 3 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — CORE ENTRY — NIGHT]

GHOST WALKER holds the core's surface entry alone. Below him, the corporate force breaks off from Swarm Tech and comes for the door — white plating, pulse rifles, Arc Sentinel at the head. Ghost Walker steps into the light, glitch-camo failing, no cover left, rail-pistol empty. Just the neural spike, and the bodies he's been counting.

ARC SENTINEL: Stand down, assassin. It's over.

GHOST WALKER: It's never been over. I just ran out of ways to pretend it was clean.

ARC SENTINEL: We don't have to do this.

GHOST WALKER: Yes we do. You serve the order. I'm the one who asks what it costs. And the cost has to be *me*, this time. Not another poor kid from the lower city. Not a mother in a doorway. Me.

He raises the neural spike — and instead of a weapon, it becomes a *key*, jammed into the entry's lock, shorting the seal and fusing the door behind him. He is buying Zero-Day minutes. And he is not coming back through.

GHOST WALKER: (to the soldiers, calm) They arrive, and someone important stops existing. Tonight, that someone is the part of me that only removed the guilty. The guilt was always the point.

The door seals. The soldiers rush it. Ghost Walker holds the line, and the counting stops for good.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Ghost Walker backlit at the sealed door, the neural spike fused into the lock, the soldiers surging forward.]

---

[SCENE 4 — INT. THE CORE — NIGHT]

Neural Burn's voice is the last thing Zero-Day hears clearly — the burned man, the only mind the loop couldn't overwrite, speaking through the static with terrible, steady clarity.

NEURAL BURN: (comms) Zero. I can see it all now. The loops. All of them. They always ended the same way. You at the socket. The staff intact. The city reset.

ZERO-DAY: And now?

NEURAL BURN: Now you do the thing none of them did. Break it. Not to win — because you can't win a circle. But to *choose*. That's the whole secret. The loop can't account for a man who chooses different.

ZERO-DAY: Then here's my different.

He plants his feet. The hologram head blazes full and steady. And with both hands, Zero-Day drives the Key-Staff down against the socket and *snaps it in two*.

Light detonates. The core screams. And somewhere in the architecture of the city, a clock that has ticked in a circle for years finally stops.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day breaking the Key-Staff, the core erupting in white light, the loop tearing open.]

---

[SCENE 5 — INT. ARCOLOGY — KAIROS'S COMMAND — NIGHT]

[LOCATION CHANGE: the core → the Arcology]

The wall of screens goes white, then dark, then — for the first time in Kairos's long memory — shows something he did not already know. He reaches for the countdown, and the countdown isn't there. The circular sweep has stopped. The helm's clock face no longer turns.

KAIROS: (quiet, almost wondering) It's... stopped.

NEXUS COMMANDER: (comms) Director, the reset sequence is dead. Every node. The city is— it's not resetting.

KAIROS: I know.

NEXUS COMMANDER: What are your orders?

Kairos looks at his own reflection in the dark screen. For the first time, the warden has no line to read.

KAIROS: There are no orders, Commander. Not this time. He did what no version of him has ever done. He chose *different*.

NEXUS COMMANDER: Then what happens now?

KAIROS: Now... we find out what the wall was for.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Kairos's helm, its clock face stilled at last, the wall of screens dark behind him.]

---

[SCENE 6 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — DAWN]

[TIME: the first unscripted sunrise]

The city wakes to something it has never seen: a sunrise that wasn't scheduled. Pink and gold light spills over the floodwater, and the water, for the first time in memory, doesn't glow acid-green — it shines. The NU11 — what's left of them — gather on a rooftop to watch it.

SWARM TECH: We did it. The reset stopped.

NEURAL BURN: We chose. That was always the only weapon that worked.

SWARM TECH: Ghost—

NEURAL BURN: I know. I felt him stop counting. He's free now, in the only way the city ever lets anyone be.

Zero-Day stands at the edge, the broken staff still in his hands, his hologram head flickering — but softly now, like a thing at peace. He looks out past the Seawall, and his smile fades.

ZERO-DAY: Wait. Look.

Beyond the seawall. Past the edge of the map. Where the flood had always ended in fog and nothing — there is something. A shape. A *skyline*. Another city. Bigger. Darker. And it has been there the whole time, watching, on the other side of the loop.

NEURAL BURN: (a whisper) The wall wasn't keeping us in. It was keeping *that* out.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: The four — the three — silhouetted against the first free sunrise, and beyond the seawall, the dark shape of another city.]

---

[SCENE 7 — EXT. THE SPRAWL — DAWN — CONTINUOUS]

Every screen in the city flickers on at once. But the broadcast that follows is not Terable Corp's. The seal is different. The signal is older — older than the loop, older than the flood. And the voice that speaks is not Kairos, not the ghost in the static, but something that has been waiting for exactly this moment.

UNKNOWN VOICE: (low, vast, patient) Zero-Day. Afterimage of the Root. You've done what no cycle has managed. You've opened the door from the inside.

ZERO-DAY: Who are you?

UNKNOWN VOICE: We are what the loop was built to contain. And we have been waiting a very long time for you to stop running in circles.

The screen fills with the other city — vast, impossible, lit by a different kind of light. Zero-Day grips the broken staff, and the crew closes in around him, and the first free sunrise keeps rising over the floodwater.

UNKNOWN VOICE: The war you were fighting was only the gate. Come see what was on the other side.

[REFERENCE IMAGE: Zero-Day's face lit by the alien broadcast, the other city filling every screen behind him.]

---
